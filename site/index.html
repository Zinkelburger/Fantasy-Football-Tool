Same list for ESPN and Yahoo — here's why

ESPN and Yahoo score defenses differently. Yahoo pays more at the extremes: a shutout is worth +10 there vs +5 on ESPN, and getting blown out costs as much as −10 vs −6. But both systems reward the same thing — facing a bad offense — so when we re-ran our model under each system, the order barely moved. Your point totals will differ by platform; the ranking won't.

Ranked by own team's implied total — the score Vegas expects that offense to put up — plus a bump for domes. Pick the offense and the venue, not the kicker. Finding 28 →

Live in September

Weekly QB/RB/WR/TE rankings need a few weeks of real-season data. The model is already built and graded: it blends recent scoring with expected points — what each player's targets and carries should have been worth — plus the matchup and the Vegas line. Expected points is its strongest signal, and it powers the waiver read too: a player whose chances jump before his scoring does is the one to grab off the wire early. It ships once it has been graded against a real benchmark, not before.

The models

what each one predicts, what it reads, and how it was tested

Why there's no ranking for QB, RB, WR and TE

Because we tested one and it lost. We built a projection model for returning players — 20 inputs: usage, expected points, touchdown luck, age, seasons played, draft capital, contracts and cap share, team changes — and graded it against the draft market with both lists on the same players. The market won at every position, .598 to .544. Finding 25 →

Then we settled it in 9,000 simulated leagues with full drafts and seasons. Drafting by the model instead of ADP roughly halved title odds (10.8% against 20.7%), and every blend of the two finished behind the plain board. Finding 32 →

We built a rookie model too, on draft capital, combine numbers and landing spot, deliberately without fantasy ADP. It lands on top of NFL draft order almost exactly. Its projections are honestly calibrated — rookies it put at 8+ points a game averaged 10.0 and were startable 86% of the time, against 1% for its bottom tier — but that tier information is just what round a player was drafted in, which is public and already priced into every draft board. Finding 31 →

So the honest advice for skill players is the boring one: take your platform's ADP in order and don't reach. What did survive from that work is descriptive rather than predictive — expected points, touchdown-luck regression, and the position-room readout in the draft tool — and it's below or still shipping. The code and the dead ends are kept in archive/season-projection-model/.

Expected points — the "2025 expected" column

Not a prediction — an audit of last season. For every 2025 play it asks what a typical player would have scored from the same chance — who got the ball, where on the field, down and distance — and adds it up. A player who scored far above his chances usually got there on touchdown luck, which doesn't repeat; the chances themselves do. That gap is the strongest buy/fade signal in our research at WR and TE, and the player model above already weighs it. Finding 16 → Finding 17 →

Kicker model

Predicts: kicker points, weekly and for the season. Reads: the points Vegas expects his own offense to score — that one number is nearly the whole model — plus the venue: a dome is worth about +0.7 points a game, wind costs about −0.5. A kicker's own history predicts almost nothing. Finding 28 →

Defense (D/ST) model

Predicts: defense points, weekly and for the season. Reads: the points Vegas expects the opponent to score — that does about 97% of the work; each point off the opponent's total is worth about +0.38 D/ST points. The season list sums it over the whole 2026 schedule. Finding 27 →

Weekly player model — live in September

Predicts: this week's points for QB, RB, WR and TE. Reads: recent scoring, expected points from recent usage (its strongest signal), how many points the opponent's defense has been giving up to his position, and the Vegas line for the game. It powers the in-season weekly boards once real games start.

Injury model

Predicts: availability, from the 2018–25 injury reports: the chance a player listed on Friday actually plays Sunday, what playing hurt costs him, how long each injury type keeps a player out, and the rust in his first game back. Finding 21 →

Vegas offense table

Not our model — the betting market's. Sum the lines for all 272 games on the 2026 schedule and you get expected points per game for every offense. It feeds the kicker and defense models above. It is not a tiebreak between players: everyone can read the same lines, so it's already in each player's draft price. Finding 30 →

The stats we use, in plain English
Spearman / rank correlation
How well two rankings agree, from −1 to 1. 1 = identical order, 0 = no relationship. Our usual yardstick, since fantasy is about who finishes ahead of whom.
Pearson correlation
How closely two sets of numbers move together, −1 to 1. Like Spearman, but it also cares how big the gaps are, not just the order.
Implied total
The score Vegas expects a team to put up, worked out from that game's point spread and over/under.
ADP
Average draft position — where real drafters are taking a player. The market's opinion of him.
All-play
Your record if you played every team in the league every week. Measures team strength with schedule luck stripped out.
Backtest / LOYO
Testing a model on past seasons it never saw while being built ("leave one year out"), so it can't grade its own homework.
MAE
Mean absolute error — how many points a prediction missed by, on average.
95% CI
The margin of error: the range the true number sits in, 19 times out of 20.

My league

Your team, your matchup and your waiver wire, in one place. Everything here reads your league live — nothing is stored on a server, because there isn't one.

Your username is enough — Sleeper's API is open, so there's nothing to log into.

It's the leagueId= number in your ESPN league's web address. Public leagues work right away. A private league needs our browser extension installed and you signed in to ESPN — your ESPN login can't reach us from this page on its own, and the extension never hands it over, it just reads the league and passes the answer back.

Read-only either way, and your league choice stays in this browser — there's no server to send it to. Yahoo needs a login server we don't have, so it isn't supported.
