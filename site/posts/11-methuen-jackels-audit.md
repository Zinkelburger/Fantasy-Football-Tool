# 11 — Auditing the Jackels: strong drafts, two repeat leaks

**Confidence: High** (it's our league's own data, fully counted)

## TL;DR

We graded the Methuen Jackels' drafts against all 12 teams. The 2024
haul was the best in the league, 2025 was fourth, and a
perfect-hindsight redraft keeps most of the actual picks. The two
real leaks, each committed twice: paying a premium for a tight end,
and buying a "discounted" veteran receiver in rounds 4–5.

## How we graded

You can't judge a draft by the final standings — waivers, lineup
calls, and luck pile on top. So we scored every team's *drafted
roster only*: the points it would have produced with a perfect lineup
set every week. Nobody manages perfectly, but the same generous
yardstick applied to all 12 teams makes the ranking fair.

## The good news

The 2024 Jackels draft produced 1,779 perfect-lineup points — first
of 12 teams — with Derrick Henry, Josh Jacobs, and Terry McLaurin all
arriving well below their market price. Patience, not reaching. The
2025 draft came in fourth, and most of the gap back to 2024 was three
injuries (Tyreek Hill, Bucky Irving, Najee Harris) erasing about 140
expected points.

We also replayed each draft with perfect knowledge of the season: at
every Jackels pick, take the best player still on the board. The
striking part isn't the score (the real 2024 draft captured 81% of
that impossible ceiling) — it's that the all-knowing version *keeps
most of the actual picks*. The player identification is close to
optimal. The remaining gap concentrates in two habits.

## The two leaks

| Leak | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Premium tight end | Kincaid (rd 8) → TE27 | LaPorta (rd 6) → TE24 |
| Discount veteran WR, rds 4–5 | Cooper → 74 pts | Tyreek → 30 pts |

Both are patterns we've since tested league-wide. Paying up at tight
end doesn't work ([finding 05](#/blog/05-never-pay-up-for-te)) —
nearly-free tight ends outscored both picks. And the fallen star at a
discount is the worst-performing discount type we've measured
([finding 09](#/blog/09-decline-discount-trap)). Across two years the
leaks cost roughly a full top-20 receiver season's worth of points.

## The elephant

Best draft in the league, 24-4 across two seasons, two #1 seeds —
and two runner-up finishes. That's not a hidden third leak; it's what
playoffs do to the best team, and it gets its own write-up
([finding 12](#/blog/12-championship-variance)). Worth noting: in
both years the eventual champion drafted worse than we did, with the
#3 and #7 hauls.
