# 23 — Running backs run out faster, so take yours first

**Confidence: High** (eight seasons of real results, plus a forced-swap test in the simulator)

## TL;DR

Taking a running back before a receiver is right — but not for the reason people think. RBs aren't underpriced; the market charges the correct premium for them. The real edge is that RB value drains off the board about 45% faster than WR value, so even at fair prices, the order you buy in matters. Take the back first. The receiver will still be there three rounds later.

## What the value curves say

We took eight seasons of results and asked what the market's #1, #5, #12 running back and wide receiver actually returned in points. Compared at the same position rank, the back wins: the top RB outscores the top WR by about 35 points a season, the twelfth-best back wins by about 11, and past rank 18 or so the gap disappears.

But compare at the same *draft pick* — the same price — and the premium vanishes. The market moves running backs up the board by almost exactly the right amount. There is no cheap position to exploit.

## So why take the back first?

Because of the slope. Both positions are fairly priced, but RB value falls 45% faster with every step down the board. The receiver you skip now has a close cousin available three rounds later; the back you skip does not. We proved this in the simulator by running the same drafter twice with everything identical except one pick forced to RB instead of WR. Forcing the back in round 1 or 2 produced a small but real gain in all-play record (your record if you had played every team every week). By round 3 the gain was gone — the curves have already converged.

One myth worth killing: at the same price, RBs miss only about two-thirds of a game more per season than WRs. Early backs carry that tax easily. Late backs are the real trap — past the top twelve, the bad-RB outcome is worse than the bad-WR outcome, and the waiver wire restocks receivers better.

## The fine print

This is standard scoring only; leagues that award points per catch shrink or even flip the premium. And it doesn't automatically crown [RB-RB-RB](#/blog/01-robust-rb-wins): part of that plan's edge comes from our room [drafting backs a few picks late](#/blog/03-adp-discipline-is-not-an-edge). In a disciplined room the dogma fades while the take-the-back-first logic holds — even [perfect hindsight drafts](#/blog/24-hindsight-optimal-drafts) open with a running back only 58% of the time.
