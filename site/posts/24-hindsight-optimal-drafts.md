# 24 — Knowing the players beats clever draft order 40 to 1

**Confidence: High** (the numbers come from an exact search; the interpretation needs care — the oracle has information, not skill)

## TL;DR

We computed the perfect draft: if you knew exactly how every player's season would go, what is the best possible 15-round draft? Perfect foresight is worth about 685 extra points a season. The gap between the best and worst sensible draft strategies is about 17 points by the same yardstick. Knowing which players will hit matters roughly forty times more than the order you draft positions in.

## What we computed

For each of six seasons and every draft seat, we searched for the single best 15-round draft against a room of steady computer opponents — a true ceiling, not an estimate. The first surprise is what the ceiling looks like: even a perfect draft still loses about 4% of its weekly head-to-heads. Nothing removes variance from this game.

## What perfect drafts look like

The oracle's habits mostly confirm what the other findings say from the ground. It opens with a running back 58% of the time, usually the season's actual #1 back — the top of the RB curve is real even [with the answer key](#/blog/23-early-rb-vs-wr). It buys the season's actual best QB in rounds 1–3 in 69 of 72 drafts: the early-QB *option* is [fairly priced](#/blog/06-qb-timing) because you don't know which QB will hit, but the *right* QB is a bargain. It almost never takes a tight end early, and always takes its kicker last.

The season, though, is won in rounds 4 through 8. That's where the oracle loads up on players who go at middle-round prices and finish as top-six backs and top-four receivers. The market's biggest pricing mistakes live in the middle rounds — in round 1 the oracle mostly agrees with the crowd. And the endgame is for lottery tickets: 83% of its final-round picks are players nobody drafted at all, the season's [waiver-wire heroes](#/blog/22-waiver-wire-reality) bought in advance.

## How to read this honestly

The 685 points are foresight, not achievable skill — no one knows which undrafted receiver breaks out. What transfers is the structure: back first, QB timing, cheap tight end, and the middle rounds as the place player judgment pays. The 17-point spread among realistic strategies restates [an earlier finding](#/blog/03-adp-discipline-is-not-an-edge): once you draft with basic discipline, sequencing cleverness is nearly used up. Any further edge has to come from evaluating players better, not ordering picks better.
