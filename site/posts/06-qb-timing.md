# 06 — Pay for a QB if you like — never plan to stream one

**Confidence: Medium-High** (the sim and the raw numbers agree; the
effects are moderate rather than huge)

## TL;DR

Expensive quarterbacks are worth it — they're just not a bargain.
Taking one early neither helps nor hurts in our league. What hurts is
waiting past round 10 and planning to live off the waiver wire: that
plan cost about 3 points of all-play, because most years the wire
holds no good quarterbacks at all.

## What the market gets right

Quarterback has the biggest point gap of any position: the guys
drafted early average around 21 points a game, and the ones drafted
late around 13. So paying up buys real points. But the draft already
knows this — in simulation, a team that grabs an elite QB early
finished at .592 all-play (your record if you played every team every
week), essentially identical to the .591 of a team with no quarterback
opinion at all. You get what you pay for, exactly.

## What actually costs you

The team that banned quarterbacks before round 10 and planned to
stream replacements finished at .563 — roughly 3 points worse. The
reason is supply. In two of the last six seasons, zero waiver-wire
quarterbacks finished in the rest-of-season top 8, and the rare hits
were only obvious in hindsight. With twelve teams rostering about 24
quarterbacks, essentially every startable passer is already owned.

## The hindsight test

We also ran drafts where an oracle knows how the season turns out.
In 69 of 72 of those perfect drafts, the oracle takes the season's
actual best quarterback in round 2 or 3 — the right QB is a bargain
every single year. "Fairly priced" just means nobody knows which one
he is in August. (Our [round-by-round sweep](#/blog/14-qb-round-sweep)
puts the sweet spot at rounds 4–6.)

## The rule we'd actually use

Don't reach for the first quarterback off the board. But when a top-6
guy is still sitting there in rounds 3 through 6 — Josh Allen at pick
30 in our 2024 draft, who finished as the QB2 — take him, and never
leave the draft planning to stream the position. One caveat: our
league scores 4 points per passing touchdown. At 6 points, paying up
early would probably become a genuine edge, not just a fair trade.
