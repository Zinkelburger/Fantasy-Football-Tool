# 08 — In the mid rounds, draft the top receiver on a bad team

**Confidence: Medium** (a clear effect, but from 47 players in one
six-year window)

## TL;DR

In the middle rounds, target a team's clear #1 receiver on a team
coming off a losing season. From 2020 to 2025, those receivers
finished in the top 24 at their position 43% of the time, versus 25%
for mid-round receivers with neither trait — nearly double the hit
rate. Bad teams still throw to somebody.

## Who hits and who busts

We took every receiver drafted in roughly rounds 4 through 10 over six
seasons — about 200 players — and sorted them by two traits: was he
his team's top receiver, and was his team coming off a losing year?
The group with both traits hit top-24 at 43%. The group with neither
hit at 25%. Worth knowing: mid-round receivers as a whole underperform
what they cost, so this is less "free money" and more "the only group
that comes close to earning its price."

The surprising flip side: #1 receivers on *good* teams busted most
often of all, at 46%. Those tend to be aging veterans whose price
rests on reputation — the pattern we chase down in
[the decline-discount trap](#/blog/09-decline-discount-trap).

## Why it works

Bad teams spend their Sundays trailing, and trailing teams throw. The
clear top receiver soaks up those targets, and standard scoring pays
yardage — which garbage time supplies for free. Meanwhile the market
discounts the whole roster because the *team* is bad, so the one
player the offense actually runs through goes a few rounds too late.

We've cashed this ticket twice. McLaurin in 2024 went as the 32nd
receiver drafted and finished #5. Olave in 2025 went 34th and
finished #6. Both were the clear top target on a team nobody believed
in.

## Treat it as a lean, not a law

Two honest limits. The headline group is only 47 players, so the gap,
while large, is measured loosely — and we noticed the pattern *after*
seeing picks like McLaurin work, so the real test is whether it keeps
working in future seasons. And this is about who to pick *within*
rounds 5 through 9. It is not a reason to spend more early picks on
receivers — [that still loses](#/blog/02-zero-rb-is-a-trap).
