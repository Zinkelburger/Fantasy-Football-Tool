# 04 — A hot start tells you nothing about a kicker

**Confidence: High** (every kicker, every week, six straight seasons)

## TL;DR

The top-scoring kicker after week 1 finished the rest of the season
around 19th out of 34 kickers — the middle of the pack — and even the
best kicker through four weeks rarely stayed on top. Draft any kicker
with your last pick, swap for bye weeks, and never spend a waiver
claim chasing last week's hero.

## What we checked

We took every kicker with weekly stats from 2020 through 2025 and
asked one question: does a hot start predict the rest of the season?
Here's the best kicker through the first four weeks of each year, and
where he ranked from that point on.

| Year | Hot kicker (wks 1–4) | Rest-of-season rank |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | Stephen Gostkowski | #26 |
| 2021 | Justin Tucker | #10 |
| 2022 | Younghoe Koo | #23 |
| 2023 | Jake Elliott | #19 |
| 2024 | Brandon Aubrey | #8 |
| 2025 | Spencer Shrader | lost the job |

The full picture — every kicker's early rank plotted against his
finish — is a shotgun blast with no pattern in it. And 2025's week-1
hero, Shrader, didn't even stay employed long enough to qualify.

## Even if you could predict it, the prize is tiny

The gap between the best startable kicker and the worst is about 3
points per game — the smallest spread of any position on the roster
([the value curves](#/blog/07-what-a-starter-is-worth) make this
concrete). So kicker is broken in both directions: you can't identify
the good one in advance, and even a perfect guess is worth a field
goal a week.

To be clear, kickers do score. The top kicker averaged 11.5 points a
game, more than the top tight end. The claim isn't that the position
is worthless — it's that no early pick or waiver move can *select*
the good one, so paying anything for the attempt is waste.

## The two exceptions, and what they actually tell you

Tucker in 2021 and Aubrey in 2024 did stay good after hot starts. Both
were elite kickers attached to strong offenses — and that's the real
lesson. Knowing the *offense* has a little predictive value. Knowing
last week's box score has none.
