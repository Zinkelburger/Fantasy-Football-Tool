# 12 — Two straight finals losses are bad luck, not a broken plan

**Confidence: High** (simulation math plus the league's own record book)

## TL;DR

The Jackels were the best regular-season team in the league two years
running — 24-4 combined, #1 seed and most points scored both years —
and lost the championship game both times. That's not a broken
process. A title comes down to two single-week coin flips, and even a
dominant roster only tilts those flips.

## What actually happened

2024: 13-1, the most points in the league by 160, the #1 seed — and a
loss in the final to the Bombers, who had the #3 draft haul. 2025:
11-3, most points again, #1 seed again — and a loss in the final to
the Bullies, who went 7-6 in the regular season and had the
seventh-best draft. The league's two best drafting seasons
([we checked](#/blog/11-methuen-jackels-audit)) produced two silver
medals.

## The math of a short bracket

Weekly dominance compounds much more weakly than it feels like it
should. Suppose a team is good enough to beat an average playoff
opponent 65% of the time — a huge weekly edge. Even with a
first-round bye, the title takes two straight wins, and 65% twice is
only about 42%. Play two seasons at that level and you'll lose at
least one of the two finals more often than not.

The simulator says the same thing from the other direction: the best
draft strategy we've ever tested wins the championship about 20% of
the time ([finding 01](#/blog/01-robust-rb-wins)). That's roughly two
and a half times a team's fair share in a 12-team league — and it
still means that four seasons out of five, the best plan in the room
does not win the ring.

## What the league should take from this

The tempting lesson after any season is "copy the champion." But in
both years the champion drafted worse than the runner-up. Titles
identify the luckiest good-enough team, not the best process. If you
want to judge process, look at all-play record — your record if you
had played every team every week, which strips out schedule luck —
and total points. By those measures, one team has been the best in
the league two years running.

None of this means the playoffs are pure luck. A dominant team really
does win far more titles over time. It means two specific finals are
far too small a sample to indict anything.
