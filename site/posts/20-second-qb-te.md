# 20 — Backup QBs and TEs are a free choice; timing isn't

**Confidence: High** (for "it makes no difference" — 1,440 seasons per version and two different base drafters agree; the timing details are Medium)

## TL;DR

Whether you spend a late pick on a backup quarterback or tight end genuinely doesn't matter. We forced teams to take one, and banned teams from taking one, and every version finished within a third of a point of the control. Two timing mistakes are real, though.

## What we tested

We ran our simulated league with strategies forced to draft a second QB or second TE at round 10, 12, or 14 — and with strategies banned from drafting one at all. Each version played 1,440 seasons against an identical field, and we repeated the whole experiment with two different base drafting styles. Every version landed within about a third of a point of all-play (your record if you had played every team every week) of the control. Taking the backup costs nothing; skipping it costs nothing. That bench spot is yours to play with.

## Why the pick is worthless even though the backup plays

The surprise is that the backup genuinely plays. On rosters frozen after the draft, a round-12 backup QB started about four weeks a season — byes, injuries, the odd week he looked better than the starter — and added around 23 points. That sounds like value.

It isn't, because the waiver wire sells the same ticket. Almost all of those starts come in weeks where your lineup slot would otherwise be *empty*, and an empty slot gets filled by a Tuesday claim just as well as by a draft pick. [Our waiver study](#/blog/22-waiver-wire-reality) found the same thing from the other side: a waiver QB is the one pickup that reliably works. A drafted backup and a claimed one plug the same hole.

## The timing mistakes that are real

If you do want a backup QB, take him around round 10; waiting until 12–14 was mildly but consistently bad, because by then the startable backups are gone and you're burning the pick. Don't double up either — backup TE in round 12 plus backup QB in 13 was the worst combination we tried. And the big one: pushing your *starting* TE to round 10 with no follow-up plan cost about a point and a half of all-play, the largest penalty in the whole experiment. Waiting on TE [still works](#/blog/05-never-pay-up-for-te), but only if you then stream the position — playing whichever waiver TE has the best matchup each week — instead of riding one late pick.

One caveat: this is calibrated to our family's habits. A room that hoards QBs early would make late backups scarcer and could change the QB answer.
