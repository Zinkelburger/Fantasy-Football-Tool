# 21 — What a Friday injury tag is actually worth

**Confidence: High** (the play rates and the Questionable discount come from about a thousand real game weeks; the per-injury-type details are Medium)

## TL;DR

Out or Doubtful means he is not playing — the actual play rates are 0.1% and 1%, so treat both as zero. Questionable means 69% to play, and about 83% of his usual production when he does. Once a player actually sits, the typical absence is two weeks, and a quarter never play again that season.

## Reading the tag

We matched eight years of Friday injury reports against Sunday box scores. Doubtful is not really "doubtful" — it's Out with paperwork; Doubtful players suited up 1% of the time. Questionable is the only tag with real uncertainty: 69% to play overall, but position matters. Receivers and tight ends play about 75% of the time, running backs 68%, and quarterbacks just 47%. A Questionable QB is a genuine coin flip to take the field.

## Starting the Questionable guy

When a Questionable player does play, he delivers about 83% of his normal output on average — but again the position matters more than the average. A Questionable QB who plays is essentially full price. A Questionable RB costs you about 10%, a WR about 20%, and a TE about 25%. Bench the tight end on any close call; start the quarterback.

## Once he sits, how long is he gone?

We tracked 675 absences of fantasy-relevant players. The typical absence is two weeks, but the injury named on the first report changes the plan:

| First-listed injury | Typical absence | Never returns that season |
|---|---|---|
| Hamstring | 2 weeks | 10% |
| Concussion | 1 week | 27% |
| Ankle | 2 weeks | 28% |
| Knee | 2 weeks | 28% |
| Foot | 3 weeks | 34% |

Hamstrings come back — hold. Feet are the stealth season-enders. Concussions are usually short but occasionally very long. Absences also get worse as they drag: a player already out three-plus weeks ends up missing nearly five on average, and a third never return that year. One more thing to price in: the first game back runs 15–25% below normal, so ease him back in rather than starting him in a must-win on faith.

## The fine print

Injury labels are coarse. "Knee" blends a sprain and a torn ACL, so the table understates the bad tail once real news says ligament. And "never returns" counts every reason a player stayed out — including benchings and cuts — which makes it the right number for roster planning but not a medical prognosis.
