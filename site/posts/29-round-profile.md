# 29 — What hits, round by round (same answer in PPR)

**Confidence: Medium-High** (1,489 drafted players over eight draft
boards, scored three ways; the late-pick age profile is Medium — new
this year, so 2026 is its first real test)

## TL;DR

We took every drafted player from the last eight seasons and asked one
question per round: how often did that pick return a weekly starter?
The curve falls from 81% in round 1 to 5% in round 15, and it's
essentially identical in standard, 0.5PPR and full PPR. The
"stash running backs late" instinct fails — late WRs actually hit a
little more than late RBs, in every format. What actually marks a good
late pick: youth at WR and TE (late hits are about two years younger
than the busts), and remembering that TE and QB starters stay on the
board far deeper than RB or WR ones do.

## The round-by-round curve

Chance a pick returns a starter (top-12 QB/TE, top-24 RB/WR by season
total), standard scoring, 2018-2025:

| Rounds | QB | RB | WR | TE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | 91% | 76% | 67% | 100%* |
| 3-5 | 62% | 50% | 54% | 60% |
| 6-8 | 55% | 32% | 30% | 51% |
| 9-11 | 41% | 12% | 17% | 43% |
| 12-15 | 16% | 7% | 8% | 22% |

*only 7 early TEs in eight years — that cell is Kelce/Andrews/Bowers.

QB and TE stay startable absurdly deep because "startable" means
top-12 at those positions — which is exactly why we tell you to
[never pay up for a tight end](#/blog/05-never-pay-up-for-te) and to
[relax about quarterback](#/blog/06-qb-timing). RB and WR fall off a
cliff after round 8: from round 9 on, five of six picks there return
nothing you'd start.

## The late-RB myth

It feels like late-round RBs "hit big" — someone's handcuff always
becomes a league-winner, and everyone remembers him. The count says
otherwise. In rounds 9-15, RBs returned a starter 8.9% of the time,
WRs 11.2% — and the WR lean grows in PPR (8.4% vs 12.0%). The gap
went WR's way in six of the eight seasons. None of it is
statistically significant, so the honest claim is: **there is no late
RB edge, in any format**. If one late-round season felt like the RBs
hit and the WRs didn't, that was that season's luck, not a strategy.

What late RBs do have is [handcuff
value](#/blog/13-handcuffs-are-free-insurance) — your starter's
direct backup pays exactly in the weeks you need him. Draft the
handcuff for that reason; don't draft random RBs for a hit rate they
don't have. Deliberately loading the bench with RBs
[bought about 1% of championships](#/blog/19-bench-composition).

## What a good late pick looks like

We compared the late-round picks that hit with the ones that busted:

- **At WR and TE, the hits are young.** Late WR hits averaged 1.7
  years younger than the busts, TE hits 1.9 years (both p ≈ 0.01).
  It isn't rookies specifically — just youth. The proven veteran
  sliding to round 11 is the trap; the second-year receiver nobody's
  sure about is the ticket. This rhymes with the
  [bounce-back trap](#/blog/09-decline-discount-trap) and
  [WR aging](#/blog/10-wr-age-effects) findings.
- **At RB, nothing separates hits from busts.** Not age, not last
  year's role. A late RB is a raffle ticket; buy the one raffle with
  known odds — the handcuff.
- **Prior-year usage tells you nothing late.** Everyone still on the
  board in round 11 had a weak role last year. That's why they're
  there.

One honest label: we found the youth pattern by exploring this data,
so 2026 is its first test on picks it hasn't seen.

## We checked all of this in PPR

Everything on this site was built on standard scoring, so we re-ran
the analyses under 0.5PPR and full PPR:

- Only **3.8%** of drafted RB/WR seasons flip top-24 status between
  standard and full PPR. The round table above barely moves.
- [TD luck regresses](#/blog/16-td-luck-regresses) in every format
  (slightly smaller in PPR, where touchdowns are a smaller share of
  scoring).
- [Targets predict next season](#/blog/17-buy-targets-not-efficiency)
  in every format — the effect is smaller in PPR only because PPR
  scoring already pays for volume every week.
- [Hot finishes predict nothing](#/blog/15-late-season-momentum-myth)
  in every format.

The one thing we have not yet re-run under PPR is the simulation
family (zero-RB, QB-round sweeps, bench composition) — those model
our standard family league. Queued.
