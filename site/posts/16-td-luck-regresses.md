# 16 — Last year's touchdown luck doesn't carry over

**Confidence: High** (the effect showed up in all eight year-to-year comparisons we checked)

## TL;DR

Touchdowns are the flukiest stat in football, and drafters keep paying full price for them. Players who scored more touchdowns than their chances deserved score about 1.6 points a game less the next season than equally productive players who got unlucky. Fade last year's TD-lucky players and buy the TD-unlucky ones.

## Separating luck from skill

For every carry and every target, play-by-play data can estimate how likely a touchdown was — a target at the two-yard line is usually a score, a checkdown at midfield almost never is. Add those chances up and you get a player's *expected* touchdowns. The gap between actual and expected is, roughly, his touchdown luck.

Then the test: does beating your expected touchdowns one year predict beating them again the next? Across nine seasons of rosterable players, no — the carryover is statistically zero. But touchdowns are about 40% of standard scoring, so a lucky year inflates a player's points, his final ranking, and his draft price.

## What that's worth

Take two players with the same points per game, one TD-lucky and one TD-unlucky. Next season the lucky one scores about 1.6 points a game less — roughly 23 points over our 14-week season, hiding in plain sight. That gap was negative in every one of the eight year-pairs we checked. QB passing touchdowns show the same pattern at the same size, but on a smaller sample, so treat that as supporting evidence rather than proof.

## Names for this draft

The biggest gaps between actual and expected touchdowns in 2025:

| Player | TDs | Expected | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jahmyr Gibbs | 18 | 11 | fade at cost |
| De'Von Achane | 12 | 7 | fade at cost |
| Tee Higgins | 11 | 6 | fade at cost |
| Justin Jefferson | 2 | 9 | buy |
| CeeDee Lamb | 3 | 8 | buy |
| Christian McCaffrey | 17 | 21 | buy |

"Fade" means don't pay the touchdown-inflated price, not never roster. These are one-to-two-points-a-game nudges — they don't override a changed role, age, or injury.

## Where this could go wrong

Expected touchdowns come from a model, and a model can be wrong about specific players — a true goal-line hammer might beat his number year after year. Zero carryover means such players are rare, but check the usage before fading a repeat offender. And Jefferson's quiet 2025 had other causes too; still, he's the top buy here *and* in [our targets finding](#/blog/17-buy-targets-not-efficiency), which is exactly why we like him.
