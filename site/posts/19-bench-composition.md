# 19 — Bench RBs are lottery tickets that barely pay

**Confidence: Medium-High** (a clean test with a clear answer; the ticket math itself is solid)

## TL;DR

The theory says to stash running backs on your bench because they can become starters overnight, while bench receivers stay dead weight. The first half is true — and it's nearly worthless. Tilting a bench toward RBs bought about one extra percent of championships, inside the noise.

## The true half

We looked at every player drafted in roughly rounds 8 through 15 from 2020 through 2025. Bench RBs and bench WRs averaged the same number of startable weeks — about three — but the shape differs. A late RB turned into a starter for three or more *consecutive* weeks 21% of the time, versus 13% for WRs. The overnight-starter path is real, and it's RB-specific: when a starter goes down, his backup inherits a whole job at once.

## Why it doesn't show up in the standings

We then ran our simulated league with one team tilting its bench picks toward RBs, another toward WRs, and one not tilting at all. The RB-tilted team gained about half a point of all-play (your record if you had played every team every week) and about one percent of titles — differences small enough to be chance.

Three reasons the lottery doesn't pay. The waiver wire sells the same tickets: when a starter goes down, his backup is usually claimable that Tuesday, so stashing mostly pre-pays for what waivers offer — the same conclusion as [our handcuff finding](#/blog/13-handcuffs-are-free-insurance). Bench spots also have a day job, bye-week coverage, where a WR body works exactly as well. And the tilt only moves picks in rounds 9–15, where every option is worth [just a few points either way](#/blog/07-what-a-starter-is-worth).

## The one tactical takeaway for our league

We checked the simulator against our six real drafts and found one mismatch: our actual room goes on a running back frenzy in rounds 10–12, harder than the model does. So if you want lottery RBs, take them by round 9, before the run starts. Rounds 10–12 are then the cheapest time in our draft for TE and WR darts, because everyone else is looking the other way.

Verdict: let RB break ties for your last bench spots. Don't spend mid-round picks on the theory.
