# 17 — Bet on targets, not on efficiency stats

**Confidence: High** (the target signal was positive in seven of eight year-pairs, and the efficiency zeros had plenty of data behind them)

## TL;DR

Two receivers score the same points per game; next year, bet on the one who had more passes thrown his way. Targets predict next season's scoring even after you account for this season's points. The efficiency stats people cite — yards after catch, catch rate — add nothing at all.

## What we tested

We took every rosterable player from 2017 through 2025 and asked one specific question: once you already know how many points a player scored this year, does any other stat tell you something *extra* about next year? Most don't. Targets per game for wide receivers do — the effect was positive in seven of eight year-to-year comparisons. Carries per game for running backs showed a faint echo, too weak to bank on, and for tight ends there was nothing.

The efficiency side was a clean sweep of zeros. Yards after catch, catch rate, even catch rate adjusted for how deep the targets were: none added anything once points were known.

## The twist worth understanding

Yards after catch is a *real* skill — the same players post good YAC year after year. It still doesn't help you, because that skill is already baked into the points you can see. A stat can be stable and useless at the same time. You're not asking "is this player good"; you're asking "is he better than his points say," and YAC can't answer that. Targets can.

## Why targets specifically

Our league is standard scoring — a catch earns nothing by itself — so a receiver who piles up targets without big plays *looks* worse than he is. His points understate his opportunity, and opportunity is what carries over. Points built on nine targets a game repeat; points built on a couple of long touchdowns don't. It's the same logic as [the touchdown-luck finding](#/blog/16-td-luck-regresses): bet on what persists, fade what doesn't.

## Names for this draft

Volume buys — receivers whose 2025 targets outran their points: Ja'Marr Chase, Justin Jefferson (also the biggest touchdown-luck buy, so he's flagged twice), Chris Olave, CeeDee Lamb, Amon-Ra St. Brown. Efficiency fades — points outran their volume: Christian Watson, Alec Pierce, Tee Higgins (also flagged twice, as TD-lucky), Jameson Williams.

One caveat: efficiency still matters when you're judging whether a player *keeps* his targets. It just deserves no extra weight on draft day when production is equal.
