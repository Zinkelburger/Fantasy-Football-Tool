# 22 — The waiver wire is worth about ten points a season

**Confidence: Medium-High** (a clean controlled comparison, three drafting styles agree; a later audit suggests these numbers run a little low)

## TL;DR

We ran the same team through the same season twice — once allowed to use the waiver wire, once forbidden. The wire was worth 5 to 12 points a season, roughly 1% of a team's total, and nearly all of it comes from patching quarterback and kicker. The wire is roster maintenance, not a strategy.

## The experiment

Identical draft, identical opponents, identical schedule — the only difference was permission to make waiver claims. We ran it for three different drafting styles and all three agreed: about ten claims a season bought roughly a point apiece. For scale, the entire question of [which round to draft your QB](#/blog/14-qb-round-sweep) spans about four points of all-play; the whole waiver wire is worth a quarter of that.

## Where the points come from

Breaking the gain down by lineup slot tells the story. QB and kicker gained under all three styles. RB and TE actually *lost* points under all three — claiming there just churned the bottom of the bench, and sometimes churned off someone better.

The reason is roster structure, not football smarts. You start one QB and one kicker and roster one or two of each, so when yours is hurt or on bye you have an actual hole, and the wire fills it. At RB and WR you carry five to eight bodies for five starting slots; a real hole almost never opens.

The pickups themselves say the same. A waiver QB scored 11.9 points a week while held, was kept nearly six weeks, and was cut quickly only 10% of the time — a real player. A waiver RB scored about 4 a week and was cut within two weeks 41% of the time. That's not a lottery ticket hitting; that's churn. It's why a [drafted backup QB adds nothing](#/blog/20-second-qb-te) — the wire plugs the same hole — and it matches [the bench-stash null](#/blog/19-bench-composition).

## What to actually do

Don't plan a draft around the wire; depth you draft is not replaceable by depth you claim. Do go to the wire for a QB or kicker hole — that's where every point of the gain lives (the kicker part is mostly bye-week patching, which is fine, since [kickers are noise](#/blog/04-kickers-are-noise) anyway).

## One honest asterisk

A later audit of our simulated managers caught two flaws — claims wasted on players already done for the year, and starting holes left unfilled — both of which make the wire look *less* valuable than it is. We're rerunning with the fixes. The headline number could grow some, but the shape — small overall, concentrated at QB and kicker — is exactly what the roster-structure mechanism predicts, and we expect it to hold.
