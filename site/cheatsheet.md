# The draft plan on one page

Everything we have tested, reduced to what you actually do on draft
night. Every rule links to the write-up behind it, where you can see the
data and how confident we are.

## Before you pick

- **Bring a ranked list and follow it.** Drafting off credible rankings
  instead of by feel is worth about ten points of team strength over a
  season. That is a bigger effect than anything else on this page.
  [Finding 03](#/blog/03-adp-discipline-is-not-an-edge)
- **Spend your prep on players, not on draft strategy.** Knowing which
  players are good is worth roughly 40 times as much as picking in a
  clever order. [Finding 24](#/blog/24-hindsight-optimal-drafts)
- **Draft off ADP and don't reach.** In 9,000 simulated leagues, taking
  the market in order won titles two and a half times as often as a
  typical drafter, and it beat every custom ranking we could build,
  including our own. [Finding 32](#/blog/32-draft-sim)

## Rounds 1 to 3: running backs

- **Take a running back with each of your first three picks.** In 1,440
  simulated seasons of our league it won the title 20% of the time,
  about two and a half times what an average team in that league wins.
  [Finding 01](#/blog/01-robust-rb-wins)
- **The reason is that running backs run out first.** Their value drops
  off almost twice as fast as receiver value does, and the waiver wire
  never restocks them. [Finding 23](#/blog/23-early-rb-vs-wr) ·
  [Finding 07](#/blog/07-what-a-starter-is-worth)
- **Don't let anyone talk you into Zero RB.** In our standard-scoring
  league it won 9.7% of titles, which is barely more than an average
  team wins. [Finding 02](#/blog/02-zero-rb-is-a-trap)

## Rounds 4 to 8: quarterback and receivers

- **Take your first quarterback in rounds 4 to 6.** Anywhere from round
  2 to round 8 is fine. The real mistake is waiting past round 10 and
  planning to stream. [Finding 14](#/blog/14-qb-round-sweep) ·
  [Finding 06](#/blog/06-qb-timing)
- **Target the number one receiver on a bad team.** A team's top
  receiver coming off a losing season finishes in the top 24 of his
  position 43% of the time, against 25% for other mid-round receivers.
  The draft tool marks these players "Top receiver on a bad team".
  [Finding 08](#/blog/08-bad-team-wr1-edge)
- **Buy targets, not efficiency.** A receiver with a lot of targets and
  modest points is worth buying. Big points on few targets do not
  repeat. [Finding 17](#/blog/17-buy-targets-not-efficiency)
- **Touchdown luck resets every year.** Avoid last year's touchdown-lucky
  players and buy the unlucky ones. The draft tool marks both.
  [Finding 16](#/blog/16-td-luck-regresses)
- **Avoid the receiver who is cheap because last year was bad.** Top 20
  two years ago, terrible last year, priced as a bargain now: that
  profile busts 53% of the time.
  [Finding 09](#/blog/09-decline-discount-trap)
- **Ignore the "injury-prone" label at quarterback and receiver.** Missed
  time at those positions does not carry over from year to year, so take
  the discount other drafters hand you. Be mildly careful at tight end
  only. [Finding 18](#/blog/18-injury-prone-is-mostly-myth)
- **Be careful with receivers aged 29 and 30 at mid-round prices.** That
  age band busted more than any other, though we only have a small
  sample of them. [Finding 10](#/blog/10-wr-age-effects)

## Rounds 9 to 12: tight end and the bench

- **Never reach for a tight end, and never shop for one in rounds 5 to
  9.** Tight ends taken early do beat the receivers and running backs
  taken at the same picks: they win that comparison 69% of the time and
  bust at half the rate. But across 3,600 simulated seasons, every
  drafter who *planned* to take one won fewer titles than a drafter who
  just took the best player available, and how much he lost tracked how
  far above market price he had to pay. So take an elite tight end only
  if he falls to you. Otherwise wait until round 10. After round 4 the
  odds drop sharply, which means rounds 5 to 9 cost you a real pick for
  round-12 results. [Finding 33](#/blog/33-te-same-pick) ·
  [Finding 05](#/blog/05-never-pay-up-for-te)
- **A second quarterback or tight end is optional, but the timing is
  not.** Take one late or don't take one at all. Never take one early.
  [Finding 20](#/blog/20-second-qb-te)
- **Don't draft to a roster template.** Any roster shape you would
  plausibly want is worth about the same as simply taking the best
  player available, but that only holds if you work the waiver wire. If
  you set your lineup and forget it, stay balanced and take a backup
  quarterback. [Finding 34](#/blog/34-roster-shape)
- **Don't fill your bench with backup running backs.** Stashing them
  wins about one percent of championships.
  [Finding 19](#/blog/19-bench-composition)
- **Your star running back's backup is insurance, not profit.** Owning
  him pays off exactly when your starter can't play, and adds nothing
  overall. Take him if he's free, never reach for him.
  [Finding 13](#/blog/13-handcuffs-are-free-insurance)

## Rounds 13 to 15: kicker and defense

- **Spend your last two picks here, and don't think hard about them.**
  Last year's kicker stats predict almost nothing about this year's.
  [Finding 04](#/blog/04-kickers-are-noise)
- **Defense comes down to one number: how many points the opponent is
  expected to score.** Draft whoever plays the worst offenses early,
  then change defense every week all season.
  [Finding 27](#/blog/27-dst-model)
- **For kickers, pick the offense and the stadium, not the leg.**
  [Finding 28](#/blog/28-kicker-model)
- **"He's on a better offense" is not a tiebreaker.** Vegas does know
  which offenses will score the most, but everyone can see that, so it
  doesn't help you choose between two skill players.
  [Finding 30](#/blog/30-good-offense-tiebreak)

## During the season

- **The waiver wire is maintenance, not strategy.** It is worth 5 to 12
  points across a whole season, mostly from patching quarterback and
  kicker. The one thing worth claiming early is a player whose usage has
  jumped: more targets or more carries.
  [Finding 22](#/blog/22-waiver-wire-reality)
- **Hot and cold streaks mean nothing.** How a player did over his last
  three weeks predicts nothing about next season. Price him on the full
  season. [Finding 15](#/blog/15-late-season-momentum-myth)
- **Read injury tags like this.** Out and Doubtful mean zero points. A
  player listed as Questionable plays 69% of the time, and when he does
  he is worth about 83% of his usual production.
  [Finding 21](#/blog/21-injury-model)
- **Don't overthink weekly matchups.** The opponent and the weather are
  worth about a point a week at best, and Vegas has already accounted
  for both. [Finding 26](#/blog/26-team-environment)

## What to expect from a pick

- **A first-round pick gives you a weekly starter 81% of the time. A
  fifteenth-round pick gives you one 5% of the time.** Early picks buy
  reliability and late picks buy lottery tickets. Spend them that way.
  [Finding 29](#/blog/29-round-profile)
- **Losing two finals in a row is normal bad luck, not a broken plan.**
  [Finding 12](#/blog/12-championship-variance)
