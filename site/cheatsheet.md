# The whole system on one page

Thirty findings, boiled down to what you actually do on draft night.
Every rule links to its write-up — click through to see the data and
how sure we are.

## Before you pick

- **Bring a list and follow it.** Sticking to credible rankings beats
  drafting by feel by about ten points of season strength. Everything
  else on this page is a fight over the last one.
  [Finding 03](#/blog/03-adp-discipline-is-not-an-edge)
- **Spend your prep on players, not draft tricks.** Knowing who's good
  beats clever pick order about 40 to 1.
  [Finding 24](#/blog/24-hindsight-optimal-drafts)
- **Use our board, with one caution.** The projections out-rank the
  draft market at QB, WR and TE — at RB, follow the market.
  [Finding 25](#/blog/25-player-model)

## Rounds 1–3: running backs

- **Open RB-RB-RB.** In 1,440 simulated seasons of our league it won
  the title 20% of the time — two and a half times the fair share.
  [Finding 01](#/blog/01-robust-rb-wins)
- **The reason: RBs run out first.** Running back value falls off
  almost twice as fast as receiver value, and the waiver wire never
  restocks it. [Finding 23](#/blog/23-early-rb-vs-wr) ·
  [Finding 07](#/blog/07-what-a-starter-is-worth)
- **Don't get talked into Zero RB.** In our standard-scoring league it
  won 9.7% of titles — barely better than autopilot.
  [Finding 02](#/blog/02-zero-rb-is-a-trap)

## Rounds 4–8: quarterback and receivers

- **Take your first QB in rounds 4–6.** Anywhere from 2 to 8 is fine;
  the real mistake is waiting past round 10 and planning to stream.
  [Finding 14](#/blog/14-qb-round-sweep) ·
  [Finding 06](#/blog/06-qb-timing)
- **Target the top receiver on a bad team.** A team's #1 receiver
  coming off a losing season hits top-24 43% of the time, against 25%
  for the rest of the mid rounds. The draft tool marks these with a
  green ◆. [Finding 08](#/blog/08-bad-team-wr1-edge)
- **Buy targets, not efficiency.** A receiver with lots of targets and
  modest points is a buy; big points on few targets don't repeat.
  [Finding 17](#/blog/17-buy-targets-not-efficiency)
- **Touchdown luck resets.** Fade last year's TD-lucky players, buy
  the unlucky ones — the tool marks both.
  [Finding 16](#/blog/16-td-luck-regresses)
- **Skip the bounce-back-discount receiver.** Top-20 two years ago,
  cratered last year, "value" price now: that profile busts 53% of the
  time. [Finding 09](#/blog/09-decline-discount-trap)
- **Ignore "injury-prone" at QB and WR.** Missed time doesn't carry
  over at those positions — take the discount other drafters give you.
  Mild caution at TE only. [Finding 18](#/blog/18-injury-prone-is-mostly-myth)
- **Squint at 29–30-year-old receivers at mid-round prices.** That age
  band busted most, though the sample is thin.
  [Finding 10](#/blog/10-wr-age-effects)

## Rounds 9–12: tight end and the bench

- **Tight end in round 10 or later.** Half of every year's top-8 TEs
  cost a round-8-or-later pick or nothing at all.
  [Finding 05](#/blog/05-never-pay-up-for-te)
- **A second QB or TE is a free choice — the timing isn't.** Take one
  late or not at all; never early.
  [Finding 20](#/blog/20-second-qb-te)
- **Don't overload the bench with lottery-ticket RBs.** The
  stash-a-back theory buys about one percent of championships.
  [Finding 19](#/blog/19-bench-composition)
- **Your stud RB's handcuff is insurance, not profit.** Owning him
  pays out exactly when your starter sits and adds zero edge overall —
  take him if he's free, never reach.
  [Finding 13](#/blog/13-handcuffs-are-free-insurance)

## Rounds 13–15: kicker and defense

- **Spend your last two picks here, and don't think hard.** Last
  year's kicker stats predict almost nothing.
  [Finding 04](#/blog/04-kickers-are-noise)
- **Defense: one number — the opponent's implied total.** Draft
  whoever faces the worst offenses early, then stream all season.
  [Finding 27](#/blog/27-dst-model)
- **Kicker: pick the offense and the dome, not the leg.**
  [Finding 28](#/blog/28-kicker-model)
- **"He's on a better offense" is not a tiebreak.** Vegas does know
  which offenses will score — it just doesn't help you pick between
  two skill players. [Finding 30](#/blog/30-good-offense-tiebreak)

## During the season

- **The waiver wire is maintenance, not strategy.** Worth 5–12 points
  a season, mostly patching QB and kicker — but a usage jump (targets,
  carries) is the one thing worth claiming early.
  [Finding 22](#/blog/22-waiver-wire-reality)
- **Streaks mean nothing.** A hot or cold last three weeks predicts
  zero about next season — price the full season.
  [Finding 15](#/blog/15-late-season-momentum-myth)
- **Read injury tags like this:** Out and Doubtful mean zero. When a player is Questionable, he
  plays 69% of the time at about 83% strength.
  [Finding 21](#/blog/21-injury-model)
- **Don't overthink start/sit matchups.** Opponent and weather are
  worth about a point a week at best, and Vegas already priced them.
  [Finding 26](#/blog/26-team-environment)

## Why you can trust the averages

- **A round-1 pick returns a weekly starter 81% of the time; round 15,
  5%.** Early picks buy floors, late picks buy lottery tickets — spend
  them that way. [Finding 29](#/blog/29-round-profile)
- **Losing two finals in a row is a coin-flip outcome, not a broken
  plan.** [Finding 12](#/blog/12-championship-variance) ·
  [Finding 11](#/blog/11-methuen-jackels-audit)
