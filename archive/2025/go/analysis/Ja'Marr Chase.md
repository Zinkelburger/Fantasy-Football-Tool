Short verdict: Ja’Marr Chase remains a top-tier, high-floor/high-ceiling WR1 for 2025. The Tee Higgins data provided is about Higgins, but it underscored a key point: Chase’s presence boosts the Bengals’ passing game without capping Higgins; two elite pass-catchers can coexist and sustain a very high team ceiling.

2025 fantasy outlook (Chase)
- Projection band: Top-6 WR with 1,150–1,500+ receiving yards, ~8–12 total TDs, and ~115–165 targets (rough macro range; expect closer to 130–150 targets in a healthy, full season).
- Floor/ceiling: Consistent weekly upside in favorable matchups; floor remains solid in any pass-heavy game script, but injury to Burrow or offensive line struggles can cap ceiling.
- Target share context: Higgins in the mix (~7.4 targets/game when Chase is healthy) implies Chase still dominates as the clear alpha, but Cincinnati’s offense supports two high-volume receivers. Chase should see a high target share regardless, not a depleted ceiling due to Higgins.
- Offense stability: Burrow health is the biggest driver. If Burrow plays 15–17 games and the line holds up, Chase is an easy top-6 pick with weekly 8–12 target upside.
- Risk factors:
  - Burrow injury/health volatility remains the primary downside; any significant drop in passing volume hurts Chase most.
  - O-line/loss of ancillary weapons could shift game flow away from pass-heavy scripts.
  - TD regression risk exists but is mitigated by volume and explosiveness.

Reddit/FF Hound sentiment cues (from the provided data)
- Main takeaway: Higgins’ relevance is tied to Chase’s health; when Chase is on the field, Higgins still produces meaningful volume (supports the idea of a high-scoring Bengals offense without stifling Chase).
- Implication for Chase: Even with Higgins as a secondary target, Chase’s ceiling remains intact; the offense’s overall pass volume supports two top-tier weapons.
- FF Hound notes mirror the Higgins analysis but don’t contradict Chase’s elite status; the consensus remains: Chase is a core anchor, with Higgins as a complementary, high-floor co-anchor.

Actionable drafting notes
- Early-round pick: Chase is a strong lock for a top-6 pick in standard and PPR formats. Do not overpay for a stacked offense narrative; treat him as the primary target with legitimate 1A upside.
- Stacking strategy: Consider Burrow–Chase stacks where affordable; Higgins to Chase stacks are viable but don’t overcommit to one path—Chase’s value stands on his own.
- Contingency planning: If Burrow’s health becomes a documented concern in preseason, be prepared to pivot to a high-floor WR1/elite RB, but Chase’s intrinsic talent and Bengals’ pass-happy tendencies argue against avoiding him entirely.
- Weekly play: Expect Chase to be matchup-proof enough to start in as many lineups as possible, with the upside games against weak secondaries or in shootouts.
- Buy-low window: If there are early-season reports of reduced passing volume or Burrow’s lingering concerns, use that as a buying opportunity while the upside remains intact.

Bottom line
- Chase is a high-confidence, premium WR1 with top-5 upside in a healthy season.
- The Higgins dynamic is a plus for overall Bengals passing volume and does not meaningfully diminish Chase’s ceiling.
- Biggest risk remains Burrow-health-related; monitor preseason reports and offensive line status.
