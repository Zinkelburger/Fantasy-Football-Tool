- Role and usage projection: Primary role in the passing game; likely sees most of Washington’s third-down work and as a safety valve. Down-to-down carries could be modest behind Robinson and Chris Rodriguez Jr.; expect a fragmented backfield with Ekeler as the best PPR floor option.

- Team context and competition: Brian Robinson Jr. is the early-down, lead profile. Jacory Croskey-Merritt (Bill) and Chris Rodriguez Jr. are in the mix; Ekeler’s value hinges on how much Washington lets him operate as a pass-catcher and on Robinson’s durability/role. Camp notes emphasize Ekeler as a receiving threat; JCM/Rodriguez could siphon some early-down snaps.

- Receiving usage/value: Strongest asset. Reddit data cites: 366 receiving yards, 11.4% target share, and solid yards-per-target (high efficiency). In PPR leagues, Ekeler has a meaningful floor when on the field; in standard leagues, his value depends on goal-line TDs and workload.

- Red zone/TD upside: Turbulent. In a three-man committee, TDs may be harder to come by, especially if goal-line carries tilt toward Robinson or if the offense shifts other weapons (Deebo Samuel-like role not guaranteed). Expect some TD volatility.

- Health and age risk: 30 years old entering 2025. Durability risk is real; could see reduced early-down workload vs. a younger backfield mate if the coaching staff prioritizes durability and role clarity.

- Draft/ADP sentiment: Consistently seen as a late-round value (ETR/ Hedge notes around RB46-48, Round 12). Kin to a “RB2 with RB2 upside” but not a slam dunk; value hinges on continued heavy passing-game usage and favorable game scripts.

- Draft strategy implications:
  - If you already have Robinson: Ekeler is a fine late-round stab for PPR upside; monitor preseason reps for clear role signals.
  - If you don’t own Robinson: Ekeler offers a high-floor stack-y option in a capped-ceiling backfield; still risky if the committee roles don’t separate.
  - Best-ball/bench stash: Reasonable late-round pick; use as a flexible piece that can become a weekly starter in PPR if Ekeler claims more passing-down work.

- Red flags to monitor (preseason/early season): 
  - Real-time splits on third-down/receiving snaps between Ekeler, JCM, and Rodriguez.
  - Any shift in Washington’s offensive usage of RBs (e.g., more routes run by Ekeler or fewer by the other backups).
  - Robinson health durability; trade rumors that could raise Ekeler’s target share.

- Bottom line: Realistic value lies in PPR floor with modest upside. Ekeler is a solid late-round steal only if you’re comfortable with a committee and don’t rely on him for weekly top-12 RB upside. If you’re aiming for a high-floor, flexible RB2 in a bench-heavy build, he’s worth a pick; otherwise, treat him as a wait-and-see beat with a clear path to relevance if the passing game demand spikes.
