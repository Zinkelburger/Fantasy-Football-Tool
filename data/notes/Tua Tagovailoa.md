**Tua Tagovailoa** (ATL, QB, bye 11) — board rank 232 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** A minimum-salary bridge quarterback the room has already written off, holding a starting job only until Michael Penix is ready.

- Availability and role are the same fact: he is expected to open as Atlanta's Week 1 starter, but explicitly as a placeholder, only while Penix finishes ramping up after being medically cleared. The room's read has moved in one direction. He signed a roughly $1.3M veteran-minimum deal as a stop-gap while Penix rehabbed and held a four-month head start; by Aug 25-26 the stated consensus was that Penix had won what commenters called a one-man competition, with reports of Tua looking bad in camp and one commenter saying he did not complete a pass in practice the day before. By Sept 1-3 he is back to being the Week 1 starter, and the reason given is Penix's ramp-up rather than anything Tua did — one commenter says he was outplayed by Jack Strand in camp and did not "win" anything.
- Injury: per Marc Raimondi (ESPN) on Aug 11, his tight back issue seems to be in the rearview mirror. The larger health story is the concussions that ended his Miami run and an Alabama hip injury the room blames for the peak version never reaching the NFL — a 65-point comment calls him an all-time "what if" who played well while physically compromised.
- The scouting is brutal and consistent across threads: an extreme check-down merchant with no depth, with one poster saying his longest completed pass all preseason was about 3 air yards and that he never saw him throw past 12 yards downfield. Elsewhere the room calls him "completely cooked" and argues Miami's offense improves specifically by replacing him. His ceiling in the room's own words is "average Kirk Cousins", and the Falcons' quarterback situation is cited as the main drag on their scoring — the reason to fade their skill players.
- There is a real counter-case and it is thin but sourced. Per Raimondi he went 7-of-7 in Falcons 11-on-11 on Aug 11 and was starting to show comfort in the new offense under HC Kevin Stefanski and OC Tommy Rees, after a 6-of-8 best practice of camp on Aug 9. One contrarian pitch for 6-point-passing-TD leagues: healthy, he has been one of the better pocket quarterbacks in the league, he is in a division with no good defenses, and Atlanta gives him three high-end pass catchers. Several predict he wins a playoff game there.
- Market: one claim only — he is extremely cheap in dynasty, buyable for a third or late-second rookie pick, with the plan to flip him midseason if he holds the job and plays well.

**Draft take:** Streaming-only and not for long — he opens Week 1 solely because Penix is not ready, and every scouting read in the corpus says the arm that made him worth starting is gone.
