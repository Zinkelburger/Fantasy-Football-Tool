**D'Andre Swift** (CHI, RB, bye 10) — board rank 53 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The season's first panic-and-relief cycle resolved in his favour, and the room wants to buy the dip rather than fade it.

- The 9/3 practice scare is over. He exited late in a full team period holding his midsection after a carry from the 1, per Sean Hammond, and some reports had him in significant discomfort; the Bears then put out an all-clear on Odunze and pointedly left Swift out of it, which the room read badly. Schefter reported on 2026-09-04 that it was "a cramp" per sources, not a structural injury — blamed on late-camp Midwest heat. The worst case anyone had floated was an oblique strain costing roughly three weeks. Treat him as a Week 1 go; the Bears' first required injury report is Wednesday.
- Some doubt Schefter here, citing his miss on the Coby Bryant timeline, and the room's own framing is that this is normal Swift: a perpetual Questionable tag, Q most weeks the last two seasons, and he suits up.
- Role is as clean as it has been. Chicago waived Salvon Ahmed at cutdowns (Matt Zenitz, CBS), leaving Swift plus Roschon Johnson as a blitz-pickup and special-teams RB3 — nobody behind him is rostered to take offensive touches, and the contingency plan is an outside add. Kyle Monangai is the looming threat but is banged up, and commenters say that even with Monangai healthy it is hard to see Swift losing the lead role because he is the more complete back.
- Coaches are talking him up: Ben Johnson and GM Ryan Poles have both been glowing over the past month, and Johnson, who has coached him for years, recently said this is the best Swift has looked at this point of a season in his career. The play-caller is the bull case — Johnson produced two fantasy-viable Bears backs last year and did the same with Gibbs and Montgomery.
- Market vs the board: the board has him 53 and the room now pays slightly earlier. He is the article's headline value around RB18 / pick 4.08, drafters call the mid-fourth rich after the market over-corrected on Monangai's injury, and he rarely reaches the fifth as the default pick for anyone who skipped RB early. Hartitz targets him at the 4/5 turn, Murchison has him as his RB2 on a number of teams, Twerski ranks him second among mid-round RBs behind Etienne and is comfortable with him as an RB1, Loza keeps ending up with him — with the caveat that she ties her enthusiasm to Monangai being hurt. The room wanted to buy the injury-scare value outright.

**Draft take:** Take him in the fourth without flinching at the cramp — he is the lead back in a Ben Johnson offense with nobody rostered behind him.
