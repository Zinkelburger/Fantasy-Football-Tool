**D'Andre Swift** (CHI, RB, bye 10) — board rank 53 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The season's first injury overreaction, now resolved, on a back the room already thought was underpriced.

- The 3 September practice exit was a cramp, not a structural injury. Swift left late in a full team period after a carry from the offense's own 1-yard line, holding his midsection, and per Sean Hammond some reports had him in significant discomfort; the Bears then put out an all-clear on Rome Odunze and pointedly said nothing about Swift, which the room read as bad news ("excluding Swift is certainly a sign, probably not a good one", 58 points). Schefter has since reported the abdomen issue was cramping, which walks back the sideline reports. The worst case commenters had feared was an oblique strain costing about three weeks. Chicago's first required injury report is Wednesday and Ben Johnson does not meet the media again until next week, so there is nothing further to confirm it before drafts.
- The room's own caveat, worth carrying: some doubt Schefter's reliability here, citing his miss on the Coby Bryant timeline. The counterweight is that this is normal Swift — he carries a perpetual Questionable tag, was Q most weeks the last two seasons and suits up, and one commenter notes he has missed one game as a Bear and six in his career.
- Role is the lead back, and the coaching staff keeps saying so. Ben Johnson, who has coached him for years, said recently this is the best Swift has looked at this point of a season in his career; Johnson and GM Ryan Poles have both been glowing about him for a month. Courtney Cronin (ESPN) had him "terrific through the first two weeks" in camp and everywhere on a Sunday — run game, passing game, taking a direct snap in 11-on-11 — i.e. full three-down usage.
- The workload path is open while Kyle Monangai is banged up. Commenters expect Swift to be a top-12 Week 1 back if Monangai is out, and even with a healthy Monangai they find it hard to see Swift losing the lead role because he is the more complete player. Chicago waived Salvon Ahmed at cutdowns (Matt Zenitz, CBS), leaving Roschon Johnson behind Swift as a blitz-pickup and special-teams RB3 — nobody currently rostered takes offensive touches from him, and the room expects Ahmed back on the practice squad as the first call-up only if Swift misses time. The standing bull case is the play-caller: Ben Johnson produced two fantasy-viable Bears backs last year and split Gibbs and Montgomery in Detroit, which is also the bear case for a ceiling.
- Market: he costs about 51st, right at his board rank, but goes earlier in practice — reported gone at 4.05 and at the 4/5 turn in multiple drafts, with posters settling for Jadarian Price when Swift went ahead of plan. One article's headline value has him RB18 at roughly pick 4.08; commenters say he was a steal in the sixth earlier in the summer and that the market over-corrected once Monangai got hurt. He is the default pick for anyone who skipped RB in the first three rounds, so he rarely reaches the fifth. Analysts line up behind him: Twerski ranks him second of the mid-round backs he believes in and is comfortable with him as an RB1; Hartitz targets him at the 4-5 turn; Murchison has him as his RB2 on a number of teams; Loza keeps ending up with him. In dynasty he is the sixth most-traded player at a 4,834 market value, trading at fair value.
- Sentiment: the room wants to buy the scare rather than fade it — "I would love to get some injury scare value on Swift, I'm in no matter what."

**Draft take:** Take him at the 4/5 turn without hesitating over the cramp; the lead role, the play-caller and an empty depth chart behind him all point the same way.
