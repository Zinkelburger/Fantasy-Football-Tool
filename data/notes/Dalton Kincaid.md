**Dalton Kincaid** (BUF, TE, bye 7) — board rank 98 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** A room that likes the player and does not believe he plays a full season — "really feeling a Kincaid year" drew 50 upvotes against a counter of low floor, no boom weeks, hurt every year.

- Availability is the entire argument. The room reports him healthy as of Sept 3, with one commenter asking about a pinky injury and getting no answer. His games played have trended down every year of his career, and he was on a pitch count for most of last season even in the games he did play. One commenter prefers Freiermuth or Juwan Johnson purely on health grounds.
- Snap share is the second doubt: he plays only about half the snaps, coming off the field on run downs because he does not block well. The counter the room accepts is that his rate metrics — aDOT, yards per route run, yards per catch — were excellent when he was out there, and that Dawson Knox only shares the field in two-TE sets and is irrelevant to his snaps if the knee holds.
- The role case for 2026 is Moore's arrival pulling safeties out of the middle, which the top-voted read (48 points) says is exactly where Kincaid feasts — a bigger seam and middle-of-the-field role, conditional as ever on his staying healthy.
- Market: usually available in round 11 of a 12-team draft, going around pick 103, which the room treats as the price of a zero-TE build. Hartitz rates him around TE10 and wants him or Isaiah Likely in the round 7-10 range if he missed Bowers and Loveland early; Cooper puts him in the tier he waits for — Kelce, Andrews, Likely, Goedert — after taking seven to nine backs and receivers first.
- Sentiment adds little beyond the price: drafters cite glowing beat reports all summer and Josh Allen's passer rating when targeting him.

**Draft take:** A round-11 zero-TE starter whose ceiling is capped by a half-snap role and a history of missed games — draft him, and draft a second tight end behind him.
