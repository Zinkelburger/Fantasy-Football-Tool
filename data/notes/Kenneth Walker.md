**Kenneth Walker** (KC, RB, bye 5) — board rank 22 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The best backfield situation he has ever had, attached to a durability record the room has argued about for a month without settling it.

- **The late-August foot/ankle issue is resolved.** He returned to a full practice on Wednesday 9/2 with no limitation reported, and Chiefs beat writer Kuntz confirmed full participation in the media window that day. Before that it was vague enough that one analyst had him tiered as "pending this weird shoe injury?".
- The durability argument is genuinely two-sided. One thread posts the full history: 2022 hamstring, sports hernia and two ankle sprains; 2023 groin, ankle, shoulder and repeated obliques; 2024 two Grade 1 abdominal strains, a calf strain and a Grade 2 ankle sprain that put him on IR for a career-low 11 games; 2025 camp foot soreness and a Week 12 glute issue while playing all 17. The 142-point rebuttal counts 10 missed games in four years, mostly from one injury, plus a cited stat that 73% of RBs miss at least a game. The Seahawks-fan version matters most for usage: the injuries follow workload, which is why Seattle capped him.
- **Kansas City signed him to be the lead back, and the reported split is not a bell cow.** A commenter relays Andy Reid saying the backfield would be 60/40 in Walker's favour; others expect closer to 70% on the size of the contract. Camp reports have him getting the ball "all the time", working as the main red-zone back and the primary screen target, and Matt McMullen (Chiefs.com) writes his pass-catching has flashed nearly every day. Behind him are only Emmett Johnson and Brashard Smith, and with Mahomes coming off an ACL, KC is expected to run more.
- **The bear case is his own résumé, not the situation.** In four seasons he has never topped roughly 250 touches, never hit double-digit TDs, never reached 300 receiving yards, never caught more than 46 passes and never finished better than RB15, with finishes of RB18/19/22/27 and PPG of 13.1, 16.5 and 11.3. Seattle split him roughly 70-30 between the 20s with Charbonnet taking the goal line inside the 10, on 36 targets. The counter to Andy Reid optimism: Mahomes rarely plays under center, keeps his eyes downfield rather than checking down, and Reid's goal-line trickery vultures scores.
- **Market: rank 22 matches what the room pays, roughly.** Reported at early-to-middle second round, several taking him at 1.11-1.13 rather than risk it, one at the high $40s in auction; the repeated view is he will not last past mid-round 2. Maher calls him the value of the second-round back group in non-PPR at RB7 and 15th overall; Murchison calls him a sure-fire RB1 if healthy.
- A 1,253-point thread split cleanly: top comment "the drumbeats for this guy are deafening" (722), top reply that the hype is already baked into the price (229).

**Draft take:** A fair second-round price for the first genuine lead role of his career, and the health record is the thing you are actually buying — take him at the 1/2 turn only if you can also stomach Emmett Johnson late.
