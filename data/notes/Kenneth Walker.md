**Kenneth Walker** (KC, RB, bye 5) — board rank 22 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The most argued-over back in the second round — everyone agrees the Kansas City job is real, and nobody agrees he can hold it for seventeen games.

- Healthy going into Week 1. An ankle or foot issue surfaced in late August (a Ringer host: "definitely watch the ken walker foot thing that just popped up, could be nothing but he's had foot issues"), but he was a full participant at the Sept 2 practice with no limitation noted, per Chiefs beat writer Kuntz and the practice report. Treat the earlier scare as closed.
- Durability is the whole bear case and it is genuinely contested. One side counts a Seattle pitch count, career snap shares of 47-64%, and a back who misses two to four games a normal year; a 142-point reply counters that he has missed 10 games in four seasons, mostly from a single injury. He has also never carried a bellcow load — Seattle split his reps even in his healthiest, most effective year, at a 48% carry share.
- The role is as good as the position offers. He signed as Kansas City's only major offensive investment at $30M guaranteed; a commenter relays Andy Reid putting the backfield split at 60/40 in Walker's favor, while others expect closer to 70% given the contract. Chiefs.com's Matt McMullen wrote in camp that his vision and contact balance were consistently impressive and his pass-catching flashed nearly every day. The room expects a run-heavier Chiefs season with Mahomes coming off an ACL, and expects Walker to absorb the manufactured screen and jet-motion work that used to go to Rashee Rice.
- The counter-case is scheme, not talent: Mahomes rarely plays under center, keeps his eyes downfield rather than checking down, and Andy Reid's goal-line trickery has historically split the touchdowns. A dissenter adds the cold record — in four seasons Walker has never topped roughly 250 touches, never hit double-digit TDs, never reached 300 receiving yards and never finished RB15 or better.
- Board and room agree on price. Drafters report paying a late second or the 2/3 turn against an ADP near 21. Maher calls him the value of the group in non-PPR at RB7, 15th overall against a consensus of 20, and has him rising rather than falling from half PPR to standard; Twerski ranks him second among hero-RB builds.
**Draft take:** Take him at the turn as your RB1 if you can absorb a two-to-four-game absence — the volume and the goal-line work are the best he has ever had, but you are paying a second-round price for a workload he has never actually carried.
