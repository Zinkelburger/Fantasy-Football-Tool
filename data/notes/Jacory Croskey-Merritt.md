**Jacory Croskey-Merritt** (WAS, RB, bye 7) — board rank 100 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Likely Washington lead back whose case the room keeps discounting for the offensive line and a total absence of receiving work.

- Role: expected to open as Washington's lead back with Rachaad White taking the majority of the pass-catching work; one commenter's read is that the two cannibalize each other into fantasy irrelevance (Aug 11). Nothing since contradicts the split.
- The line is what moved the market. That same commenter had been getting more bullish on camp usage reports until Washington lost its starting left tackle for most of the year, and dropped him specifically for that. Twerski (Sep 3) calls RB25 about his ceiling even if he wins the backfield outright — one of the worst offensive lines in the league plus no receiving work is a bad combination in PPR.
- Receiving profile is the hard cap: Dopp cites 12 receptions last season, Washington's addition of a capable receiving back in White, and a quarterback who takes rushing work, leaving a touchdown-dependent flex in a split backfield. Dopp ranks him last of Brooks/Dobbins/Rodriguez/JCM and prefers Jonathon Brooks despite the injury history; Loza has him around RB40 and prefers Blake Corum.
- The bull case is thin but honest: he did not enter last season as the starter and a supporter argues for a step forward now that he is. Detractors counter no vision, barely 3 YPC in half his games, and no PPR value at all; one owner who rostered him says "he was terrible and nothing has changed" (35 upvotes) while another argues he wins the backfield because White is bad outside of receiving. Two more optimistic predictions have him an RB2 and Washington's back for three to five years, or a Jamaal Williams line of 900-1,100 yards with 15-plus touchdowns.
- Market: board rank 100 against drafters reporting they got him in the 11th round (Sep 2), where the consensus still called him too low and split on whether the line justifies the discount. So the room buys him later than the board and argues about it. One dynasty take raises selling on the general principle that teams move off late-round backs regardless of production.

**Draft take:** Buy the 11th-round version on volume, but don't spend a pick near his board rank on a touchdown-dependent flex in a split backfield behind that line.
