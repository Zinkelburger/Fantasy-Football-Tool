**Jacory Croskey-Merritt** (WAS, RB, bye 7) — board rank 100 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Nobody disputes the lead-back job; the objections are the offensive line and the fact he does not catch passes.

- Role: Washington's starting back, with Rachaad White taking the majority of the pass-catching work and Jayden Daniels taking rushing work off the top. One OP expects the two backs to cannibalize each other into fantasy irrelevance.
- The receiving hole is the recurring number: only 12 receptions last season. Twerski calls RB25 about his ceiling even if he wins the backfield outright — one of the worst offensive lines in the league plus no receiving work is a bad combination in PPR. Dopp reaches the same place, calling him a TD-dependent flex in a split backfield, and ranks him last of Brooks, Dobbins, Rodriguez and JCM. Loza puts him around RB40 and prefers Blake Corum.
- The dated change is the line, not the depth chart: an OP who had been getting more bullish on camp usage lowered him in his rankings on 08-11 specifically because Washington lost its starting left tackle for most of the year.
- Bull case: he did not enter last season as the starter and should take a step forward now that he is; one commenter prefers him to Jonathon Brooks and names him the Washington back to own, not being high on White as a runner. Two predictions in one thread have him as an RB2 and Washington's back for three to five years, one projecting a Jamaal Williams line of 900-1,100 yards with 15-plus touchdowns.
- Bear case, and it has the votes: on a 20-upvote comment that is just his nickname shouted, the 35-upvote reply is "not behind that offensive line," and a manager who rostered him last year says he was terrible and nothing has changed. Detractors add no vision, barely 3 YPC in half his games and zero PPR value.
- Market: drafters report getting him in the 11th round, later than board rank 100 — the room calls even that too low but splits on whether the line justifies it. One post floats selling on the general principle that teams move off late-round backs regardless of production.

**Draft take:** A touchdown-dependent early-down back behind a bad line — good value in the 11th, a mistake at any price where he costs you a receiver.
