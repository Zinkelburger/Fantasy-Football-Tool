**Bucky Irving** (TB, RB, bye 10) — board rank 36 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** genuinely bearish, and paired against David Montgomery in one thread as the most-overrated to Montgomery's most-underrated.

- Health: he had offseason shoulder surgery and was only fully cleared right before camp, having played through the shoulder for much of last season and averaged 3.4 yards a carry down the stretch. One commenter says the shoulder is healed and that is why he expects Irving rather than Sean Tucker on red-zone work. The room still treats him as fragile — "Bucky never seems to stay healthy" — and one says a feature-back workload produced two serious injuries last year.
- Role is the argument, and it has moved against him since August. The 9/4 read is a three-man committee, with one commenter estimating he loses about 40% of snaps to Kenny Gainwell and Tucker. Hartitz projects Irving at roughly a 50% snap share with Gainwell near 35% and Tucker near 15% — but the other two taking the most valuable touches, targets and goal-line carries — and likens it directly to the Tuten/Rodriguez split.
- Bucs fans push back: the team loves him, he has looked great in camp, and they project 50-55% of touches and close to 300 if he is healthy, with Gainwell taking passing-down work rather than the job. The receiving argument is theirs too — 528 receiving YAC in 2024 on fewer receptions than Gainwell, 8.3 YAC per catch to Gainwell's 6.7 — and the room's answer to the Tucker fear is that Tucker only got goal-line work last year because Irving was playing through the shoulder. All of that holds only while Irving is healthy; Gainwell's share rises immediately if he is not.
- Two structural knocks that do not depend on the committee: Liam Coen, the OC when he broke out in 2024, is gone, and one commenter argues the near-every-down usage he got is unsustainable for his size and that a rotation is coming back on purpose. He led Tampa in carries inside the five only as a rookie.
- Market has slid a full round past the board. Board rank 36, but Maher has his ADP at 46-47 and ranks Bhayshul Tuten above him; Twerski has him immediately behind Tuten and lists him among low-end RB2s worth trading for; Cooper was out on him in round 4 but is fine at the September price in round 6; Sanderson names him one of two backs he actively likes in the 5-6 range. Reported picks are the 4th, 5th and 6th, and a 215-point comment says "not interested unless he falls to the 5th". Dynasty has him RB20, which several call far too low.

**Draft take:** let the board's round-3 rank go and take him in the 5th or 6th where the room is actually drafting him — the touches are real, the goal-line and passing-down work are not his, and the shoulder is one bad hit from Gainwell.
