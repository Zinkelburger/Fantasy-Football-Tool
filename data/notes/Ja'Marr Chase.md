**Ja'Marr Chase** (CIN, WR, bye 6) — board rank 3 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** A take-regardless top-three pick carrying a knee the room has not stopped watching since 25 August.

- The knee, in order. He left Tuesday's practice on 8/25 after landing awkwardly on an underthrown deep ball; per Ben Baby he walked off under his own power, was back on the sideline unsupervised, in good spirits and reenacting the landing with teammates. Chase himself called it "a little hyperextension" and said he could have kept practising. He did not practise on 8/26 after banging up the same left knee again — notably wearing no protective equipment on it, which the room read as precautionary rather than a setback.
- Latest team word, 1 September: per Ben Baby, Zac Taylor says Chase will be limited in practice through the week but that he "feels good about him." He is practising in some capacity and nothing from the team puts Week 1 in doubt. Analysts still describe him as banged up in early September — Maher framed his own third pick as a choice between a banged-up Chase and a banged-up Puka Nacua, and Hartitz cites the knee as part of why he would rather take Christian McCaffrey at 4.
- Role is unchanged and barely discussed, which is what you would expect. The one note: a Bengals fan says Chase has been working short and goal-line fades, reading his own 23-touchdown talk as a plan to take some of the running backs' scoring upside.
- Market: he went 1.01 "in a ton of leagues" and is FantasyPros' consensus #1 in PPR and Yahoo's #1 WR; in a 12-team superflex auction he cost 26% of budget, ahead of McCaffrey, Barkley and Taylor. The knee news produced a one-afternoon discount only — people report taking him in the late 1st, 3rd and 4th rounds that day — and it did not stick. The room's own stated contingency was that if the knee were still ambiguous at draft time he could slide to 6-8, and it did not come to that. One drafter passed on him at pick five for a fully healthy Jaxon Smith-Njigba and got pushed back on hard.
- Sentiment: the surrounding worry is Joe Burrow's availability, not Chase's talent. The long argument is about 2025, when he finished WR4-WR5 with Burrow out half the year and owners who paid a top-two pick or $69 of a $200 auction budget point to five or six dud weeks and two missed games. Murchison would not let him past 1.03 and ranks him ahead of Taylor and McCaffrey.

**Draft take:** Still the first receiver off the board and a fine pick at 1.01-1.03 — the knee is limited-practice noise, and the real risk in the profile is Burrow's health, not his.
