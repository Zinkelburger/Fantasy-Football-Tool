**Bijan Robinson** (ATL, RB, bye 11) — board rank 2 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** heavily nominated as a bust — about 1,175 upvotes in one thread and 136 in another — and in every case the argument is Atlanta's quarterback play and roster, never his ability.

- No availability question at all: he has never missed a game, and the room rejects the "due for injury regression" line outright. The only blip anyone can name is a game he technically played and finished with 0.9 half-PPR points.
- Role is bell-cow and getting bigger. He logged 366 touches in 2025, third behind McCaffrey (413) and Taylor (369), and is one of only six backs who cleared a 70% snap share that season, at 16.9 carries a game. He put up roughly 2,300 scrimmage yards and 12 touchdowns on an offense the room agrees was bad, on 103 targets and 79 catches.
- The goal-line story broke on 9/1 and the room was overwhelmingly unbothered — 35 top-level comments, 1,358 upvotes. The rebuttals that carried: he has been the second-best back on rush attempts inside the five over the last two seasons; his touchdown total was suppressed by an offense that scored 350 points against 480-plus for the top teams, not by Tyler Allgeier; and this is no worse than the Allgeier years, which he won leagues through. Allgeier is now gone — the two split red-zone carries 32-32 in 2025 — leaving Brian Robinson as the complement. He signed first on 8/4, three years for $66.75M with $51M guaranteed.
- The quarterback change is read as a positive. Atlanta runs designed pass plays for him and drew him around 110 targets last year, and Tua checks down to backs constantly — the room's line is that Bijan is "QB and team proof" because either quarterback hands off. The honest caveat one commenter attaches: without Cousins the realistic receiving floor is nearer 65 catches, and defences can stack the box given how little help he has.
- The one specific, sourced fade is Hartitz's rule against backs from likely bottom-10 scoring offenses: only 14 running backs from such offenses have posted top-12 PPR-per-game numbers in a decade, and never more than two in a season. That is why he has Jahmyr Gibbs as the clear 1.01.
- Market and board agree: he goes 1.02-1.03 almost everywhere, in a tier with Gibbs, and the room's answer to "am I an idiot for taking Bijan first" was a clear no led by a 124-point comment. Managers report him occasionally falling to 1.04-1.07 when Chase, Taylor, CMC, Puka or Smith-Njigba go early, and a "he won't be a top-5 RB" comment was voted to -33.

**Draft take:** take him at 1.02 without hesitating, and take him gladly at 1.04 — the fade is entirely about Atlanta's offense, and the touch count, the snap share and the vacated red-zone work are all his.
