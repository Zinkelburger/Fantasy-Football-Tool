**George Kittle** (SF, TE, bye 8) — board rank 102 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room drafts him happily and will not start him — a discount on a top-6 tight end whose Week 1 is a coin flip.

- Achilles, and the news is trending his way. Ian Rapoport reported on 2 September that Kittle will travel to Australia with the 49ers and is "trending in the right direction to play" in the 10 September opener against the Rams, after two positive practice days; San Francisco had already activated him from Active/PUP in late August, per Schefter. He is eight-plus months post-surgery at Week 1, following a lost season — a Week 1 hamstring, a re-injury, then the torn Achilles in the playoffs.
- Why the timeline is faster than the literature: the tear was high and clean near the soleus rather than mid-tendon, so more blood supply and no screws in the ankle, which specialists in these threads call the best-case version of the injury — the watershed mid-tendon tear covers 75-85% of cases. He had a modern SpeedBridge repair, which the physical therapist says allows faster recovery than the technique the pessimistic research is based on. A doctor relayed in another thread says tight ends have the highest rate of return to pre-injury level from an Achilles of any skill position. The same voices still want one to two more months of ramp-up before he is fully himself, and expect him to lean on experience early while the athleticism comes back.
- The room does not buy the green light as a start recommendation. "Trending in the right direction" plus boarding a plane is called the softest possible green light, and lining up for 40 snaps after that travel is a different sentence; several suspect Shanahan gamesmanship. Most who drafted him also rostered a backup — Juwan Johnson, Jake Tonges, Isaiah Likely, Mark Andrews — and the top reply in one thread (32 points) says he would not start him and wishes he had stayed home. Hartitz would start Juwan Johnson or Likely over him in Week 1. The dissent that matters: commenters dismiss the idea of a snap count outright — "this is a Shanahan team, if you're playing you're getting your full workload" (41 points), with McCaffrey cited as the precedent for a game-time decision who then took 33 touches.
- Role, if he is on the field: San Francisco has almost nobody else to throw to besides Deebo Samuel, De'Zhaun Stribling and dump-offs to McCaffrey, so he gets fed. He finished second among tight ends from Weeks 9-17 at 18.0 points per game and top-6 at the position in seven of eight seasons. Two live caps on that: his blocking is load-bearing for the run game — "the 49ers run game doesn't work at all without Kittle" — and one commenter argues the scheme only unleashes him a couple of games a year.
- Market: he falls to the 8th through 10th rounds, later than his board rank and about two rounds after Tucker Kraft, with managers describing the 10th as a steal; a 116-point comment calls him the best tight end value in drafts this year, and the room says he should be going ahead of Kyle Pitts and Harold Fannin. Dopp names him a top-three value pick at ADP. The counter-price is the 5th round, which those same commenters call too rich. In dynasty superflex he went below his 4,153 market value in 68% of 236 trades. The bear framing is that he is being drafted at his best-case recovery outcome and is really a 2027 play.

**Draft take:** Draft him in the 8th or later and roster a startable tight end behind him for the first month — the price is right, the Week 1 snap count is not knowable, and his game is athleticism he has not got all the way back.
