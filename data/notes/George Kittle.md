**George Kittle** (SF, TE, bye 8) — board rank 102 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the medical reporting is unusually optimistic and the managers who drafted him still will not start him in Week 1.

- Eight months out from Achilles surgery and trending toward playing. Ian Rapoport reported on 2026-09-02 that Kittle traveled to Australia with the 49ers for the Sept. 10 opener against the Rams after two positive practice days and is "trending in the right direction to play"; he came off Active/PUP on 08-23 per Schefter. The in-thread pushback is fair: that phrase plus boarding a plane is called the softest possible green light.
- The optimism has specific medical grounding: the tear was high and clean near the soleus, with muscle involvement and better blood supply, rather than the mid-tendon watershed tear in 75-85% of cases, and he had a modern SpeedBridge repair that recovers faster than the technique the pessimistic literature is based on. The same voices still put him one to two months of ramp-up short of fully healthy, and expect him to lean on experience early while athleticism returns. The bear case is age: 32, and drafted at his best-case recovery outcome.
- Week 1 usage is genuinely unknown. The room expects a snap count at best and several think he does not play — long travel, Jake Tonges is a capable backup, the rest of the offense is healthy. The counter-precedent, at 41 points: Shanahan does not do snap counts, "if you're playing you're getting your full workload", with McCaffrey as the example.
- Role once he is on the field is load-bearing: San Francisco has little else to throw to besides Deebo Samuel, De'Zhaun Stribling and 100-plus dump-offs to McCaffrey, he was the target hog above Jauan Jennings the last two years, and the run game measurably works better with him blocking. The dissent is that the scheme uses him to block and unleashes him a couple of games a year.
- Market: he is falling to the 8th through 10th rounds, about two rounds after Tucker Kraft and well past his board rank of 102, with the room saying he should go ahead of Kyle Pitts and Harold Fannin. Dopp names him a top-three value at ADP and a 116-point comment calls him the best TE value in drafts; earlier reports of a 5th-round price were called too rich.
- Sentiment: hedging, not commitment. The top reply on the travel news (32 pts) still says no, one puts it at 50/50 that he plays but is not the same player, and most who drafted him also rostered a backup TE; Hartitz would start Juwan Johnson or Isaiah Likely over him to open.

**Draft take:** draft him in the 8th or later and pair him with a startable TE for the first month — the discount is real, but a Week 1 snap count is not something to build a lineup on.
