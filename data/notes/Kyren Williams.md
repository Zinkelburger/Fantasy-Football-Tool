**Kyren Williams** (LAR, RB, bye 11) — board rank 23 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Defended hard — the room's line is that the annual split narrative has now been wrong three years running — with the real dissent aimed at his goal-line share, not his job.

- The job is not in question, and the room's lead evidence is money: a three-year, $33M extension with $23M guaranteed, in year one, at 26. Teams do not pay that to a back they intend to split evenly. That comment carried 773 points.
- He holds it on blocking and receiving rather than rushing. The room repeatedly calls him one of the league's best pass-protecting backs and says that is why McVay plays him on third down and in the playoffs; Blake Corum's rushing edge is described as marginal. Corum was under 38% of snaps across the last five games including the playoffs, and Kyren is a team captain.
- Workload 2025: 309 touches, about a 65% share. His median snap share fell from 77% early to 58.25% across the final 12 weeks as Corum's carries rose — read in-thread as rest management, not demotion. **The minority role risk is real and specific:** with an improved Rams defence and a 38-year-old Stafford, LA may ice games early and hand the fourth quarter to Corum, cutting touches without costing him the lead job.
- Production cited in his defence: 44 touchdowns in three years, career yards per carry above 4.5, the league lead in RB success rate in 2025, and a top-10 finish three straight years. He is still the short-yardage and goal-line back, though he lost a game-losing goal-line fumble to San Francisco.
- The best-supported fade is efficiency decay near the end zone: rushing fantasy points per game inside the 10 have fallen 7.7 to 5.9 to 5.0 across three seasons, and receiving FPG from 5.9 in 2023 to 4.4 over the last two. He was only 11th in FPG among backs (15.6) on the No. 1 scoring offence (30.0 PPG) behind the No. 1 run-blocking line. Several commenters add that the Rams' one-yard runs have become one-yard passes to Davante Adams — **that fade lasts only as long as Adams keeps the goal-line target share**, and the counter-argument is that those slant TDs regress back into rushing scores.
- Analysts split on price, not role. Hartitz would be "REALLY surprised" if Kyren returns to 2023-24 form without Corum going down. Sanderson's contrarian take targets exactly this profile — volume-and-environment backs pushed back up the board on the theory the dead-zone fade was wrong — and ranks him far behind Hampton, Achane and Henry in half PPR. Maher is a spot or two ahead of consensus, so no edge at cost.
- Market matches the board: around pick 21, round 2 in 12-team, 14th RB off the board in Sleeper PPR. The annual downfall prediction is treated in-thread as three years wrong.

**Draft take:** Take him at the 2/3 turn as a floor RB1 and expect the split talk to be wrong again — the thing that would actually cost you points is Adams keeping the goal line, not Corum taking the job.
