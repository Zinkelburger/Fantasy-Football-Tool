**Rome Odunze** (CHI, WR, bye 10) — board rank 66 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the cheaper Bears receiver, and in redraft the room is taking him ahead of Luther Burden anyway.

- Cleared after Thursday's practice exit, with the room's own caveat attached. He came up hobbling on his right leg during an install period on 2026-09-03, tried to stay in, and trainers pulled him off; per beat writer Sean Hammond and follow-up reporting he "should be all good", and that all-clear landed before the D'Andre Swift update the same day. The room reads it as heat and end-of-camp precaution, with him attempting to return as the tell. The skepticism is specific: Odunze himself told reporters Bears safety Coby Bryant "should be fine" after an August practice injury, and Bryant then had surgery and was ruled out four to six months.
- The foot, not the leg, is the standing question. He described managing it as his "new normal", then later said it feels amazing and is not limiting him, which the room takes as the operative quote. Injury analyst Jeff Mueller warned (2026-08-18) about biomechanical change after a foot stress fracture, compensatory injury risk and a possible loss of burst that would specifically limit a vertical role; one commenter flags managed plantar fasciitis.
- Role: the outside X, facing the jam and the tougher coverage, in the part of the field where Caleb Williams is least accurate. He led Chicago at a 17% target share last season (DJ Moore 16%) and was WR7 in points per game on about seven targets a game over the first eight weeks — but the route-share environment got harder, with Colston Loveland and Luther Burden projecting 75-80%+ route shares against 20-40% last year. Cooper rates him the biggest target threat in Chicago; the competing read has him third most weeks, a spiky field-stretcher. He gains directly while Burden is out.
- Market: ADP around 68, half a round behind Burden — but redrafters are not paying that order, taking Odunze ahead of Burden in three of four drafts one commenter ran, early fifth in one and late fourth in another. Dynasty is the reverse: the most-agreed answer in one thread (101 upvotes) has him going three to four rounds after Burden in startups, and owners will not sell a cheap share at any price.
- Sentiment: repeatedly named the best value of the Bears pass catchers, with several floating a third-year breakout. Podcaster Chris Harris prefers him to Burden on hands and route-running while doubting he was at full strength after the injury; the counter is that Burden is the one the head coach keeps praising.

**Draft take:** take him in the 5th and let the room pay up for Burden — the foot history, not the Thursday leg scare, is the thing that would sink it.
