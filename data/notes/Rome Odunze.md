**Rome Odunze** (CHI, WR, bye 10) — board rank 66 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room's preferred Bears receiver over Luther Burden and the price has already caught up, with a foot history and a crowded target tree as the two real objections.

- He left Bears practice on 3 September hobbling with what looked like a right-leg issue during an install period, per Sean Hammond; trainers pulled him off although he tried to stay in and did not look overly concerned. Preliminary indications, and a follow-up report, are that he "should be all good", and the room treats it as a nothing burger precisely because he tried to return — the expectation is no games missed, or one out of caution. His 9/4 exit alongside D'Andre Swift is read as end-of-camp heat.
- The room does not fully trust that all-clear, and says why: Odunze himself told reporters that Bears safety Coby Bryant "should be fine" after an August practice injury, and Bryant then had surgery and was ruled out four to six months. Take the "should be all good" as what it is.
- The older foot problem is the one with a named source attached. Injury analyst Jeff Mueller warned at length in August about Odunze's "new normal" after a foot stress fracture — potential biomechanical changes, risk of compensatory injuries, possible regression in burst and acceleration that would specifically limit a vertical role — and advised caution. Odunze had described managing the foot as his own "new normal" before later saying it feels amazing and is not limiting him; the room mostly treats the second quote as operative, and one commenter separately flagged managed plantar fasciitis.
- Role: he led all Chicago receivers at a 17% target share last season, ahead of DJ Moore's 16%, and averaged more fantasy points per game than Moore did — with Moore gone the room moves him from 1B to 1A. He was WR7 in points per game before his injury on about seven targets a game over the first eight weeks. The caveat is the shape of the role: Cooper has him as the outside X facing the jam and tougher coverage, and the quoted August analysis has him facing 75-80%+ route shares from Colston Loveland and Luther Burden rather than the 20-40% they ran last year, while keeping the boom-bust vertical assignment where Caleb Williams is least accurate. One commenter casts him third in the pecking order most weeks behind Loveland and Burden while both are healthy. BettingPros has his line at 775 yards.
- Market: ADP 68, right around his board rank, but in live drafts he goes earlier — ahead of Burden in three of four drafts one commenter ran, late fourth and early fifth in others, and one manager admits reaching at the 4/5 turn. The discount that existed in August, when he went nearly two rounds after Burden, is gone. In dynasty startups he still goes three to four rounds after Burden, and managers report being unable to buy a cheap Rome share at any price. Twerski is the dissent: he would rather take Bhayshul Tuten or Parker Washington than draft Odunze in that range and prefers Burden comfortably.
- Sentiment: repeatedly named the best value among the Bears pass catchers, with four top-level predictions in one thread ranging from top 10 to top 3 at the position, and several floating him as this year's JSN-style third-year breakout. Chris Harris has been higher on him than Burden — better hands, better route runner at this stage — while noting he was not at full strength after the injury.

**Draft take:** Fine in the 5th and a reach in the 4th — the target share and the vacated Moore volume are real, but you are buying a vertical role in a three-way tree from a quarterback who has struggled to feed it.
