**Dalton Schultz** (HOU, TE, bye 8) — board rank 141 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The best-established deep tight end in the corpus, on a target share created by injuries around him — and the room's own caveat is that Houston just got healthy at his position.

- Role: Houston's TE1, occupying roughly 30% of the Texans' slot snaps, which one commenter uses to argue the room is overvaluing Houston's slot receiver job. With the receiving room thinned out — only while Tank Dell and Jayden Higgins are out for the season — a podcast host's line is that "realistically the biggest boost is to Dalton Schultz." Another thread names him a deep TE target because the Houston receiver room is "decimated."
- Target competition: Nico Collins takes his share, Kayshon Boutte is a deep threat rather than a target hog, so the only real competition for underneath volume is rookie Jaylin Noel.
- The counterweight is real and less discussed: Houston now has four healthy tight ends, where last season injuries left Schultz as the only one and forced OC Caley to abandon 12 and 13 personnel for a six-lineman package. Commenters expect a lot of two-tight-end sets this year — that restores Caley's preferred structure but spreads tight end snaps beyond Schultz. One says rookie Marlin Klein is already the TE2 and fighting Schultz for the job; the rest of the room is behind him, with Brevin Jordan often injured and a poor blocker and Cade Stover coming off a knee injury.
- He is described as a decent pass catcher who is not much of a blocker — which is why the two-TE sets are a threat rather than an opportunity.
- Analyst takes: Cooper calls him the deepest tight end he would be comfortable starting Week 1, and only in 14-16 team or two-TE leagues, noting two more touchdowns would have moved him from about TE10 to TE4 and that he is usually still available when you need him. Loza takes Schultz over Hunter Henry for floor in a tight-end pairing while calling the pecking order behind Nico Collins a massive question mark.

**Draft take:** A last-round floor tight end in deep or two-TE leagues — the receiver injuries are the entire case, and Houston's healthy tight end room quietly argues the other way.
