**Chase Brown** (CIN, RB, bye 6) — board rank 17 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the most-agreed-with name in several threads and, in the same breath, the player 13 self-aware commenters said they cannot bring themselves to draft at the right price.

- Role: Cincinnati's unquestioned lead back, with commenters expecting "another 300 touch season." Bengals fans say there is no replacement on the roster — running back is the team's worst position group, with an aging Samaje Perine in a niche role and Tahj Brooks not panning out — so the workload is unthreatened this season. He out-touched Perine 115 to 62 over the last six games.
- How he is used, per Ben Solak (ESPN): multiple Bengals snaps with Brown in the slot and flying across the formation in jet motion, on 45 slot snaps and 25 out wide in 2025 — Cincinnati prefers him as a pass catcher and not a pass protector. The room's scouting agrees: route running, hands and yards after catch, plus a downhill runner who can turn the corner, with 328 carries in his senior college season offered as proof he can take a heavy load.
- Only while Joe Burrow is on the field: the top-5 case is explicitly conditional. He was fine in Week 1 last year, dropped off for eight weeks after Burrow's surgery, then averaged 22.3 PPG as the overall RB2 from Weeks 13-18 on roughly a 63% snap share. Flacco having a full offseason is the stated mitigation, and it is a partial one.
- The ceiling argument is scheme, not job security: Zac Taylor's system will never be run-heavy, and Brown is at best the third most important skill player behind Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins, sometimes fourth on Gesicki days. The counter is that this makes him script-proof — third-best pass catcher in a high-volume offense with a bad defense. Note the room also says Cincinnati receiver injury news is not a Brown boost: he is better off with Chase and Higgins healthy.
- Market: the room pays at or above the board. He goes late 1st/early 2nd, ahead of Saquon, Achane and Jacobs in most drafts and ahead of Hampton everywhere except ESPN boards; one commenter would take him at pick 9. Hartitz names Brown, Cook and Barkley his big three from that cluster and ranks him over Walker and Hampton; Murchison has been pounding the table all summer; Dopp gives him a real shot at a top-5 finish off 21+ PPG from Week 8 on. Keepers are near-unanimous: Brown at $11 over Hampton at $26 was a no-brainer (47 pts).
- The fade is honest rather than dismissive: his last-10-game production is called unsustainable, his good games came mostly against bad defenses, and he had long touchdown droughts. Several say plainly that he is correctly valued with no competition for carries and still will not draft him. Separately and not a 2026 usage question: the room expects Cincinnati to let a 27-year-old fifth-round back walk after this contract year.

**Draft take:** a first-round-price bell cow whose floor is tied entirely to Burrow's health — take him at the 1/2 turn in PPR and accept you are buying the quarterback's availability as much as the back.
