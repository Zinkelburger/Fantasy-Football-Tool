**A.J. Brown** (NE, WR, bye 11) — board rank 28 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** one 2,058-upvote thread is entirely about him, and its conclusion is that he is a good pick with a high floor — while rejecting the premise that a second-round receiver is ever low risk.

- Health is the live question and the evidence points both ways. The most-repeated risk is knees the room calls degenerative: both scoped, apparently needing to be drained since, plus a recurring hamstring several read as a receiver aging. Two commenters say the Rams pursued a trade for him and backed off specifically over the knee; one expects four to five games lost to the hamstring on his established pattern. Against that, commenters following camp say he has participated fully and has not missed a day of practice all summer, camp buzz is positive, and he torched the Eagles secondary in a joint practice.
- The strongest decline evidence is charted, not anecdotal: Matt Harmon has his route win rates falling from the 90th percentile or better against man, zone and press to roughly the 40th-50th in each — from a rare three-category elite profile to average. His 2025 line was 80/1,000/7 for WR14 (WR11 PPR by one count) on 121 targets and a 26.65% target share, with the lowest yards per target and per reception of his career. The room disputes whether that is age, the offense or effort.
- Role in New England is entrenched: the WR1 after the offseason trade, catching from Drake Maye, with the room near-unanimous that Romeo Doubs and Hunter Henry are not serious competition, especially with Kayshon Boutte traded away. One commenter expects 150-plus targets. Two scheme objections: the Patriots throw only about 25-30 times a game, and in Josh McDaniels' offense only Randy Moss and Davante Adams have led in catches and yards from outside in twenty years. Plus a split on his last stint with Vrabel — across 43 games in Tennessee he finished under 50 yards or under five catches in 32 of them, high efficiency and low volume.
- The behavioural risk is argued at length by Eagles fans: a behind-the-scenes trade request after week 4, publicly calling the offense a shitshow, giving up on routes late, and drops in the Wild Card loss. The direct rebuttal is that Pro Football Reference credits him with one drop all last season — the lowest drop rate of any receiver with 70-plus targets.
- The room pays slightly ahead of the board. He goes at the 2/3 turn as roughly WR6-WR8, Sleeper ADP around 19.5, reported picks from 12 to 34, and he will not last past pick 16 in New England-area leagues. Hartitz ranks him over Justin Jefferson and CeeDee Lamb; the standing advice is to take Lamb or Jefferson at 1.07-1.10 and still get Brown in the 2nd.

**Draft take:** buy him at the 2/3 turn where the target share is nearly free of competition, but do not reach into the late first for a 29-year-old whose route wins have already halved.
