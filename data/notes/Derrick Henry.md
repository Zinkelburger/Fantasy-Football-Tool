**Derrick Henry** (BAL, RB, bye 13) — board rank 13 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Nobody disputes the workload; the entire argument is age, and the room notes it has lost that argument five years running.

- The job is not in question. The room treats him as the unquestioned lead back — the reason Baltimore can stash a rookie back on IR — with Adam Randall developing behind him and no commenter suggesting a split. He averaged 18.1 carries a game in 2025, enough that Keaton Mitchell could not find touches.
- Per Jeremy Fowler (ESPN, 8/11), Henry gets his normal heavy workload under a coordinator who comes from Chicago's third-ranked rushing attack, though it may look different at times: Fowler saw designed runs out on the perimeter, and Baltimore will try to get Henry involved in the passing game at least somewhat. A commenter expects the same — more outside runs and more passing-game work on top of the goal-line role.
- The caps are format and shape. Cooper notes he has not been a full-time back for a while and gives up the pass work, which costs him in full PPR. Last season's 1,700 yards and 16 touchdowns came with huge fantasy-playoff games but poor early weeks even when Lamar Jackson was healthy, and the value now rests on a goal-line monopoly; two commenters tie the down weeks to Justice Hill's health and expect Hill to take more touches this year. One flags the loss of center Tyler Linderbaum as under-discussed, with the likely successor on season-ending IR.
- Market: a round-2 target off the turn rather than a first-rounder, going within 10-15 picks of Kyren Williams, with the board a touch more aggressive than the room. The format split is explicit — Maher moves him from 23rd in half PPR to 17th and RB8 in standard. Murchison's half-PPR order for that second-round group is Henry, Achane, Hampton, Kenneth Walker, Kyren Williams; Twerski ranks him last among the 1/2-turn hero-RB options and names him one of his ADP fades.
- Sentiment: twelve top-level fades in one thread (167 upvotes) on age and cumulative mileage, with several adding that last year's totals were an illusion built after his owners were already sunk. What makes the fade weak is how many of the faders admit they have made this call for years and been wrong, including one who drafted him last year amid the same cries and was rewarded. The counter-case is the most stacked boxes in the league last year while still leading in yards per carry, and roughly 100 yards and a touchdown a game.

**Draft take:** Take him at the round-2 turn in half PPR or standard where his goal-line monopoly pays; in full PPR let someone else own the age risk without the receptions.
