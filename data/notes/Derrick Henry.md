**Derrick Henry** (BAL, RB, bye 13) — board rank 13 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** a heavily faded player whose faders keep saying, in the same comment, that they have made this call for years and been wrong every time.

- Role, per beat report: Jeremy Fowler of ESPN (8/11) says Henry gets his normal heavy workload under a coordinator who comes from Chicago's third-ranked rushing attack, though it may look different at times — Fowler saw designed runs out on the perimeter — and Baltimore will also try to get him involved in the passing game at least somewhat.
- Everything else confirms he is untouched. No commenter in any thread suggests a backfield split; the Aug 29 and Aug 31 threads both use his security as the premise, with Adam Randall framed as developing behind him ("give Adam a year under Derrick") and a rookie back stashable on IR because Baltimore does not need him.
- The two real limits: Cooper caps his upside because he has not been a full-time back for a while and gives up the pass work — which is why this is a full-PPR fade and not a half-PPR one, and which Fowler's report only partly softens. And one commenter (8/25) flags the loss of center Tyler Linderbaum as undertalked-about, with a reply noting the likely successor is on season-ending IR.
- Age is the standing knock: "78 in RB years," 32 with heavy mileage, and several call last year's totals an illusion built late in the season after his owners were already sunk. The counter from inside the same threads is a healthy Lamar, the most stacked boxes in the league last year while still leading in yards per carry, and five straight years of people calling him done.
- Market: he goes in round 2, within 10-15 picks of Kyren Williams, and most of the room would rather have the Henry side of that. Maher gets him the expected non-PPR bump — 23rd in half PPR to 17th in standard, RB8. Murchison's half-PPR order for that second-round group is Henry, Achane, Hampton, Walker, Kyren, with Henry first. Twerski ranks him last among the 1/2-turn hero-RB options and names him an ADP fade. That is a genuine analyst split at equal weight, and scoring format is most of it.
- Sentiment: 12 top-level fades (167 upvotes) on age and mileage, and one drafter holding both Henry and McCaffrey says Henry is the one he is more worried about. On the other side he is named a legitimate dark horse for an RB1 finish off roughly 100 yards and a touchdown a game.

**Draft take:** take him at his round-2 price in half PPR or standard, where Murchison ranks him first in that tier; in full PPR the pass-work hole plus the age curve makes him the fade Twerski calls him.
