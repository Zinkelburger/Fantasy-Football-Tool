**Bhayshul Tuten** (JAC, RB, bye 7) — board rank 47 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the most genuinely split player on the board — one thread's second-most-upvoted comment, at 620 points, is "I intended to fade Tuten but ended up with him in all my leagues".

- No injury. The whole question is the Jacksonville committee. The bull case has him out-playing Chris Rodriguez and LeQuint Allen for roughly 70% of snaps including passing work, and a bellcow role by Week 4. Jaguars fans push back with a 40/40/20 split — Tuten and Rodriguez platooning drives, Allen on obvious passing downs — which holds while Allen is healthy.
- What is reported rather than projected: he took seemingly equal reps with Rodriguez through a full late-August practice, is still listed only as a co-starter, and commenters note that every time Tuten comes up, Coen brings up Rodriguez instead. No coach or beat writer has called him the bellcow.
- The one thing Coen has said on the record cuts against a workhorse role. Quoted from an SI piece, the Jaguars head coach says Tuten will keep getting kickoff returns and that returning helps his run game — "there's no dancing. You've got to go" — after "really, really big explosive kickoff returns for us last year". The room reads it as a tell: no team keeps its RB1 on kickoffs, so several expect him off returns if he wins the job outright and on them if the backfield stays a committee.
- Hartitz likens the committee to Bucky Irving's — Tuten leads on snaps while the other two take valuable touches — and Cooper expects Rodriguez on goal-line snaps, against a fan's counter that Tuten went 5 TDs on 7 carries inside the 5 last year. He was a rookie behind Travis Etienne, so there is no snap-share baseline to argue from.
- Market is the decision-relevant part: the board rank sits ahead of where the room takes him. Reported landing spots run from the mid-5th and 6th round to the 8th, about a full round after Jadarian Price. Analysts call that a discount — Ciely says people are going too low on him, Maher has him 53rd and RB20 ahead of Irving and Quinshon Judkins, Loza calls him a surprising fifth-round value. Against them, a ranking sheet built from Vegas season props has him about 40 spots below his ADP, the largest gap of any player by roughly 3x.
- Sentiment is close to even with no strong upvote signal. The fade case: he did little with his chances last year, was not a highly drafted prospect, and could go the Zamir White route. Asked to choose between Tuten and Irving, the room leans Irving.

**Draft take:** do not pay the round-4 board rank — the reps say co-starter, the head coach still has him returning kicks, and the room is getting him in the 5th to 7th, so let him fall and take him there.
