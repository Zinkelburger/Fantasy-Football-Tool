**Bhayshul Tuten** (JAC, RB, bye 7) — board rank 47 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Genuinely split with no consensus — the room fades him, then drafts him anyway, and the honest summary is that nobody knows what his snap share is.

- No injury claims on Tuten. The availability that matters is LeQuint Allen's: one commenter argues Tuten would not even be the third-down back if Allen were healthy, and the 09-02 Jaguars-fan split of 40/40/20 assumes Allen returns to take obvious passing downs. Chris Rodriguez was brought in to compete and, per multiple commenters, specifically for short-yardage and goal-line work.
- Role is the whole argument and it is unresolved. The best evidence either way is a beat report from 08-26: Tuten took seemingly equal reps with Rodriguez throughout a full Wednesday camp practice. Jaguars fans note that every time Tuten comes up Coen brings up Rodriguez instead, that the buzz has been co-starter talk rather than a lead role, and that he is still listed only as a co-starter.
- Head coach Liam Coen, quoted from an SI piece, says Tuten will keep getting kickoff returns and that the return work helps his running — "there's no dancing. You've got to go." The room reads that against him: no team keeps its RB1 on kickoffs, so several expect him phased out of returns if he wins the job and kept on them if the backfield stays a committee.
- The bull case: he out-plays Rodriguez and Allen for roughly 70% of snaps including passing work, Jacksonville has historically given its backs workhorse roles, and one commenter expects him a bell cow by Week 4. Supporters add he was the better receiving back and more efficient than Travis Etienne, and went 5 TDs on 7 carries inside the 5 last year. Cooper says he is too special to keep off the field but expects Rodriguez on the goal line; Hartitz compares the committee to Bucky Irving's; Twerski calls him his favorite dead-zone back.
- The bear case is equally specific: a near-50/50 committee, no snap-share baseline at all because he was a rookie behind Etienne, vision as the questioned trait, and — the point the room keeps making — no coach or beat writer has ever called him the bellcow.
- Market: the board's rank 47 matches the room, which takes him in the 5th of 12-team (around RB25), with reported picks stretching to the mid-5th, the 6th, the 7th and the 8th — so he routinely goes past his cost. Several posters with both available took Jadarian Price instead. Analysts run above the market: Maher has him 53rd and RB20 against ECR 59/RB23, ahead of Bucky Irving and Quinshon Judkins despite both going a round earlier; Twerski has him just ahead of Irving; Loza calls him a surprising fifth-round value; Jake Ciely names him a player people are going too low on. Directly against that, a commenter who built a ranking sheet from Vegas season props has him grading roughly 40 spots below ADP — the largest gap of any player by about 3x.
- Sentiment: the second-most-upvoted comment in one thread (620 points) is "I intended to fade Tuten but ended up with him in all my leagues."

**Draft take:** Fine in the 5th or later and a mistake in the 4th — the only hard evidence in the corpus is a camp practice where he split reps evenly with Chris Rodriguez, and nobody in the building has called him the lead back.
