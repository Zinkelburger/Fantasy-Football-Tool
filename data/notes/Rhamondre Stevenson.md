**Rhamondre Stevenson** (NE, RB, bye 11) — board rank 65 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room has flipped from TreVeyon Henderson to Stevenson, and camp reporting rather than opinion is what moved it.

- He is healthy and the Week 1 lead back, with Henderson not cleared as of Sept 1 (ankle); posters say they will start Stevenson regardless, including against Seattle. His own durability is the standing caveat — one full season in five years, and last season's Henderson RB1 stretch happened while Stevenson was hurt.
- Camp reporting is the bull case: he has taken the majority of the work since day one as a three-down back, with the second-most catches of any Patriot in camp, and was already working more with the ones before Henderson's injury. Andrew Callahan (Boston Herald) at the Patriots-Colts joint practice: Stevenson is "still very much the leader here" — he took the first two handoffs from Maye, led all backs with two catches, and caught a red-zone touchdown.
- The role is early downs, goal line and pass protection. He is called a top-3 pass-protecting back, and the room's read is that if Henderson cannot handle the pass-block scheme Stevenson does all the meaningful work. His fumbling reputation is contested with numbers: 3 fumbles all season, two in Weeks 3-5 and none afterward, fewer than James Cook's 6. Last season the split moved from near-even in the regular season to 70-30 in the playoffs once Stevenson returned, and he took roughly twice Henderson's playoff touches.
- The overall-RB1 argument and its rebuttal both belong on the page. The article the room reacted to has him on a full-season pace of about 1,600 yards, 50 catches and 15 TDs at 5.7 yards per carry over eight post-bye games against five of the NFL's seven best defenses. The statistical answer: the PPR RB1 has scored 362-416 points the last four years, his career best is RB7 at 249 points (69-1,461-6), and the late pace was inflated by one blowout against a collapsed Dolphins team. A separate ceiling worry is that New England adding A.J. Brown and Romeo Doubs cuts into his receiving volume and touchdown equity.
- Advanced splits favour him over Henderson — first vs third in explosive play rate, 33 missed tackles forced on 130 carries vs 30 on 180, and 1.45 vs 1.02 yards per route run — with the rebuttal that this is cherry-picked.
- Market: ADP around RB26, drafted in rounds 6, 7 and 9, generally within a few spots of Henderson though some report him going 2-3 rounds earlier now. Twerski is the dissent, grouping him with the dead-zone backs he would rather not draft.
- Sentiment is strongly in his favour — 47 upvotes on a bare "RHAMONDRE" — but the room flatly rejected the RB1 premise, with a 1,371-point top comment calling him "a charcoal black overcooked horse." He turns 29 this year, and the live dissent is that he reverts to "Captain Butterfingers."

**Draft take:** Draft him at his RB26 cost and start him Week 1 as the clear lead back — the realistic outcome is a strong RB2, not the overall RB1 the article sold.
