**Isaiah Likely** (NYG, TE, bye 8) — board rank 101 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** a rare late tight end with a real opportunity change, and the strongest-sourced claim on him is the bearish one.

- Role, per beat and preseason tracking — the best-sourced thing anyone has on him and it cuts against the hype: he played 6 of 16 first-team snaps in the preseason opener, all in 11 personnel, and came off for Theo Johnson and Chris Manhertz whenever New York went heavy. The room lands on roughly a 60% snap share as a move/F tight end.
- Be careful with the "Giants WR2" story. The report driving it is Graziano's bold-predictions column, and the top comment on that thread (827 upvotes) is that blogs ran a prediction as reported fact. What is actually reported is softer: he signed at the 4th-highest AAV among tight ends after following Harbaugh — three years, $40M, up to $47.5M — and was the No. 1 target in camp, a standing measured with Malik Nabers out. A separate commenter (9/4) says the camp word is that Likely, not Malachi Fields, is the real WR2 because of extra film-room time with Jaxson Dart; that is one commenter.
- Every upside case runs through Nabers' recovery. The league-winning-punt version has him the de facto second target on a team whose quarterback targeted tight ends heavily and whose primary slot receiver tore his ACL; the bigger version has him as Dart's top option for a stretch if Nabers needs time. The bull argument otherwise is just the money — teams don't pay a tight end $13M a year to block.
- Cooper's caveat is the one to hold: the opportunity the position rewards is finally there, but he is slow and will need heavy volume — the Zach Ertz path — and Cooper would draft a second tight end if Likely were his starter. Hartitz has him around TE10 with Dalton Kincaid and would start him over George Kittle in Week 1; Murchison likes him as a late TE1.
- The bear case is his own record: 42-477-6 is his career high in four seasons, he was TE47 at 3.4 ppg behind Mark Andrews last year, his targets per route (22.3, 15.5, 21.8, 16.4%) and YPRR (1.51, 1.62, 1.82, 1.40) have never sustained, and he graded 56.4 at PFF on limited 2025 snaps, below Theo Johnson.
- Market: roughly TE12, rounds 9-13 in 12-team PPR, with reported picks at 102 and 128 — board rank 101 is the front edge of that. One league took him in the fifth, which a commenter calls drafting him at his ceiling. In dynasty he went below his 3,887 superflex market value in 69% of 306 trades, the highest trade count on that buy-low list.

**Draft take:** the late tight end to take if you punt the position, but pay the rounds 9-13 price, not the fifth-round one — the only hard usage data anyone has is a 60% snap share in 11 personnel.
