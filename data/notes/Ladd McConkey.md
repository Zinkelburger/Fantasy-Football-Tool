**Ladd McConkey** (LAC, WR, bye 7) — board rank 50 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** a bet on the Chargers offense hitting, with a clean role opening in front of him and a real disagreement about whether the profile is worth round 3.

- Healthy. One commenter reports it was recently revealed he played all of last season with an undisclosed injury, believed to be a shoulder — cited as an example of NFL injury reports being unreliable, and worth exactly the confidence a single commenter's recollection earns.
- The role change is the substance: Keenan Allen, who took 777 yards on a team-leading 81 receptions, signed in Indianapolis, and the Chargers replaced him with tight ends rather than receivers. That returns McConkey to his natural full-time slot role — Allen is described by his backers as the thing that capped him last year, not Quentin Johnston. Two of the league's best tackles are back from injury, and the coordinator change moves a precision, on-schedule route runner out of a Greg Roman offense.
- The pushback is real: one camp expects Quentin Johnston to be the Chargers WR1 outright and outproduce him, and Sanderson names him the receiver he is least confident in across the WR13-24 range — "a lot of optimism about a high variance profile in an un-concentrated offense" — while also admitting he is worried about not owning enough of him, because that range is stacked with his targets.
- Market: he goes at the 3rd/4th round turn, roughly board rank 50. Maher has him 30th overall against an ADP of 40 and an ECR of 35-38, which makes any 4th-round pick a discount. Hartitz ranks him second in the round 3-4 WR tier behind Waddle and ahead of Flowers, Egbuka and Rice, prioritising him over Egbuka because of the toe, and wants him around $30 in auction. The room called "Ladd and QJ are safe" its second-biggest comment (600 points) when the Allen signing closed the door on an LA return, and expected his ADP to rise from ~49.
- Sentiment: 15 top-level fades, almost all cost-based ("not interested at cost," "not very fun to own") and one appearance on a "burned me, never again" list — against six top-level predictions of a top-five or overall WR1 finish and Loza calling him this year's Jaxon Smith-Njigba.

**Draft take:** take him at the 3/4 turn — the Keenan Allen vacancy plus a healthy line is a concrete reason to buy, and the fades against him are about last year's price, not this year's role.
