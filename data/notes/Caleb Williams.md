**Caleb Williams** (CHI, QB, bye 10) — board rank 85 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Analysts like the situation more than the player, and the number the fade keeps returning to is his completion rate.

- The case against is one statistic: 58.1% completion last season, second-worst among qualified starters, below Dillon Gabriel and Cam Ward and barely above J.J. McCarthy and Shedeur Sanders. The defence in the same thread cites 4,000 yards, 20 TDs to 7 INTs, a roughly 90 rating and upper-half EPA, arguing the low rate reflects deep shots.
- The bull case is the environment, and it turns on that same number: commenters argue a modest completion-rate improvement makes him a top-five fantasy QB given his receivers, and that Chicago's difficult schedule helps because he will have to throw. Hartitz expects a year-three leap in year two of Ben Johnson's offence, with targets centralised on three players and plenty of multi-TE sets — good for Loveland, Burden and Odunze alike.
- A cost the room mostly has not priced: Chicago lost the coordinator who oversaw his top-10 passing-yard and passing-TD season to Baltimore, where he is now the new play-caller. That is cited on the Ravens' side of the ledger, not usually on Chicago's.
- Analysts split narrowly. Maher has him QB7 and takes him over Trevor Lawrence and Jaxson Dart at the same price on rushing upside plus his play-caller, though he is below ECR on all three. Twerski takes Williams over Dart. Loza ranks him QB12 — nervous about consistency, citing the 4th-hardest QB schedule and erratic 2025 play, but still expecting QB1 numbers.
- Market: he goes considerably later than his ADP of 66-87 suggests — taken at pick 110 of a 12-team, targeted at 7.08 in a 10-team and in rounds 8-9, and once in the 11th of a 10-team league to the drafter's surprise. The room's read is that he is flying under the radar rather than being uniformly cheap; one posted 14-team board had him at 3.8.
- The fade worth weighting: a rival room says his average play is mediocre behind an insane highlight reel, that he sailed throws over open receivers, was flat-out bad through three quarters on average, and that his biggest throws came off secondary busts. Several commenters separately hold zero shares of any Chicago skill player because the whole offence is priced above a mediocre 2025.

**Draft take:** Take him in the 8th-to-9th where the room actually drafts him, not at the board's 85 — the top-five outcome needs a completion-rate jump nobody has evidence for yet.
