**Justin Jefferson** (MIN, WR, bye 6) — board rank 16 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room defends the player and fades the quarterback — in the fade threads, essentially every reason given is Minnesota's QB rather than anything about Jefferson.

- No injury. He played all 17 games in 2025, was never on the injury report, held a 30% target share and still finished WR37 (WR25 in half-PPR), which the room blames entirely on historically bad quarterback play and an offense that could not stay on the field.
- The live risk is behind Kyler Murray, not in front of him: if Murray misses time it is J.J. McCarthy or a Wentz-type again. One Vikings fan's split on last season — 95 receiving yards per game with Carson Wentz throwing against 54 with McCarthy — drew a 47-upvote follow-up that making Jefferson look pedestrian is the most damning thing about McCarthy. A commenter relaying an unnamed Vikings beat reporter (Aug 25) expects Wentz, signed for $3M, to be the QB2, and reads it as the staff giving up on McCarthy.
- Role is unchanged and elite: the target hog in Kevin O'Connell's offense, No. 1 all-time in receiving yards through six seasons, career averages of 105 catches, 1,534 yards and 8 TDs. The bar the room sets is low — Murray only has to be QB20 for Jefferson to hit.
- The ceiling cap is specific and repeated: Murray has never eclipsed 4,000 passing yards or 26 passing TDs and runs a lot near the goal line, works outside the numbers and on scrambles, and cannot use Jefferson over the middle. The read is a high floor with a capped touchdown ceiling that keeps him out of the top three, unlike Lamb with Dak or Brown with Maye.
- "QB-proof" gets picked apart: his best seasons came with Kirk Cousins and Sam Darnold, both of whom produced elsewhere, so 2025 is treated as the exception rather than proof he is quarterback-independent.
- Market: he goes about 15th and Yahoo has him WR6, behind Chase, Nacua, St. Brown, Smith-Njigba and Lamb — but he is slipping in this room. One poster got him in the third round of an 8-team league "because people refuse to believe Kyler Murray can be adequate"; several would rather go RB-RB and only take him if he lasts past the RB group; one calls a mid-first insane and would buy in the late 2nd. Asked directly at pick 13 the room still takes him in full PPR, with the dissent taking CeeDee Lamb over the Minnesota QB situation.
- The named analysts split. Dopp is buying him everywhere — alphas eat, and he stayed a top-10 WR through bad QB play before. Murchison names him one of the two early-round players he is most concerned will miss expectations, expects a bounce-back but not the top-three finish, and would shop him for RB help. Twerski is on the bounce-back side. The sharpest dissent is an owner who says Jefferson himself was off all last season, that camp video still looked mortal, and names him his No. 1 hate of the year at a WR30+ floor.

**Draft take:** Take him at the back of round 1 where the room's Kyler Murray discount has pushed him — the floor is a 100-catch target hog — but do not pay a top-five WR price for touchdown volume this quarterback has never produced.
