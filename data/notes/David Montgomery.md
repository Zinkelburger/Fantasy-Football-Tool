**David Montgomery** (HOU, RB, bye 8) — board rank 48 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** this sub's favourite value running back all offseason — "People are sleeping on David Montgomery per usual" was the second-biggest answer in one thread at 286 points — with the fade being the price, not the player.

- No injury. He did not play in the preseason exhibitions while Woody Marks did, and the room reads that as a veteran being kept fresh; he has missed only ten games in seven seasons.
- Role is the whole file and the sources disagree. Houston traded meaningful capital for him and gave him a new contract, and his coach has publicly called him a three-down back in a room with low competition for touches. The team's stated problem last season was having no back who could punch it in near the goal line. Against that, Jonathan Alexander of the Houston Chronicle expects him to split work pretty evenly with Marks on a hot-hand basis rather than operate as a workhorse — though commenters note that piece is a repackaging of an Aug. 23 report, not new information.
- The room largely rejects the even split: the majority expect him to lead, take most carries and nearly all goal-line and red-zone work, with several projecting a 60-70% snap share. Even the posters arguing for a real Marks role concede Montgomery takes the vast majority of red-zone carries. Fantasy Points says Texans insider sources — explicitly not beat reporters — describe a 2024 Joe Mixon usage plan, about 19.3 carries and 3.7 targets for 18.9 points a game. Maher stops short of a workhorse projection and expects Marks involved on passing downs; whether the split is 60/40 or 50/50 is where the ceiling lives.
- The bear case is the environment: commenters call Houston's offensive line the worst in the NFL (PFF 32nd), though others say the interior was rebuilt around first-round guard Rutledge and that run blocking was near dominant in joint practices. He is 29, on his third team, and Stroud has regressed. Murchison endorses the fade: a high floor on usage, a very low ceiling given the offense.
- The corpus does not resolve one thing: Lions fans say he pushed for a bigger role after his snap share was cut, while he has publicly denied asking out.
- Market has caught up to the board. Board rank 48, and he now goes in rounds 4-5, which drafters call the classic dead zone and several call a reach — a Texans follower calls the 4th a reach and the 5th good value. Maher has him RB22 with double-digit touchdown upside, off 4.5 yards a carry and 8 rushing TDs on a career-low 158 attempts last year. Loza has him as a middle-round must-have who keeps falling to her; Twerski names him an ADP fade; Hartitz goes back and forth "by the hour" in the round 5 range.

**Draft take:** take him in the 5th, not the 4th — the goal-line role is real and reported, but you are buying touchdowns from a bottom-tier offense, and the price no longer includes a discount.
