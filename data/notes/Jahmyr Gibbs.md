**Jahmyr Gibbs** (DET, RB, bye 6) — board rank 1 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the consensus 1.01, and the only argument against him is that he has never carried the workload he is now being priced for.

- Healthy, no reported issue. The standing worry is the load itself: commenters expect the enlarged role to get him banged up at some point, and one would take Bijan Robinson over him purely on hamstring history. Whether his frame holds up as a first-time bellcow is the entire bust case — not performance.
- Detroit's backfield is now his alone. David Montgomery, who took roughly a 36% backfield share, is gone, and Isiah Pacheco went on IR (four-game minimum) on 9/1. Behind him are UDFA-level bodies, Jacob Saylors and Sione Vaki, who commenters say cannot pick up a blitz or take short-yardage work — so he loses rest snaps as well as gaining goal-line touches. Dopp confirmed on 9/3 that Detroit has added nothing behind him. The room expects his snap share to climb from roughly 65% toward 75%-plus, some saying 90% of the workload; the top comment in the IR thread (790 points) was "congrats on 30 touches a game." The one caveat anyone raises is a commenter calling him a weak pass protector, unsupported by any reps in the corpus.
- He signed a three-year, $67.5M extension on 8/6 — up to $75.75M with $51.5M guaranteed, about $250k a year more than Bijan's deal two days earlier — making him the NFL's highest-paid back, confirmed by his representation. The room reads the money as Detroit committing to a workhorse and as why the Lions let Montgomery walk. Holdout risk is gone.
- Usage context worth carrying: 320 touches in 2025, comfortably under the 370-touch danger line the room applies to McCaffrey, Taylor and Bijan — but he averaged only 14.3 carries a game and was **not** among the six backs who cleared a 70% snap share. The per-game rates are what people are buying: 24.4 FPG in the 10 games he did clear 70% snaps, 27.4 FPG in the six Montgomery missed. He led this group with 51 red-zone carries to Montgomery's 33 and should absorb most, explicitly not all, of Montgomery's ~10 red-zone scores.
- Two structural knocks that hold up: Detroit's line has weak links at guard and center — one commenter in August reported the Lions without their first two centers for at least a few weeks — and he is the boomier of the top two backs, 40 points one week and 10 the next, having started 2025 hot and faded while Bijan improved.
- Market: the room pays what board rank 1 says. He is the default 1.01 in 12-team redraft, though the vote against Bijan is closer to 51/49 and multiple commenters call Bijan there equally defensible; one report has him falling to 1.06. In superflex he goes over Josh Allen for a large share of the room, and in a 12-team SF auction he was the most expensive player at 35% of budget (Bijan 33%, Allen 32%); redraft auctions price him near $75 of $200. Twerski has him alone at No. 1 "comfortably" over Bijan, Hartitz would take him over Bijan even to diversify across leagues, and Sanderson takes him 1.01 in superflex over any quarterback. The useful dissent, 134 upvotes across 8 comments, is superflex players calling Allen/Gibbs/Bijan a tossup.
- Sentiment is euphoric — comps to prime LaDainian Tomlinson and Marshall Faulk — and should be discounted as such; roughly nine managers holding the 1.01 faded him anyway on a hunch about the frame.

**Draft take:** take him 1.01 in redraft and in superflex; the price is fair, the situation is the cleanest at the position, and the only real risk is one nobody can price.
