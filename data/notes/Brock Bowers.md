**Brock Bowers** (LV, TE, bye 13) — board rank 25 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the room's most divisive tight end — named 39 times in one thread, with the loudest dissent being "he had 2 great games last year, the rest was streamer TE level".

- Health: last season was cut into by a knee sprain, and the room's own split of the tape is the useful number — in games where he was not wearing a knee brace he averaged 7.2 targets, 62.0 receiving yards and 16.4 points per game, all TE2 marks, and he cleared a 24% team yardage share in both NFL seasons. He still finished TE11 overall. The only current-year concern anyone raised was an unverified clip circulating on X in late August; no beat or team reporting supports it, and others say he last looked fine.
- Role is as close to uncontested as the position gets: next to no target competition, a lock to lead Las Vegas in receiving, and treated by Raiders fans as the team's actual WR1 — "just a big WR" rather than a traditional tight end. He finished third among tight ends at 16.6 points per game from Weeks 9-17 in a season he spent partly injured, and one commenter projects roughly 160 targets.
- The bull case beyond usage is the staff: the new Raiders coach called him a football-playing robot from heaven, and having made Jaxon Smith-Njigba the league's receiving leader last year is expected to feature him the same way. The room also expects Las Vegas to trail often behind a middling-to-bad defense, which points at volume.
- The knock is the quarterback room. Kirk Cousins and Fernando Mendoza compare unfavourably with the passing behind past elite tight end seasons; the defence of it is that Cousins feeds tight ends — they credit him for Kyle Pitts's 2025 — against dissenters citing Cousins's 2.8-yard average air yards and calling that Pitts season one outlier game. Commenters also split on an Ashton Jeanty absence: one side says all volume funnels to Bowers, the other that defences then scheme solely against him and Las Vegas never reaches the red zone.
- The room is drafting him later than the board. Board rank 25, but actual 12-team results cluster in the 2nd through 4th: 2.14, the 2/3 turn, 3.04 in a 14-team, the 3rd twice, and 4.07 in a 10-teamer where the room kept passing. Anything before the late 2nd was called a reach. Loza has often passed on him and McBride for a cheaper tight end around round 4. Cooper's structural objection is the sharpest: no tight end has ever finished as the top overall flex and the best PPR TE season ever is 330 points, so a second-round tight end needs a top-ten all-time season to pay off.

**Draft take:** do not spend the board's late-first/early-second on him — the room has been letting him get to the 3rd, and that is the only price at which the position math works.
