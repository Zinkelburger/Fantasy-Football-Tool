**Brock Bowers** (LV, TE, bye 13) — board rank 25 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Nobody argues about the player; the entire argument is whether any tight end is worth a second-round pick.

- Health: no current injury reported. Commenters split his last season by the knee brace he wore — in the games without it he averaged 7.2 targets, 62.0 yards and 16.4 FPG, all TE2 marks. An 08-20 thread circulated an X video of him looking iffy at practice, but there was no beat or team confirmation of any knee issue and others said he last looked fine.
- Role: functionally the Raiders' WR1. Next to no target competition, a lock to lead Las Vegas in receiving, and he has cleared a 24% team yardage share in both NFL seasons. The room expects Las Vegas to trail often behind a middling-to-bad defense, which points at volume.
- The one live unknown is the quarterback, and the corpus does not agree on who it is — Kirk Cousins is the common assumption (commenters credit him for Kyle Pitts's 2025 and call the room a better spot for a TE than Brissett), while other threads name Geno Smith or the rookie Mendoza. Dissenters point at Cousins's 2.8-yard average air yards and call the Pitts season one outlier game.
- Market: a second-round pick everywhere, and going earlier than that in practice — drafters report taking him in the second, one at the 2/3 turn around picks 20-21, one keeping him over CeeDee Lamb. Murchison likes him ahead of Trey McBride but only drafts either at the 2/3 turn from a Gibbs or Bijan start. Cooper's objection is structural: no TE has ever finished as the top overall flex and the best PPR TE season is 330 points, so a second-round TE needs a top-10 all-time year to pay. Loza has often passed on him and McBride for a cheaper TE in round 4. The format matters — the room says late second in 12-team flex, first round in no-flex, and higher still in 10-team or smaller.

**Draft take:** Take him at the 2/3 turn and not before — the target monopoly is the safest thing at the position, but the price already assumes a career year and the positional math says you rarely get paid for it.
