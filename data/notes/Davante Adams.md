**Davante Adams** (LAR, WR, bye 11) — board rank 56 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room agrees on the role — the best goal-line target in football — and splits on whether a season where he scored on a quarter of his catches can repeat.

- No current injury; the risk named is age and soft tissue. He turns 34 this season, hurt his hamstring at the end of last year, and one commenter counts hamstring issues in two of the last three seasons. Another notes the Rams managed his snap count late in the year because he needs the rest at this stage.
- He occupies Sean McVay's X role — the job Odell Beckham held before tearing his ACL in the Super Bowl and the one Allen Robinson failed in — and the room stresses that the role's defining requirement is red-zone production. That is exactly what he supplies: 14 receiving touchdowns last season on top of career years of 18, 14 and 13, top-three in targets inside the 10 nearly every year since 2020, and 25 such targets in 2025. Los Angeles repeated the same goal-line look all season — motion Adams out to a one-on-one, Kyren Williams to the flat — and he also funnels the tight-end red-zone targets that would otherwise go to Terrance Ferguson.
- One correction worth carrying: the most-upvoted scouting claim about him (432 points) said he had top-3 separation among pass catchers last year. Next Gen Stats, posted downthread, says 2.17 yards of separation per target in 2025 — 81st of 85 qualified pass catchers and last among receivers with 100-plus targets. He is a big-bodied slant and red-zone winner, not a separator.
- The bear case is arithmetic and age: he is 33, the WR2 behind Puka Nacua, and his 25% touchdown rate on catches will not repeat. Matthew Berry has called him fool's gold this year, and one commenter projects roughly 800 yards and 8 TDs in about 14 games.
- His ceiling case is Nacua missing time or being suspended, which the room treats as a direct target windfall — with the dissent that Adams scores mostly in the red zone, so Nacua's absence would help him less than people assume.
- Market: he costs about 55th and is WR24 on FantasyPros, which the room calls too cheap for a receiver who finished WR7-WR9 at 33. Reported buys: 5.07, a 7th-round steal in 12-team, pick 65 in another, $18 in a couple of auctions. Several say he should be drafted up near Tee Higgins, who goes about two rounds earlier.

**Draft take:** Draft him at his WR24 price as a touchdown-dependent WR2 — the goal-line role is real and repeatable, the separation numbers say nothing else is, and the upside case is a bet on Puka Nacua missing time.
