**Oronde Gadsden** (LAC, TE, bye 7) — board rank 125 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** bearish and fairly settled — the thread arguing for him scored 0, and its top reply at 110 points simply says the doubters are right about the preseason snaps.

- Role is the problem. The Chargers' official depth chart lists Charlie Kolar as the starting tight end, and preseason usage matched: Gadsden played into the second quarter with the 2s and 3s and took special-teams snaps while Kolar and David Njoku worked with Herbert and the starters. A self-identified Chargers fan says Gadsden has acknowledged he is TE3, and the Sept 1 room describes him as a third-stringer "in the doghouse with a new OC." Njoku's arrival has managers openly discussing dropping him to a taxi squad.
- Blocking is the stated reason. Several commenters say his receiving talent is legitimate and his blocking is what buries him in snap counts. New head coach Mike McDaniel is a run-game coach the room does not expect to play a non-blocking tight end, and Gesicki's disappearance under McDaniel in Miami is the cited precedent; expected role is three-TE sets and obvious passing downs, with his points tied to routes run.
- The one coherent defence: he is the Chargers' F/move tight end by design — the new OC signed a starting Y and a blocking veteran because the room had neither, not to replace him, and the starters' reps for others reflect defined roles plus his own need for run-blocking work.
- The production argument is disputed inside its own thread. His 2025 line was 49 receptions, 664 yards, 3 TD in 15 games with seven starts, including four straight weeks of 11.8+ PPR points; one read is that the spike came after Joe Alt's injury and Quentin Johnston's absence and faded when both returned, another blames the offensive line collapsing. He had 17 catches and one touchdown over the final eight games.
- Market is where the board and the room part company hard. He is going undrafted or in the last round of early-September redrafts, with the bearish majority calling him unstartable and buyers treating him as a late dart or a best-ball touchdown play. Dynasty is the collapse story: he peaked around TE4 on KTC with owners refusing firsts, and is now had for "nickels and dimes" — recent sales include Jaylin Noel plus Terrance Ferguson plus a 2nd and two 3rds, and one manager moved him for $15 FAAB. One buy-low board has him as its No. 2 discount target, going below his market value in 73% of 230 superflex trades.

**Draft take:** ignore board rank 125 in redraft — he is the third tight end on his own team in a run-first scheme, so he is a last-round dart at best, and the only real buy is dynasty at a price that has already collapsed.
