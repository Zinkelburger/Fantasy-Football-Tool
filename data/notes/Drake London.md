**Drake London** (ATL, WR, bye 11) — board rank 20 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the talent is not in dispute anywhere in the corpus; the fade is entirely about who throws him the ball and whether he stays on the field.

- **Availability is the first risk.** A PCL injury ended his 2025 over weeks 12-18, which commenters use to discount both his and Kyle Pitts' late-season splits. One owner says that injury turned a first-place team into a missed playoff berth; the room still calls the risk/reward worth it. Nothing reports a current injury.
- **His value is a straight function of the quarterback.** The split posted on 2026-09-01 and widely upvoted: with Michael Penix starting (14 games) London went 91/1286/9, about 91.9 yards a game; without him (15 games) 77/904/7, about 60.3. The top comment in an earlier thread (563 points) expects roughly a 40% target share with Penix throwing, citing 18.5 PPG with Penix last year and 24.5 full-PPR PPG in weeks 4-11 plus heavy negative game script from a weak Falcons defense. Murchison puts it bluntly: unless Penix is declared QB1, London and Pitts will have peaks and valleys, though he still took London when the RB run passed him. Dopp trusts him as a WR1 regardless, because the whole passing game runs through him.
- The counter-case does not require Penix. Across his six healthy 2025 games London was top-3 in targets per game (10.5), receiving yards per game (108.5), YPRR (3.60) and FPG (24.5), and he has the league's highest share of team red-zone targets over two seasons (38.1%). He did that on a 74.1% catchable target rate that ranked WR56, and Tua Tagovailoa — expected to start Week 1 — has the league's third-highest catchable throw rate over three years, so one camp reads the QB change as neutral-to-positive.
- Market: Hartitz has him WR9, a low-end WR1 in half PPR, despite the QB situation. Twerski prefers the running backs in his range and would keep Zay Flowers at $16 over London at $30. The room's own ordering places him behind Olave, Collins, Nabers and Rice; he was reported going 26th overall in a 14-team draft, roughly his board rank, so there is no pricing gap to exploit.
- Sentiment: 20 top-level fades (195 upvotes), nearly all aimed at the Atlanta QB room and the new coaching staff rather than at London himself.

**Draft take:** draft him at cost as a high-variance WR1 whose weekly ceiling arrives only when Penix does — and do not pay up if you need a stable WR1.
