**Drake London** (ATL, WR, bye 11) — board rank 20 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** an uncontested alpha whose price is being set entirely by which Atlanta quarterback throws to him.

- Healthy now. He was hurt over last season's weeks 12-18 — one commenter names a PCL injury — which the room uses to discount both his and Kyle Pitts' late-season splits, and which one owner blames for turning a first-place team into a missed playoff berth.
- Role is not in dispute: he is the uncontested target hog in Atlanta, with 94 targets in the 8 games he and Michael Penix played together last year (about 12 a game, Ja'Marr Chase's league-leading rate), and the league's highest share of team red-zone targets over two seasons at 38.1%. Across his six healthy 2025 games he was top-3 in targets per game (10.5), receiving yards per game (108.5), YPRR (3.60) and FPG (24.5). Season line: 16.8 PPR PPG, a WR5-to-WR7 per-game rate.
- The quarterback split is the single most-cited number in the corpus and it is stark: with Penix starting (14 games) he went 91/1,286/9, about 91.9 yards a game; without him (15 games) 77/904/7, about 60.3 a game. Tua Tagovailoa is expected to start Week 1, and the 9/3 room reads London as the Atlanta player most hurt by it — a QB whose preseason completions travelled about 3 air yards means he needs short routes to get catchable balls.
- The counter-case, made with numbers rather than vibes: London's own catchable-target rate was only 74.1% (WR56) while he posted 4.24 YPRR on catchable targets (WR3), and Tua has the third-highest catchable-throw rate in the league over three years at 79.0%. On that read the QB change is neutral-to-positive for catchable volume even if it costs air yards. Dopp still trusts him as a WR1 because the whole passing game runs through him; Murchison warns that unless Penix is declared QB1 you get peaks and valleys, and took him anyway when the RB run passed him.
- The other conditional worth carrying: Atlanta's defense is bad enough to force negative game scripts and throwing volume, and with only Pitts alongside him there is no proven WR2 to pull coverage off — which cuts both ways in the room's argument.
- Market: he is falling on the QB news and that is the actionable fact. Board rank 20 has him in the second round; reported picks in 12-teams run 2.11, 2.12, 3.02, 3.05, 3.06 and "middle of the 3rd," with one league letting him fall into the 20th-pick range, and a 93-point comment reporting the slide to the 3rd. The thread's top comment at 1,901 points is blunt about why: "London and Nico ARE being dragged down by bad QB play, otherwise they'd be going higher." Hartitz still has him WR9, a low-end WR1 in half PPR; Twerski prefers the running backs in his range.
- Sentiment: 20 top-level fade votes (195 upvotes) aimed at the Atlanta quarterback room and new coaching staff rather than at the player, and in one earlier thread the loudest verdict was outright rejection — "Why would Drake London even be in this discussion?" at 190 upvotes.

**Draft take:** let the QB fear push him to the third round and take him there — you are buying a 38% red-zone target share at a discount created by someone else's quarterback.
