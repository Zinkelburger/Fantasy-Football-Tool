**Sam LaPorta** (DET, TE, bye 6) — board rank 86 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room likes the talent and does not trust the back, and the target competition in Detroit is a real second problem.

- Back first: he had a microdiscectomy in November 2025 for a herniated disc and will be ten months post-op at Week 1. The physical therapist working the corpus says NFL athletes take four to six months to return to contact, that LaPorta is already practising and has ramped to contact well, and is not worried about his more recent hip contusion.
- The hip is the live question. HC Dan Campbell said on 20 August that LaPorta suffered a hip injury and could be ready by the start of the season but that he does not know "for sure" — a commenter who heard the full comments reads it as Campbell simply declining to speculate on Week 1. A commenter who had the same injury and surgery reports lingering hip tightness three months post-op and a surgeon's estimate of up to a year to resolve, and is avoiding him in drafts on that basis. That is one person's medical history, not a report.
- Role: Tim Twentyman (DetroitLions.com) had him terrific through the start of camp, showing how much Detroit missed him — a big gain down the left seam from Goff and a touchdown over the middle on the next play. New OC Drew Petzing, whose Arizona offense funneled targets to Trey McBride, is the bull case one commenter is drafting on; the counterweight in the same exchange is that nobody is actually calling LaPorta Detroit's number-one pass catcher, and per podcaster John Daigle, Petzing did not install his own playbook, so the offense should look much like the second half of last year. Hartitz's version is that a healthy LaPorta evens out the target distribution with Jameson Williams rather than leading it.
- His share sits behind Amon-Ra St. Brown, Jameson Williams and Gibbs out of the backfield, which pushes him below Kraft in the room's tiers despite the talent.
- Market: a round 6/7 target, roughly where his board rank sits, on the argument that his upside is comparable to Colston Loveland's at much less cost. In auctions he runs $6-10, above Kraft, which one commenter calls backwards — a back and spinal injury is more likely to recur than Kraft's isolated ACL. The room splits between taking the discount and refusing him at any price on volatility.

**Draft take:** A defensible round-6 shot on a proven top-6 tight end, but bench-and-see through Week 1 and do not pay up for the Petzing story until the target share shows it.
