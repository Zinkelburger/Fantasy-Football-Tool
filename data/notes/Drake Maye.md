**Drake Maye** (NE, QB, bye 11) — board rank 54 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room is divided on whether 2025 was real or schedule-made, and the loudest specific objection is the price rather than the player.

- The volume case is straightforward: New England ranked 24th in pass rate yet Maye was 12th in attempts and 5th in completions, with meaningful rushing usage on top — he averaged 44.5 rushing yards per game in his playoff splits. He finished QB2 last season, a small margin behind Stafford, with Josh Allen at QB5. New England also improved the line and added A.J. Brown and Romeo Doubs; one commenter expects Maye and Brown to be a 25-30 point spike-week duo.
- The regression case is argued just as hard and is the more falsifiable one: New England played one of the easiest schedules in decades and now faces a top-2-difficulty schedule with a year of film on him. They went 2-2 without looking good in their four competitive games, and several point to the playoffs as the exposure. The rival-fan version is blunter — "Drake The Schedule Maye."
- What his defenders answer with: his year-two passing yards beat Jalen Hurts' career best by over 500 and his TD total by 6, with only two games under 200 passing yards to Hurts' eight; he was MVP runner-up producing with poor receivers; and he played the Super Bowl through a shoulder injury from the Denver game with his left tackle on a bad knee.
- Market is the sharpest disagreement on the page. Maher ranks him 75-79 against an ECR of 44 — a full-round-plus fade. Vegas left him outside the Week 1 top-12 QBs against Seattle, which surprised the room; the case for him there is Seattle's diminished secondary (Bryant and Woolen gone, Bud Clark out for the year), and the case against is that New England's line looked sub-par over the summer.
- Where he is actually being drafted: in superflex he is a top-five asset — 27% of a 12-team auction budget, level with Lamar, and reported as the second QB off the board at 2.05, at pick 8, and once as high as 1.02. In redraft he is being kept in the 9th through 12th rounds and at $2 in a $200 auction across five leagues, with the room expecting his future price to land in the teens. One commenter's advice is to sell now, "not because he is bad" but because "his value is absolutely insane."
- The recurring fade is at cost: commenters like him — one predicts a top-3 finish — but will not pay the 8th round given how much late-round quarterback value exists.

**Draft take:** Keep him at a 9th-to-12th-round price and let him go at the 5th — the schedule swing is real, and Maher has him nearly three rounds below where the board does.
