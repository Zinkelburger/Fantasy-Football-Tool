**Chris Rodriguez** (JAC, RB, bye 7) — board rank 146 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The unglamorous half of the Jacksonville backfield; the split is over whether that half is a real role or just the goal line.

- His near-term upside is borrowed from LeQuint Allen's absence. Per John Shipley (8/26) he is seen as the Jaguars' best pass-protecting back with Allen hurt, took the RB nod for the end-of-game drill — mostly pass protection for Trevor Lawrence, plus a screen earlier in the drive — and Shipley expects an early-season stock boost if Allen cannot handle his normal third-down role. That third-down work is expected to revert when Allen returns, and commenters treat the Rodriguez signing as what caps Allen's carries in the meantime.
- The settled role read is short-yardage and goal-line back behind Bhayshul Tuten. Cooper and Hartitz both frame it the same way: the valuable touches — targets and goal-line carries — come out of Tuten's larger snap share.
- The other lever is his history with Liam Coen at Kentucky, cited alongside praise this summer for his power running as the reason he could take more than expected if Tuten is not what Jacksonville expects.
- The two named analysts split cleanly. Dopp says he is highly thought of by the coaching staff, should have a legit role right away even as the RB2, and is his stash over Woody Marks and Tank Bigsby. Maher calls him a JAG with a clearer path to season-long playing time but a lower ceiling, and does not see a scenario where he is a clear RB1 above a 50% snap share.
- Against him: a career snap share around 30-35% and touchdown dependence, and last year's 4.5 yards a carry on 100 carries in Washington works out to about 38 yards a game in a talent-poor committee he never broke through. He does not catch passes, and the room rates him a worse receiver and worse back than Rhamondre Stevenson.
- Market is thin: he goes around round 12, roughly where the board has him, which several commenters call far too cheap for a co-starter who would be the full-time back if Tuten got hurt. A couple of commenters are buying in dynasty on film.

**Draft take:** A round-12 stash worth making if you own Tuten, since the goal-line and short-yardage work is his either way — but do not draft him expecting a standalone role while LeQuint Allen is coming back.
