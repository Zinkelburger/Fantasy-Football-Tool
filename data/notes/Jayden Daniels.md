**Jayden Daniels** (WAS, QB, bye 7) — board rank 64 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the fade is loud and it is aimed at Washington's offensive line, not at him — and the same room keeps drafting him anyway.

- No current injury. The standing durability worry is structural: one commenter argues his reckless scrambling is the trait that both makes him effective and keeps landing him on IR, and that coaching it out would remove the value. Others say the room has forgotten what a healthy version looks like.
- The offensive line is the whole bear case. A bad preseason showing plus the Laremy Tunsil injury drove it; a commenter pushed back that the showing was unrepresentative — one starter out sick, one injured but expected back early, a rookie unlikely to start — while conceding the line probably will not be good without Tunsil. Murchison is less than thrilled with him for the same reason, and is hoping negative game scripts let Daniels compile stats late.
- The word on the line from one commenter, relaying a Ringer host: Washington's offense has "disaster potential," and a separate commenter says the OL is "inoperable."
- Sentiment is unusually negative for a top-five QB: several independent commenters call him too high on line, schedule and weapons, one calls QB3 in superflex crazy, one says he is a player they will not draft on ick alone despite expecting a top-five QB season, and two managers say they are weighing benching him in Week 1 for Kyler Murray or Tyler Shough. The one substantive rebuttal is that his injuries were not to his throwing arm and the receiver room improved.
- Market: he is falling and getting taken anyway. Commenters report him going in the 7th, 8th, 9th and 10th rounds and as the 7th QB off the board at pick 89 — meaningfully later than board rank 64. He is ranked QB5, which is also exactly his ADP, so that ranking is the market rather than a call. The room never resolves whether last year was a bounce-back setup or a rookie-year fluke.

**Draft take:** stop reaching for him at his board rank — let the fade carry him into the 8th or later, where a top-five QB behind a bad line is a fair bet.
