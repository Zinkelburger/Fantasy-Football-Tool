**Lamar Jackson** (BAL, QB, bye 13) — board rank 33 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** a bounce-back bet on a new Baltimore offense, sold by everyone in the building and bought guardedly by everyone outside it.

- Healthy. A fan setting season expectations describes him as healthy and MVP-caliber alongside a healthy Derrick Henry, healthy pass-catchers and a healthy first-string defense; no practice or injury report is cited anywhere in the corpus.
- The scheme change is the story and it is well sourced. New OC Declan Doyle, hired off the Bears staff, said publicly he has "really enjoyed working with" Jackson; Jackson himself said on the record that defenses aren't prepared for what the new offense has in store; and Ravens DC Anthony Weaver, asked on the team podcast about Doyle versus Monken and Roman, said Jackson is special because he fits within any system and that he expects him to thrive. All three are team sources on a forecast — discount accordingly.
- What the scheme actually does, per Jeremy Fowler of ESPN (8/11): Doyle's offense wants chunk plays, and Jackson worked from under center often, throwing downfield aggressively when the timing was right and often from play-action. Commenters describe him as the best QB in the league from under center and read the fit as an advantage, and Ravens fans note his best seasons have historically come in a first year with a new coordinator.
- The mechanical worry: commenters say his rushing and passing feed each other, and that when the threat of his elusiveness was reduced last year the Baltimore line got overwhelmed. His production is tied to being able to scramble, not just to volume.
- The fade, stated plainly: he was the 12th QB in fantasy points per game last season excluding the game he was hurt in, he is approaching 30, and his value has always come from rushing — so this may be the start of a slow decline. Ten top-level fades make that case. Ravens fans reject the "just a runner" framing and cite a better career passer rating than Mahomes.
- Market: he is the clear superflex QB2 — going 1.02, 2.01 and nearly falling to 2.02 in separate 12-team SF drafts, priced at 27% of budget tied with Drake Maye and behind only Gibbs, Bijan and Allen. In single-QB the room pays much less than board rank 33: he repeatedly falls to the 4th round in ESPN mocks at an ADP near 43.6, with commenters planning to take him at 4.03 or later. Maher, whose thesis is that quarterbacks are the most over-drafted asset in 2026, has him in the 75-79 range against an ECR of 36. Loza takes him over Joe Burrow in round 5.

**Draft take:** do not pay his board rank in single-QB — wait for the round-4/5 fall the room keeps reporting, where the new-coordinator bounce-back is free.
