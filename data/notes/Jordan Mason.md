**Jordan Mason** (MIN, RB, bye 6) — board rank 104 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** broadly positive and hedged — the "too low" nominations outnumber the fades, but nobody claims the job is outright his.

- Role, as the room reads it out of camp: Mason is Minnesota's early-down and goal-line back with Aaron Jones on passing downs, and several say he has locked up the early-down work. That holds only while Jones is healthy and keeps the third-down role; one dissenter expects the veteran to keep the majority of snaps once games count, and the split may end up nearer 50/50. Mason does not catch passes, though one camp report has him much improved there.
- Scheme is the other half of the case: a commenter cites Kevin O'Connell saying the Vikings revamped the run scheme this offseason to fit their personnel, and the team hired former Dolphins OC Frank Smith as run-game coordinator. The room reads the new wide-zone look as a match for what Mason ran in San Francisco.
- Market: going in the 9th-10th round and ranked near RB40, and the top-voted reply on that price (95 points) calls it a fair gamble, because what you are buying is Jones losing touches plus the goal-line work, not efficiency. Several report taking him in every league and one has him well above his rank. Twerski puts him at the top of his must-grab handcuff list, ahead of Tank Bigsby, Mike Washington and Keaton Mitchell.
- Sentiment: backers cite 4.8 YPC last year, a 5.1 career mark, a 230-pound frame and a top-five Vikings run-block win rate, and several expect him to be Minnesota's clear RB1 by season's end. Skeptics call him a JAG and a failed bet from last season with durability questions. The room has twice told managers not to drop him.

**Draft take:** buy the early-down and goal-line share at a 9th-10th round price — the value is the volume and Aaron Jones's health, not the efficiency.
