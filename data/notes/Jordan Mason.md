**Jordan Mason** (MIN, RB, bye 6) — board rank 104 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room's favourite mid-round back, backed on volume and scheme fit rather than on talent, with a real bear case about the offense he plays in.

- Early-down and goal-line back in Minnesota with Aaron Jones on passing downs. Several commenters say he locked up the early-down work out of camp; one dissenter expects the veteran to keep the majority of snaps once games start. Everything here assumes Jones stays healthy and holds the third-down job.
- The scheme argument is the strongest thing he has. A commenter cites Kevin O'Connell saying the Vikings revamped the run scheme this offseason to fit the personnel, and the team hired former Dolphins OC Frank Smith as run-game coordinator; the room reads the move toward wide zone as a match for what Mason ran in San Francisco.
- His ceiling is capped by third downs. One commenter calls him an awful pass blocker and pass catcher with 28 career catches, so he leaves the field on passing downs — with rookie Demond Claiborne as the pass-protection option if Jones is hurt rather than Mason.
- The numbers cut both ways: 4.77 yards a carry last year behind a bad offense and a hurt offensive line that is currently healthy, and commenters argue 200-plus carries makes him a top-25 back. Against that, O'Connell's run game has ranked 29th in rushing touchdowns over the past three seasons.
- Market: he is going in the 9th-10th round around RB36/37, with reports of the 6th in a 14-team half PPR, backend picks elsewhere, and $6 of $200 in a 10-team auction. The board rank sits in the same neighbourhood; the spread across leagues is wider than the gap. The thread's top-voted reply (95 points) frames the price honestly — you are buying Jones losing touches plus goal-line work, not efficiency. He also tops Twerski's must-grab handcuff list, ahead of Tank Bigsby and Mike Washington.
- Sentiment is broad but hedged: a 283-point comment chain of "I'm targeting Mason as my RB4", seven "too low" nominations in another thread, and repeated advice not to cut him for MarShawn Lloyd. Skeptics call him a JAG who was already a failed bet last season, flag durability, and say his prototype maxes out as a flex.

**Draft take:** Take him at the RB36-40 price as the early-down and goal-line half of a split you expect to grow, and stop expecting receiving work that is not coming.
