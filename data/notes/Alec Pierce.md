**Alec Pierce** (IND, WR, bye 13) — board rank 94 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** A five-month ankle rehab that only just ended, attached to a paid-up WR1 role the room does not believe converts to targets.

- Availability is the story and it has moved. He missed all of training camp; per Jake Arthur he was not practising as of Aug 18 and was still on PUP and reported walking with a noticeable limp on Aug 26, with the calf on the injured ankle visibly smaller. Schefter reported him activated off the PUP list on Aug 27 — which the room correctly read as permission to practise, not a clearance; had the Colts missed that deadline he would have gone to reserve/PUP and been guaranteed to miss four games. He returned to practice Tuesday Sept 1 (ankle/heel) per Jake Arthur, and as of Sept 3 is back at full practice. Week 1 is not confirmed; the room's condition is a ramp-up without setbacks, and several still expect him to miss or be limited early.
- Context from a commenter who says he performs the procedure: ankle cleanups return in 6-12 weeks for soft tissue and about six months with bony work, and Pierce is roughly 5.5 months out — he expects him to be fine. Pierce has missed only three games in four years. Fantasy Points injury analyst Jeff Mueller was the Aug 11 dissent, with concerns about the injury type and timeline.
- Role: with Michael Pittman gone he is the depth-chart WR1 and the favourite to lead the Colts in receiving yards and touchdowns, on a 4yr/$114M ($84M guaranteed) extension. Backers say the new money means a wider route tree rather than a pure field-stretcher role, and note real improvement on intermediate routes last year.
- The objection is that the role never was target volume: his aDOT has been 20-plus two straight years, he has never been a target earner at any level, and both Josh Downs and Tyler Warren project ahead of him in targets. The Keenan Allen signing splits the room — one side says Allen absorbs the outside work Pierce was paid for, the more upvoted reply says Allen is a slot player now and his underneath work opens the deep routes.
- Market: the ADP fall is the top take in the biggest thread (217 pts) — he is going in ESPN rounds 8-9 and one drafter got him at pick 88, roughly where the board has him. The room calls that a value on the grounds that he only needs to be solidly productive, not a league-winner.
- Sentiment is shallow: several bare-name fades with no reasons attached, and a Ringer host naming him the player who is cooked but whose ADP doesn't show it.

**Draft take:** Fine at rounds 8-9 as a post-injury discount on a paid WR1, but he is the third target in his own offense and the first two weeks are a live risk — draft him as a bench stash who becomes a starter, not as one.
