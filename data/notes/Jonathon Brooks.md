**Jonathon Brooks** (CAR, RB, bye 5) — board rank 127 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The bear case dominates and it is specific — two tears of the same ACL, no finished NFL season, and preseason tape the room read as slow.

- He is dealing with soreness and has not been practicing with the team. Panthers beat writer Joe Person reported he worked on the side rather than with the team on 9/1, and he sat out both practices after the cut-down deadline. Head coach Canales called it normal maintenance, said Tuesday it was soreness and would not specify the location, and then said Wednesday "I'm very optimistic he'll be available for Week 1" against Chicago on Sept 13. As of 9/3 he still had not returned to practice, and one analyst mentioned a report of him tweaking something again — so treat Week 1 as coach-optimism, not a cleared status.
- The context that makes the soreness matter: he is coming off back-to-back ACL surgeries on the same right knee, missed all of last season, and has 12 career NFL touches plus roughly eight preseason carries across three calendar years. He had gotten through all of training camp and two preseason games with little issue before this, which is the one genuinely encouraging fact.
- The preseason look was not good. The room's read (47 points) was that he looked slower than in college and tied it directly to the two tears — one commenter who watched the snaps had him at 3.6 yards per carry against Jacksonville's second and third stringers. A minority called him lively while conceding it was about four carries.
- Role is a share, not a job. Canales has said the backfield will be a committee at worst, so Brooks does not have to beat Chuba Hubbard out to get touches — but that also caps him, and it holds only while Hubbard's hamstring keeps him limited. The claim that Brooks is the locked-in passing-down back came from one commenter arguing off his college receiving, produced no evidence when challenged, and the room dismissed it.
- Market: he goes around RB34 in the seventh or eighth round, often ahead of Hubbard, and drafters reported reaching to 5.09 in a 14-team. That is well ahead of where this board has him. Analysts are scattered — Murchison would go as high as the middle of round 4, Hartitz calls him overdrafted with round 8-9 as the ceiling price, and Loza was high on him in May but now will not take him before the double-digit rounds.
- The sharpest line in the room, at 122 points: "How anyone can be optimistic about this myth of a player is beyond me." The defense is not that the tears are behind him but that they lower his floor and median without lowering his ceiling.

**Draft take:** Fade him at his round-7 price and let someone else pay for the draft capital — he is worth a pick only if he slides into the double-digit rounds.
