**Jonathon Brooks** (CAR, RB, bye 5) — board rank 127 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The most polarising player on the board — second-round draft capital and an open path against 12 career touches and two ACL tears in the same knee.

- **Availability is unresolved.** He sat the first two practices after the cut-down deadline and worked on the side on 9/01 rather than with the team, per Panthers beat writer Joe Person, and was still not back at practice on 9/03. Canales called it "some soreness", would not name the body part, and said Wednesday "I'm very optimistic he'll be available for Week 1" against Chicago on Sept 13 — a coach on a forecast, not a clearance. A framing of it as knee swelling with a reduced timeline was corrected in-thread and downvoted to -13.
- **Two medical professionals in the room defend the knee itself.** A physical therapist is not fading him: Week 1 is 21 months post-op on a revision ACL, close to the ~24-month mark where research shows re-injury risk drops, the surgery was Dr. ElAttrache's, and re-injury rates for revision and primary reconstructions do not differ significantly. An orthopaedic surgeon in the same thread pushes back on the premise rather than the conclusion.
- **The eye test is the majority bear case:** slow and unexplosive in preseason, 3.6 YPC against Jacksonville's 2nd and 3rd stringers, "running in mud." Defenders note the line made the sample unreadable — backup guards, a new left tackle, a rookie right tackle — and that the whole argument rests on nine career carries.
- **Role is a committee at best.** Canales has said the backfield will be a committee at worst, which guarantees a share rather than the job; Panthers fans call it Chuba Hubbard's backfield with any takeover a slow burn. The "locked-in passing-down back" line came from one commenter who produced no evidence when challenged. Rico Dowdle is gone, there is no rookie competition and Hubbard has a hamstring issue, so the path exists — while Brooks holds up physically.
- **The board is far below the room on price.** Board rank 127; the room drafts him round 7-8, around RB34, frequently ahead of Hubbard, with one drafter reporting 5.09 in a 14-teamer. Analysts split: Murchison would go as high as the middle of round 4; Hartitz calls him overdrafted with round 8-9 the ceiling price, citing Hubbard, the preseason tape and a banged-up line; Loza, high on him in May, now rarely rosters him and will not take him before the double-digit rounds.
- **Skepticism runs hot** — "How anyone can be optimistic about this myth of a player is beyond me" drew 122 points — and the room's own bar for him is availability, not production.

**Draft take:** Not worth a round-7 pick until he practises in full; let someone else pay the hype price and take him only if he slides toward the double-digit rounds.
