**Breece Hall** (NYJ, RB, bye 13) — board rank 30 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** A broad, loud fade that is about the Jets rather than about him, and that softens as soon as the price drops a round.

- The August injury scare is closed. Hall came up limping after a screen pass and left Jets practice on 8/17 with what looked like a groin injury; head coach Aaron Glenn downplayed it the same day — "I don't think it was a big deal" — and a commenter later relayed that MRI reports had him expected to be fine for Week 1, with no outlet named. Nothing since 18 August mentions it, and the September claims treat him as available.
- Role is the lead back on a new contract. A commenter calls the deal "insulated" — the money argues the job is safe — though one dissenter expects a 60/40 timeshare. The bull case is a solid Jets run-blocking line, a more pass-heavy quarterback in Geno Smith, and an offense at least minimally better than 2025's. He has averaged 56 catches for 475 yards over the last three seasons.
- The knock on his usage is receiving volume, not touches. Dopp likes him only as an RB2 because the Jets do not use him as a pass catcher as much as they should, and says to prioritise a third back behind him. One ranker's numbers: a career-high 392 explosive rushing yards at a 7.0% explosive run rate, RB4 in the league, against a career-low 2.6 targets per game — and the third-most game-script-dependent back in football at 22.7 points per game in wins versus 10.9 in losses. On a team the room expects to trail, that split is the bear case.
- Market: he had been going around the 2-3 turn and the room's settled view is that a solid third round is his ceiling — roughly where his board rank sits. Sanderson agrees the offensive-environment risk is real but fully priced at a round-3 cost, calling him an R1/R2 talent profile and his preferred round-3 back alongside Jeremiyah Love after a WR-heavy start. Twerski sees a steep drop-off after about the top 12 backs and does not want Hall as his RB1 outside a zero-RB build.
- Sentiment is negative and worth weighing: "no one is in on Hall this year", "you couldn't pay me to draft this guy", an okay RB2 for two straight years, distrust of the coaching staff. Note that the same managers say they are fine with him in the 5th or 6th — the fade is price-sensitive, not absolute. The counter-argument the contrarians make is that he has only been bad in fantasy behind bad quarterbacks, and that he still finished RB19 last year on a team that was much worse.

**Draft take:** Let him come to you at the 3-4 turn — the talent is not in question, but a trap-door floor tied to game script means you are paying for the offense, not the back.
