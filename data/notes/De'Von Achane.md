**De'Von Achane** (MIA, RB, bye 6) — board rank 10 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the second-most-nominated bust in two separate threads, at roughly 431 and 1,499 upvotes — and in nearly every case the argument is about the Miami offense, not about him.

- No injury is reported. The durability worry is frame against workload: 5'9" and 185-195 pounds carrying a projected 200-plus carries and 80-90 targets. The record cuts the other way — one game missed in two seasons as the lead back. The other repeated fade — that Miami tanks and manages his snaps late — is speculation with no report behind it.
- Head coach Jeff Hafley is the strongest source in the file and he is emphatic. He has publicly called Achane the centerpiece of the offense, and told Rich Eisen he warned him: "there might be some games when I have to pick him up and carry him back to the locker room… after you touch the ball 40-45 times", and that he never wants to see a head tap all year. Achane's answer: "You'll never get that head tap from me." The room reads the number as rhetorical and the commitment as real; Ollie Gordon is not a clear number two.
- Baseline production: 323 PPR points and 20.3 per game last season on the 25th-ranked scoring offense, at 5.7 yards per carry, with 10-plus points in every game he played. Career per-game marks of 17.3, 17.3 and 20.3, and one of only six backs above a 70% snap share in 2025 at roughly 15 carries a game. He faced the most stacked boxes in the league and still finished third in yards against eight-plus-man fronts, behind Derrick Henry and James Cook.
- The real dispute is whether the receiving work survives the quarterback change, and the room has largely settled it. The correction repeated across threads is that his receptions were not Tua check-downs but designed pass plays with him as the intended target — roughly 50 designed screens, sweeps and manufactured touches — and Bobby Slowik, last year's passing game coordinator, is now the OC. Cooper expects Slowik to keep using him as a heavy route-runner out of the backfield. Against that: Malik Willis scrambles rather than throwing the checkdown, his career high is 21 pass attempts in a game, and Vegas props imply 58 receptions against last year's 67 — one commenter models that at roughly 15 points a game.
- Sanderson's fade is the most specific and does not depend on talent: Miami will not run many plays, and many will be Willis sacks, runs and scrambles — snaps on which nobody but the quarterback scores. He separates that from the ordinary "bad offense" objection, noting backs from mediocre offenses hit all the time.
- Market and board agree, roughly: he goes late first to mid second — reported picks at 10, 11 and 12 and at the 1/2 turn, one over Barkley at 1.09 and one over Ja'Marr Chase in round 2. FantasyPros is the outlier at 19. Murchison, asked to order Saquon, Walker, Achane, Jeanty and Hampton, put Achane first.

**Draft take:** take him where the board has him — the fade is priced in, the coach has promised the workload, and the receiving volume is scheme, not quarterback.
