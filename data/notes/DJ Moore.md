**DJ Moore** (BUF, WR, bye 7) — board rank 51 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Healthy, unambiguously Buffalo's WR1, and the loudest disagreement on the board between what analysts say he is worth and what the betting markets do.

- **No injury. He dodged camp scares entirely;** the only report is a preseason scare against Carolina, after which Bills OC Joe Brady said Moore would have gone back into the game had it counted.
- **The role is as clean as it gets:** Buffalo traded a second-round pick for him and the room treats him as the entrenched WR1 who draws the opponent's best coverage and moves all over the formation. He reunites with Joe Brady, his OC in Carolina when he took 31% and 36% of team receiving yards. Ben Solak (ESPN) asked around the Bills and heard deep and intermediate-breaking routes, more production off play-action given the run game, and Moore first up for downfield one-on-ones. The room expects heavy 13 personnel with Moore as the lone receiver, and red-zone work going elsewhere. Note the contract dispute the thread never settled — one poster says Buffalo extended him on a restructured deal, another flatly denies it; a separate claim says the dead-cap hit makes moving on before February 2028 impractical.
- **The bear case is Buffalo's distribution, not his talent.** Last year's Bills target shares were Shakir 20%, Coleman 12%, Kincaid 10%, Knox 10%; no Bills receiver has finished top-30 since Diggs' WR9 in 2023, Josh Allen has not thrown to one man for 1,000 yards since 2022, and Allen and James Cook vulture touchdowns. Sanderson's version is harsher: a 14-15% target share is the tell, he has posted sub-1.5 yards per route run two years running, and he is an opportunity bet on an older declining player.
- The counter is that Chicago's shares were nearly as flat — Odunze 17%, Moore 16%, Loveland 15%, Zaccheaus 12%, Burden 11% — so the dip was distribution, and that Caleb Williams was the second-least accurate qualified starter at 58.1%, leaving Moore's catchable-target rate around 64%, roughly 89th of 115 qualified pass-catchers. He finished WR6 in half PPR with Justin Fields and had three straight 1,150-yard seasons in Carolina. The loudest objection is effort rather than age — "Ah, the Allen Robinson Defense" at 170 points, with Bears watchers saying he ran wrong routes and gave up on routes.
- **Market: rank 51 is the platform number, and the room calls it a mirage.** ADP is WR24 on Yahoo/ESPN and WR28 on Underdog, but reported live prices run 3.01 to 7.07, with the two highest-voted replies (994 and 639 points) saying he is the most reached-for player they have seen and cannot be had in the fourth in Buffalo-heavy leagues. Maher has him 35th overall, 20 spots ahead of ECR. The books disagree: his receptions line is 59.5, below Shakir's 62.5, and BettingPros has him at 725 receiving yards, behind Burden's 875 and McConkey's 850. Cooper has money on the receptions prop while conceding the number makes him nervous for fantasy.

**Draft take:** Worth a 5th-round pick and no more — the WR1 role is real, but every number the market actually prices says Buffalo still spreads it around.
