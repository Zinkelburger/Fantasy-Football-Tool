**Javonte Williams** (DAL, RB, bye 14) — board rank 31 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the role is uncontested and the player is doubted — every fade in the corpus is about him, not his workload.

- Healthy. The only injury on record is a shoulder taken in week 15 last year against Minnesota: RB7 in full PPR through week 14, then 27/57/27 percent snap shares in weeks 15-17, and he still finished RB11-RB12.
- **A workhorse with no competition.** He owns early-down and goal-line work on a top-5 offense; commenters call it the clearest workhorse workload in the league, with Malik Davis behind him and Jaydon Blue — since cut by Dallas — left outside the backfield all camp. Dan Graziano (ESPN) reported back on 8/11 that the Cowboys liked Blue but that he was no threat to Williams' workload, and that has only become more true. Hartitz's version is the loudest: bell-cow of an offense that could lead the league in scoring, competition that is "a bunch of NPCs".
- **The ceiling limiter is receiving, and it is well documented.** 1.3 receptions a game over the final 10 weeks last year; 0.45 YPRR in 2025, third-lowest among backs in five years; passing-snap share collapsed from 77.7% in weeks 1-14 to 30.5% in weeks 15-17; 27.0% of his points came from touchdowns. One commenter expects a slightly smaller 2026 role because Dallas keeps trying to add a true pass-catching back.
- Market: RB13-RB15, late third, right about where the board has him — so the board and the room agree on price and disagree only on direction. He was the loudest "too low" nomination in one thread, with drafters reporting 2nd-, 3rd- and 4th-round picks on him. Hartitz calls him undervalued in rounds 2-3 and the dead-zone back he believes in most; Murchison tells a manager at 1.03 that his RB1 will be Javonte, Kyren Williams or Breece Hall and he would be fine with Javonte. Twerski is the counterweight — Javonte is on his ADP fade list and he does not love ending up with that trio as his RB1 unless he went true zero-RB.
- Sentiment: about five fades, all stated as a mediocre player in a powerful offense with no competition. The other side nominates him as unsexy but reliable for points and touchdowns.

**Draft take:** draft him at his late-third cost as a touchdown-dependent RB2 with a workhorse floor, and do not pay up for PPR upside he has never shown.
