**Javonte Williams** (DAL, RB, bye 14) — board rank 31 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the clearest workhorse workload in the league attached to a back the room does not think is good — the fade is about the player, not the role.

- Healthy. The relevant history is a week 15 shoulder injury against Minnesota last year: he was RB7 in full PPR through week 14, then played 27%, 57% and 27% of snaps in weeks 15-17 and still finished RB11-RB12.
- Role is as secure as any in football. He is the clear lead back in a two-man Dallas room that produced 2,100 rushing yards and 18 rushing touchdowns in 2025, owning early-down and goal-line work under an OC the room rates highly for backs. Dan Graziano of ESPN reported (8/11) that the Cowboys like second-year back Jaydon Blue but that he is not a threat to Williams' workload, and a Cowboys commenter says Williams' return to form left Blue outside the backfield all camp with Malik Davis behind him. A 44-point comment argues an improved Dallas defense means more rushing volume, not less.
- The bear case is receiving, and it is specific: 0.45 yards per route run in 2025, third-lowest among running backs in five years; his passing-snap share collapsed from 77.7% in weeks 1-14 to 30.5% in weeks 15-17; he averaged 1.3 receptions a game over the final 10 weeks; and 27.0% of his fantasy points came from touchdowns. The room grants him an extremely secure goal-line role and no explosive or receiving upside. One commenter expects a slightly smaller role in 2026 because Dallas has been trying hard to find a true pass-catching back to mix in.
- Market: he goes RB13-RB15, late 3rd round, roughly at board rank 31 — but the room drafts him earlier than that in practice, with reports of 2nd-, 3rd- and 4th-round picks and one 10th-round keeper. Analysts split hard: Hartitz calls him undervalued in rounds 2-3 and the dead-zone back he believes in most — a bell cow on an offense that could lead the league in scoring, with competition he calls "a bunch of NPCs" — while Twerski lists him among his ADP fades and does not love ending up with him as an RB1. Murchison treats him as the realistic RB1 for a manager picking at 1.03 in an RB-heavy league.
- Sentiment: about five fades, all with the same stated case — a mediocre player in a powerful offense with no competition.

**Draft take:** draft him in round 3 as a volume-and-touchdown RB2, and downgrade him a full round in PPR where his 1.3 catches a game are the ceiling.
