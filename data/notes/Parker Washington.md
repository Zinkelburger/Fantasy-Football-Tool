**Parker Washington** (JAC, WR, bye 7) — board rank 93 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The most argued player in the corpus — camp reporting and the analyst class are close to unanimous, and the loudest counter is that the price has already eaten all of it.

- The room's read is that he is Jacksonville's top target and slot receiver in Liam Coen's offense: he held the role late last season, survived a regime change as a 6th-round pick, and was featured in the same role through the preseason, with coaches and Trevor Lawrence talking him up.
- Three separate beat writers say the same thing. John Shipley (SI) called him the Jaguars' most consistent playmaker all camp (8/9) and, two days later, said he has separated himself as Lawrence's favorite target in one of the NFL's best receiver rooms. Michael DiRocco (ESPN) watched him beat Travis Hunter off the line for a deep sideline throw from Lawrence. A commenter adds that every reporter out of camp is calling him Lawrence's No. 1, and that Hunter has barely worked with the offense this summer.
- The volume record supports it: 7-plus targets in 8 of 10 games at a snap share above 50%, four of them 10-target games; from Week 9 on he led Jacksonville in yards and touchdowns; and in the five games he, Jakobi Meyers and Brian Thomas all played, he led the team in targets, catches and yards.
- The bear case is competition and sustainability. Skeptics call him "WR1 due to circumstance" — the slot led because Thomas missed time or was limited. He now shares F snaps with Travis Hunter and Z snaps with Hunter and Meyers, work he did not have to share last year. Meyers just signed for $60M at $20M a year and Thomas has a 1,300-yard season and the heaviest draft capital. And he led the NFL in contested-catch rate at roughly 65% among players with 20-plus contested targets against a 45% league average, which the room calls unrepeatable.
- The analysts split on price, not on role. Hartitz says everything out of camp is that Washington is THE guy, with no threat from Hunter; Murchison was skeptical this summer and now calls him an easy WR2; Maher has him 38th overall and WR22 against an ADP near 67, his single biggest receiver disagreement in the round 4-6 range; Twerski would take him over Hurts, Odunze, Burrow, Daniels, LaPorta, Henderson and Stevenson at cost. Against them, Cooper prefers Luther Burden because Jacksonville's target competition beats anything in Chicago after Odunze, and a Ringer host asked whether Washington is the WR1 answers "not in the traditional sense, no. In the sense that he could lead the team in receiving stats — very possible, borderline probably."
- Market, and this is the decision: the board has him near 93 while the room has been drafting him in rounds 5-8 around WR35, with reports of the 4th and 6th, $24 in auction, and an ADP that has spiralled from rounds 10-11 in early mocks to rounds 4-7 and is still climbing daily — ahead of Odunze, Evans, Watson, McLaurin, MHJ and Addison. The board is a round or two behind where he actually goes. Hartitz, who likes him, still warns against chasing the rising best-ball ADP this late because earlier drafters got him far cheaper. In dynasty he is the No. 3 most-traded player at a 4,808 market value, and several owners are treating him as a live sell-high.
- Sentiment is genuinely split rather than merely noisy: ten top-level nominations and roughly 1,480 upvotes in one thread, with "the most obvious fantasy trap in years" set against WR21 overall last year and WR14 from weeks 10-17, and "Parker Washington is the WR3... on his own team" drawing 65 points. Two practical notes from his own backers: Jacksonville's opening schedule is brutal, and he is the punt returner, worth a round in return-yardage formats if they keep him there.

**Draft take:** The role is as well sourced as anything in this corpus, but the room has already paid for it — take him in the 6th, let someone else reach in the 4th.
