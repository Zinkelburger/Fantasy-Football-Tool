**Parker Washington** (JAC, WR, bye 7) — board rank 93 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the single most argued player in the corpus — one camp calls him the most obvious fantasy trap in years at a rising ADP, the other points at WR21 overall last year and WR14 from weeks 10-17 and says he is still underpriced.

- Role, and it is the best-sourced thing on this page. John Shipley (SI Jaguars) called him the Jaguars' most consistent playmaker all camp and, on Aug 11, wrote that he has separated himself as Trevor Lawrence's favourite target in one of the league's best receiver rooms. Michael DiRocco (ESPN) had him beating Travis Hunter off the line for a deep Lawrence throw down the left sideline. He is Jacksonville's slot in Liam Coen's offense, held the role late last season, survived the regime change as a former 6th-round pick, and was featured in the same role in the preseason with coaches and Lawrence talking him up.
- The analysts have converged. Hartitz: everything out of camp is that Washington is "THE guy," with no threat of Hunter taking his snaps, and a bad offensive line argues for feeding the low-aDOT slot. Murchison was skeptical this summer and now calls him an easy WR2. Dopp would start him over Courtland Sutton and Michael Wilson in Week 1.
- The bear case is target competition, not talent: Brian Thomas Jr, Jakobi Meyers on a fresh $60M deal, Brenton Strange and a lurking Travis Hunter. Several say there is as much chance he finishes the No. 3 as the No. 1, and Cooper prefers Luther Burden for that reason. Supporters counter that he already produced with all of them but Hunter on the field — 7+ targets in 8 of 10 games at a >50% snap share and the team lead in yards and touchdowns from Week 9 on. The counter to the counter is that Thomas missed or was limited: "WR1 due to circumstance." A Ringer host split it: WR1 "not in the traditional sense, no. In the sense that he could lead the team in receiving stats — very possible, borderline probably."
- Market: this is where the board is stale. Rank 93 is roughly his early-August price; the room now drafts him in rounds 4-8, around WR35, ahead of Odunze, Evans, McLaurin and Addison, and it is still climbing daily. Maher has him 38th overall and WR22, his single biggest WR disagreement in the round 4-6 range; Twerski would take him over Hurts, Odunze, Burrow, Daniels, LaPorta and Henderson at current cost. The counterweights are honest: Hartitz warns against chasing the risen best-ball ADP this late, and one podcast host names him among the sexy guys now priced too high. In dynasty holders treat him as a live sell-high — one moved him for a 1st, a 2nd and two 3rds.
- Sentiment: the fade is a fade of the hype rather than the player — an 82-point nomination mocking the camp-report genre, and the argument that a late-season stretch in a fourth season is a weak predictor. Jacksonville's brutal opening schedule is the other caution.

**Draft take:** the role is as well-sourced as anything in this corpus and the board rank is weeks behind the market — take him in the 5th or 6th, and pass in the 4th where the price has already caught the news.
