**Keaton Mitchell** (LAC, RB, bye 7) — board rank 154 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** A speed archetype in the right scheme who cannot stay on the field, and the room is more sceptical than his ADP is.

- Two availability facts matter and neither is good for him. Omarion Hampton — whose absence is the entire case for drafting Mitchell — is healthy, and the in-thread dissent is that Mitchell needs both Hampton and Kimani Vidal out to matter. And Mitchell himself is hurt right now. Two commenters say he is already hurt going into Week 1 (Sept 3), one adding that he "always seems to be," and both recommend dropping him for a healthy bench back; a "mild setback" had held him out of consecutive practices on Aug 26 after he was hurt in a preseason game. None of this is a beat report — it is commenters — but it is current and consistent.
- Durability is the standing knock and predates the Chargers: a small back just over a year removed from a very serious injury, and Baltimore let him walk rather than find him touches.
- The role is change-of-pace behind Omarion Hampton. Los Angeles signed him to replace Haskins and Mike McDaniel wanted him specifically as the De'Von Achane archetype, having publicly hinted at playing the hot hand. The efficiency cited for him is 6.3 yards per carry — on only 112 career carries.
- The receiving profile is what kills the Achane comparison: he does not average one catch per game across 26 career games, and Hampton's injury-shortened 2025 alone produced nearly twice Mitchell's career catch total. He could not take receiving work from Justice Hill in Baltimore, is too small for the goal line, and is not a good enough blocker for third down. His 2025 behind Derrick Henry: 13 games, a season high of 9 carries twice, four or fewer in seven games, about 2 yards per carry and roughly 300 total yards. He was, however, one of Baltimore's best kick returners and could open on the Chargers' return unit.
- The contract reads as backup — about $4.4M a year over two years, outside the top 30 RB salaries and below Chris Rodriguez — with the counter that LA could have had Brian Robinson at $2.5M or Najee Harris at $1.2M and chose to pay more.
- Analysts size the role differently: Hartitz does not expect top-24 numbers but can see an RB3 season, because the Chargers offense, play-caller and archetype optimize 8-10 touches, and calls him a good later-round pick behind an anchor back. Murchison wants him only as an end-of-bench RB4/RB5 stash. Twerski names him a must-grab handcuff.
- Market: about 158th, not taken until round 13 in one reported league; several in the room argue Kimani Vidal is the actual Hampton handcuff. Ravens fans, on the back they let leave, say he lost a step, dropped passes and was a poor pass protector.

**Draft take:** Hampton is healthy and Mitchell is not, and the room says Kimani Vidal is the real handcuff — leave Mitchell undrafted unless your bench can hold a hurt third-stringer.
