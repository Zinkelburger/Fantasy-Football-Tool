**Malik Washington** (MIA, WR, bye 6) — board rank 178 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** he holds the title on paper and almost nobody buys it — the two highest-voted answers to "who is Miami's WR1" were Caleb Douglas sarcastically (149 pts) and "Devon Achane is wr1" (93 pts).

- Miami's unofficial depth chart has him as the starting slot receiver and the primary kick and punt returner, and he sits atop the ESPN and FantasyPros depth charts too. Steve Smith Sr — the quote that titled the thread — calls him "the No. 1 wide receiver" and Miami's leading receiver for 2026; commenters note Smith has no stated connection to the Dolphins beyond liking the player, so read it as evaluation rather than inside information.
- The room's verdict is slot-only gadget player. He will not be on the field in one- and two-receiver sets, Miami is shifting to heavier 12 personnel, and Jalen Tolbert plays ahead of him there. A self-identified Dolphins fan (23 pts) and a 35-point comment both say he will be behind at least two of the rookies in targets by Week 12, naming Chris Bell and Caleb Douglas, with his ceiling put at maybe four weeks of WR1 volume until Bell is up to speed. His quick-route usage specifically is at risk once Bell is active, though one reply argues the skill sets differ enough not to overlap.
- The return jobs are the hedge and are themselves disputed: he is listed first for both, one commenter projects a top 10-15 WR finish at 0.05 points per return yard if he keeps them alongside the WR1 role, and several doubt Miami keeps its lead receiver on returns at all. The counter is that a room this thin puts its most explosive healthy body in both places.
- The profile the room gives him: 5'8", manufactured touches on screens and quick slants, also takes handoffs, but with clean routes, solid hands and some downfield ability — 110 ACC receptions and 1,400+ all-purpose yards in college, compared repeatedly to Wan'Dale Robinson, with Josh Downs and Zay Flowers offered as proof small slot receivers produce. He is disputed as a deep threat. Cooper rates him the safer of the Miami options in deep leagues, over Douglas and Bell.
- Market and sentiment are both cheap, and well behind board rank 178: he goes undrafted or at the very end, auction minimum, waiver pickup. Bulls say a 30% target share even in a ~20-attempt offense is about six targets and a free flex play; bears answer he was already the option after the Waddle trade last year and the game logs explain why he is free. Comps offered are "Temu Jarvis Landry" and "Temu Wan'Dale," with 8-10 PPG as a safe bye-week flex and a WR3-in-14-team-PPR ceiling.

**Draft take:** last-round PPR and return-yardage dart, not a WR1 — the depth-chart title is real and the room expects the rookies past him by midseason, so do not pay board rank for it.
