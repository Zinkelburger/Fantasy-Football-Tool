**Jake Ferguson** (DAL, TE, bye 14) — board rank 106 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** a genuine TE5 finish that the room has correctly diagnosed as a CeeDee Lamb injury artifact, and priced accordingly.

- The production baseline is real: TE5 in total PPR points at 11.1 per game last year with nine top-12 weekly finishes, the 7th most-targeted TE, and second among all tight ends with 24 red-zone targets in 2025 after leading the position with 25 in 2023. Half-PPR splits are less kind — TE8 total, TE13 per game.
- The split that matters: 17 PPR points in games where Lamb's route share was under 50% versus 9.2 otherwise, and 0.3 points per route run with both Lamb and Pickens playing — outside the top 40, at 1.06 yards per route. A separate commenter puts it more bluntly: TE20 in points per game with everyone healthy, TE2 in points per game across the five games Lamb was banged up. The rebuttal is a set of 0.5-TEP weeks showing he still produces as the third option.
- Route competition is the newer worry. Hartitz is concerned his routes come down because of Brevyn Spann-Ford and Luke Schoonmaker, and would start Isaiah Likely over him. Commenters in August said Ryan Flournoy had taken the third-target role outright — that read is conditional on Lamb being healthy. An improved Dallas defense is also cited as a reason for fewer pass attempts.
- Camp reports run the other way: Joseph Hoyt (Dallas Morning News, 8/9) had him continuing to play well while Schoonmaker had multiple drops, and Dan Graziano (ESPN, 8/11) said that at the practice he attended it "felt like close to 1,000" catches. He faded badly over the last 8-10 weeks once Lamb returned, though his weeks 15-18 opponents were all top defenses against tight ends.
- Market: the board has him 106th; the room takes him in the 11th round or later, after every other startable TE is gone, and pairs him with Juwan Johnson as a late double-dip — one poster took Ferguson in the 11th and Johnson in the 16th. Earlier reads called him a big value in the 8th-9th, and a low-end TE1 / high-end TE2 rather than an elite one. One manager notes he had lots of offers last season and now nobody is interested.

**Draft take:** the floor tight end, not the ceiling one — take him in the 11th as the second half of a late TE double-dip, and expect real weeks only when Lamb or Pickens is out.
