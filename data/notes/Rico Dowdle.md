**Rico Dowdle** (PIT, RB, bye 9) — board rank 68 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the beat expects him to lead the Pittsburgh backfield and the room keeps finding reasons the split will not hold.

- Mark Kaboly (8/9), answering a mailbag on the Steelers split: "It's been pretty even so far in camp, but I think that Dowdle will be the RB1 and get the most snaps and opportunities. It's not going to be a 50/50 split, but 60/40 is possible." Ben Solak (ESPN, 8/11) cannot get a lean between Dowdle and Jaylen Warren but is confident both are playable in any given week — a read that only holds while Warren is splitting the backfield.
- The role case: Pittsburgh's new head coach targeted him in free agency and paid him for a significant role, he is slotted for early-down and goal-line work, and Mike McCarthy was his head coach in Dallas. One commenter predicts roughly 65/35 with Dowdle as the RB1, on the coach's history of leaning on one back and Warren having proven he cannot handle bulk. Vegas has Dowdle's rushing-yards over/under above Warren's.
- The pass-protection objection is the recurring one and it is specific: Aaron Rodgers dislikes backs who cannot block, Warren is one of the best at it and Dowdle is not, so Dowdle gets squeezed toward early downs while Warren keeps third down. One poster fades him for Warren outright, calling Warren more talented with the stronger PPR receiving role, citing what Gainwell did as the primary receiving back there last year. Another commenter has him in an uncertain committee and would start MarShawn Lloyd over him at RB2 in Week 1.
- The room relitigates his 2025 constantly. He finished RB17 with a 1,000-yard season, but over half of it came in three games, one announced minutes before kickoff, and critics call him replacement level otherwise. His Carolina stretch was 473 total yards on 53 carries at 7.3 ypc across two starts with Hubbard hurt — against, the room notes, the 31st- and 32nd-ranked rush defenses, and Hubbard reclaimed the playoff workload on Dowdle's pass blocking. Defenders call him the clearly better pure runner and Pittsburgh's best on his Carolina tape, with no true hammer back left after the Johnson trade.
- Sentiment: Twerski has him on both his sleeper list and his irrationally-confident list; one commenter simply says he is going to take over the Steelers backfield. Nobody reports an actual draft price, so board rank 68 is the only price on the page.

**Draft take:** a 60/40 early-down lead back with the receiving work capped by Warren — worth his RB price for the volume, not for a bell-cow outcome.
