**Romeo Doubs** (NE, WR, bye 11) — board rank 124 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room likes the landing spot more than the player; the argument is whether New England's WR2 job is a real target share or a placeholder.

- Role: New England's WR2 behind A.J. Brown after signing in free agency for a deal reported up to 4 years/$80M — around $20M a year with $39M guaranteed. The team then moved Kayshon Boutte, which several commenters read as locking Doubs in as the No. 2 weapon.
- The case for him is usage, not production: 832 snaps last year, the most of any Packers receiver, 73% route participation and 5.3 targets a game, second on the team. Commenters call him a legitimate NFL X receiver with less skill-set overlap with Brown than Boutte had, and multiple Patriots fans say he is the better player of the two.
- The case against is also usage: four years without a true Packers WR1 ahead of him still produced only a WR41 season, with TPRR ranks of 43rd and 60th and a career best of 55-724. One commenter dismisses him as a JAG and uses that as the reason Brown should see 150-plus targets. A separate consensus read is that Boutte's freed snaps go to Kyle Williams and Mack Hollins, making the bump to Doubs marginal at most.
- Volume context the room agrees on: the offense supported a receiver at roughly 180 targets last year, so the secondary role has real work in it even if Doubs is not a Tee Higgins-level partner. Supporters add that New England's schedule and poor rushing efficiency should force more passing than last year's 24th-ranked pass rate. His path to more than WR2 work runs through A.J. Brown's knee injury costing time or effectiveness.
- Market: the room drafts him around WR52 in the 13th round — behind Higgins, Odunze, Sutton, Pittman, Burden and Tyson — a round or two later than board rank 124 implies. Sentiment is warm: 5 nominations worth 276 points in one thread, the second-highest-upvoted answer there, mostly from people who like the move rather than the player.

**Draft take:** A real WR2 role at a WR5 price — take him in the 12th or 13th as a bet on New England's pass volume, not on a leap.
