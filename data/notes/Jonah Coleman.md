**Jonah Coleman** (DEN, RB, bye 10) — board rank 133 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** genuinely hyped and openly aware it may be a preseason mirage — the argument is not whether he is good but whether Payton's committee ever lets it show.

- He is Denver's third back behind J.K. Dobbins and RJ Harvey, and the coach is the best evidence for him. Asked why he pulled Coleman early in the first preseason game, Sean Payton said "I didn't need to see additional evidence of what I've been seeing for 3 weeks"; a commenter separately reports Payton saying Coleman already looks like a veteran, and notes Payton never said anything comparable about Harvey. The room reads it as Payton protecting a back he has already decided on.
- Standalone role, per an Aug 18 round-up: trending up as a good pass catcher whose pass blocking is looking really good, described as capable of being a three-down back and the best Denver has looked at running back since Javonte Williams pre-injury, and expected to take pass-catching work from Harvey even with Dobbins healthy. By early September Denver backers go further — the superior back to Harvey with a shot at taking the job outright in-season. The repeated counter is that he splits even if Dobbins goes down — "Coleman is in a timeshare with Dobbins healthy" — which holds only while Harvey keeps a rotation role.
- The bear case is the coach, not the player: "You don't take Payton RBs," a track record of ambiguous committees that never clarify. One commenter projects around 10 touches a game and says Coleman would have to be hyper-efficient or win red-zone and target share to matter. Even the 40-point bull comment praising his vision and refusal to go down on contact concedes meaningful production most likely still requires a Dobbins injury.
- The analysts differ only on how close he is: Murchison has him giving strong RB2 numbers whenever Dobbins misses a stretch, Cooper puts the odds of him taking the job outright at "north of zero" and keeps him over other handcuffs, and Hartitz is the low man — two injuries away from being meaningfully relevant.
- Market is dynasty-only and the room thinks it is too rich: self-reported rookie-draft cost of 1.12 twice, 2.06 and 2.07, with sightings at 1.08 and 1.09, and a 1.05 called indefensible — "the draft capital isn't there" — with 1.09-1.10 the suggested floor. He was the 108th overall NFL pick.
- Sentiment: three top-level predictions (~86 pts) have him as the most productive rookie back this year, one going as far as a top-10 RB finish.

**Draft take:** draft him at his board rank as the Dobbins insurance you actually want, but pay a handcuff price — the standalone role is Payton's to withhold and he has withheld it from better players.
