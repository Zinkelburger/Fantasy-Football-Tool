**Travis Kelce** (KC, TE, bye 5) — board rank 103 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The market claim is the only bullish thing here — everywhere else he is discussed as a name whose tier has slipped.

- Role: no reported change to his job. Kansas City kept four tight ends and a commenter notes Kelce is the best blocker of the group — a usage note, not a receiving one.
- The value case: he finished TE4-TE5 last year on 108 targets, 76 catches and 851 yards, has never finished worse than about TE5, and plays every game. Commenters say the Mahomes connection guarantees a 7-8 target floor per game, with Walker's presence softening linebackers underneath.
- The fade case, from the same threads: 6 of 17 games under 10 points last year, and he is well past his prime. Chiefs fans describe an offense forced to "treat old man Kelce like prime Kelce," with defenses jamming and blanketing him and Kelce dropping a lot of passes; several say tight end alongside receiver has to be Kansas City's next-offseason priority, and one raises the possibility he retires.
- Market: he goes as TE11 around picks 117-120, on a Yahoo ADP near 94, and multiple drafters call that clear value over what else goes in that range. Board rank 103 is a touch ahead of where the room actually takes him.
- Sentiment is uniformly cool, and the room's rankings are more useful than its adjectives: multiple posters would start Dallas Goedert over him this season, flipping only when Rashee Rice is on the field, and a roster-crunch aside voted 18-5 to start Juwan Johnson over him. He was named nine times in one tight-end thread for about 14 total upvotes, mostly as the historical example of a TE worth an early pick rather than a 2026 target. Murchison expects the Kansas City production spread out and inconsistent enough that no Chief is pinpointable week to week.

**Draft take:** Take him in the 11th as a floor TE1 if the position has run dry, but the room's own rankings put him behind cheaper starters — do not pay the 9th-round price the board implies.
