**Trey McBride** (ARI, TE, bye 14) — board rank 19 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Nobody argues he is the best tight end in football; the whole debate is whether last year's volume was a one-off and what that is worth at a second-round price.

- No injury claims. Role is as settled as it gets: the unquestioned first option in Arizona and the fixed point every Wilson/Harrison target-share argument is measured against — 72 receiving yards per game in 2025 to Michael Wilson's 50, stated flatly in a 98-point comment as the reason to be cautious on the Cardinals' receivers.
- The volume is the case: 60 more targets than the next tight end — a gap commenters compare to WR5 versus WR40-50 — 126 catches, 1,239 yards and 11 touchdowns, a second straight 100-catch, 1,000-yard season, roughly 100 points clear of TE2, and the 9th-best overall flex scorer. He averaged 14.9 half-PPR points per game, would have finished WR5 in total points, and led all tight ends from Weeks 9-17 at 21.0 per game, three clear of Kittle.
- The regression case is about the coaching staff, not the quarterback. Drew Petzing, the OC who targeted him heavily, left for Detroit; LaFleur is the new head coach and play-caller and neither he nor Hackett has historically featured tight ends, and nobody in the corpus has beat reporting either way. The usage being extrapolated from is extreme: 667 routes, 162 more than Kelce and the equivalent of a 21-game season, Arizona first in the league in pass attempts (649) and pass rate over expectation (7.3%), and 39.9% of his points scored while trailing. JJ Zacharison's version prices the inflation at roughly 54 points, dropping him from 18.6 to about 15.4 per game. Also named: a stronger Cardinals running back room, and quarterback ambiguity if Brissett is benched for Carson Beck, which several expect in the fantasy playoff weeks.
- The counter on quarterback play is his own splits — 111/1,146/2 in 2024 with Murray taking nearly every snap against 108/1,083/10 in 14 games with Brissett, better rate numbers and five times the touchdowns with the backup. And the rebuttal the room keeps landing on: strip four touchdowns and he still averaged 13.6 half-PPR per game, which would have been WR10.
- Market: the board and the room agree. ADP 20, going 20-25 overall — late 2nd to early 3rd in 12-team, the 3rd in 10-team — with reported picks clustering from 2.12 to 3.11 and outliers at pick 30 and 4.03, sometimes ahead of Brock Bowers. The room's rule is take him in the 3rd or punt the position. The analyst dissent is real: Cooper will not spend a 2nd or 3rd, arguing the season needed a perfect runout — the MHJ injury, the quarterback change, and Arizona being the only team that didn't run 30 times a game — and Loza is passing at that cost for Loveland or Warren in round 4.
- Sentiment: he was on 22.4% of ESPN championship rosters, their 4th most common player on a title team — and also the most-named of the "top-three TE who ruins your team" picks. The split is genuine.

**Draft take:** Take him at the 2/3 turn if you want a tight end at all — the volume will not repeat under a new play-caller, but strip four touchdowns and he was still WR10, and that is a price worth paying.
