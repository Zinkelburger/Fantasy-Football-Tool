**Makai Lemon** (PHI, WR, bye 10) — board rank 95 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** A rookie who missed everything and then came back to all the first-team reps — the usage signal is stronger than anything he did on a field this summer.

- Injury: a hamstring cost him all of OTAs, most of training camp and both preseason games. Per a Sept 3 article he was frustrated about it, and commenters confirm he has since returned to the field. No timeline was given and nothing suggests he misses regular-season time; one commenter expects it to linger and further concentrate targets on DeVonta Smith.
- Role, and this is the load-bearing item: on returning he immediately took all first-team reps and was the main motion man throughout practice — described as the staff's "muse" in the passing game. Commenters point out Philadelphia spent a first and two fourths to get him and read the first-team usage, not the draft cost, as the real signal.
- The job is open. With A.J. Brown gone, Lemon is one of the unproven options the Eagles need to step up as WR2 opposite DeVonta Smith, and the room is openly waiting to see him play rather than assuming it — several expect him to take the job from Dontayvion Wicks before long, though a commenter relaying film analyst Brett Whitefield's view says the takeover will be harder than assumed. Deployment is disputed: one camp has him as the slot who makes Eli Stowers redundant, another says the long-term plan is Z and offensive move piece. Either way the expectation is more three-receiver sets once he is up to speed, which is the mechanism the room says dilutes Smith's share.
- Two live caveats. He needs time to get his legs under him early in the year — one commenter sees him as a viable WR2 alongside Smith only by season's end. And he is fighting a proven DeVonta Smith for targets while Hurts has historically struggled over the middle of the field, which is exactly where Lemon's game is strongest. The upside case is explicitly conditional on an injury ahead of him: a Smith injury opens the kind of target share Cooper Kupp's opened for Puka Nacua. He also shows up on Ourlads' primary returner chart.
- Market: dynasty managers are paying up — one traded 1.08 and 2.08 to move to 1.06 for him, another traded up from 1.10 to 1.04. Cooper ranks him second among rookie receivers while noting he is nowhere near a known commodity. Twerski picks him as the rookie receiver most likely to break out, on the clearest path to targets and a good offense. No redraft-price claims either way.

**Draft take:** Worth the board's rank on the first-team reps alone, but draft him as a bench receiver who arrives midseason, not a Week 1 starter coming off a hamstring that erased his whole summer.
