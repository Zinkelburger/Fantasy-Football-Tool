**Jaxon Smith-Njigba** (SEA, WR, bye 11) — board rank 8 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Nobody argues he is bad; the entire debate is whether 2025 was a role or an anomaly, and the bust nominations are really regression forecasts.

- The 2025 usage is the whole conversation, and it is extreme: 1,793 yards on Seattle's 4,047 team passing yards — 44.29% of the offence — at 0.33 targets per route run, on only 10 touchdowns. Several commenters call that the highest receiving-yard share ever recorded, and the consensus read (52, 18 and 16 points) is that it is an anomaly nobody should project forward.
- Seahawks fans attribute the leap to opportunity rather than a change in the player: with DK Metcalf gone he became the undisputed WR1 and won Offensive Player of the Year. He remains the unquestioned alpha, and the room expects a young backfield to force passing volume.
- The regression case names concrete losses: OC Klint Kubiak is gone, Walker left for Kansas City, Charbonnet is hurt with a rookie behind, and defences now have a year of film — **the bull case runs only if the new coordinator keeps his target share**. One commenter could see 1,400 yards and would be shocked at a repeat. Rashid Shaheed's improving chemistry with Darnold is cited as a second drain. The bull counter is that more pass volume offsets lower efficiency and that Darnold feeds his top target by habit. One commenter also notes he sat out roughly eight fourth quarters in 2025 because Seattle was blowing teams out.
- Market has him where the board does, or slightly ahead. He goes about 8th, has fallen to 8-9 in several reported drafts and gone as high as 1.03; managers at picks 5 and 6 say they take him over James Cook, CMC and Jonathan Taylor if he slides. He is the cheapest of the elite WRs in auction (21% of a superflex budget, against Chase 26, Puka 23, St. Brown 22) and the near-unanimous keeper answer at a 4th- or 5th-round cost. Twerski has him sixth overall and names him and Amon-Ra the only two receivers he would refuse to pass on at picks 8 and 13; Yahoo ranks him WR4; he was 5th on the player-voted NFL Top 100 after going unranked a year earlier.
- Sentiment: he drew 20 top-level bust nominations for roughly 1,243 upvotes, but nearly all frame it as regression to WR8-WR15 rather than a genuine bust. The other side calls the fade "anti-Pacific-Northwest groupthink" and says he gives you 20 points a game regardless of the play-caller. One useful piece of history from the defenders: no receiver has led the league in receiving yards in back-to-back seasons since Megatron.

**Draft take:** Take him at 8 without hesitating and price in the step back — WR8-to-WR15 is the realistic floor being argued, and that is still worth a late first.
