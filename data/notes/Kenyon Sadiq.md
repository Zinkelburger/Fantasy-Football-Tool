**Kenyon Sadiq** (NYJ, TE, bye 13) — board rank 137 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** A first-round athlete who missed the camp he needed, sitting behind a tight end the room likes better.

- Availability: he was sidelined throughout Jets camp per a beat report, which left him behind Mason Taylor on the team's first unofficial depth chart, and he is still referred to as injured as of Sept 2. The underlying issue is offseason hernia surgery plus a setback with it; a commenter calling it a back injury was corrected in-thread, with the note that hernia repairs typically leave no lingering effects. No timeline has been given. The room's read is that missing most of camp derails a rookie tight end's learning curve and makes him a slow starter — the comparison drawn is Colston Loveland, also hurt entering his rookie year and slow to get going.
- Role depends on Mason Taylor, who is 22 and holds the Y job. While Taylor is in-line, Sadiq projects to the field mainly in two-TE sets and obvious passing downs; he is described as unable to play in-line himself, which caps him at an Isaiah Likely-style ceiling. The countervailing force is draft capital — multiple commenters expect the Jets to force him onto the field for most of the move-TE reps because of where they took him, regardless of camp. One calls the pick itself terrible and says Taylor is the better prospect.
- The film reads are bad: cannot break tackles, goes down immediately when a defender gets hands on him, and much of his college production was schemed block-and-leaks or wheel routes against busted coverage. He uses open space well but has real trouble running a route tree. Cooper is on the other side, naming him a "pure upside" late tight end who could become a top-2 target on his team without any receiver injury, and one of his favourite late-round swings. One commenter's touchdown case: Breece Hall gets the offense close to the goal line even on a bad team, and the tight end eats option and play-action scores there.
- Market: he is a late dart around pick 142, paired with another punt tight end while the room waits to see who Geno Smith checks down to. In dynasty he went 1.09/1.10 and the room now says taking Eli Stowers over him was never defensible (78 pts), citing the draft capital gap; in TE-premium he is the clear pick of the 1.08 tier.
- Sentiment: split — several worry an athlete who tested that well produced so little in college, others compare him to Vernon Davis, who did nothing for three years before a year-four breakout. Two predictions have Mason Taylor and Michael Mayer both outscoring him this year.

**Draft take:** A last-round dynasty swing on the athleticism, not a 2026 tight end — he missed camp, Mason Taylor has the in-line job, and the draft capital is the only thing arguing he plays.
