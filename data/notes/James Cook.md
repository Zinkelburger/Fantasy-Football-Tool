**James Cook** (BUF, RB, bye 7) — board rank 7 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room's highest-floor first-round back; the argument is entirely about whether he has already had his ceiling season.

- Volume is unchallenged — 18.2 carries a game in 2025 and the NFL rushing title — but the snap share is not a bellcow's: 48% in 2024 and 57% in 2025, against 75%-plus for other top backs. One commenter cannot name the Bills' RB2, which is the point.
- The passing-game story is the bull case and it is beat-sourced. Ben Solak (ESPN) says his sense was already that Buffalo wants Cook more involved as a receiver, and that the drumbeat gets louder if Ty Johnson is at all limited. Alex Brasky, after hearing OC Pete Carmichael talk about using him as a receiver, expects at least 50 receptions this year. Against it: his targets, receptions, yards and passing-down snaps have declined every season, and he was 28th in targets per game last year. Cooper adds that Cook splits pass work with Ty Johnson, splits the goal line with Josh Allen and has never topped roughly 302 PPR points even in an 18-touchdown season.
- Goal line is the one consistent knock: Cook had 16 carries inside the 5 last year and Allen had 17, and several posters cite Allen vulturing six-plus rushing touchdowns a season as their reason to pass.
- Regression case: 30 touchdowns in two years for a sub-200-pound back who scored six three years ago behind a comparably good offense, on 342 touches last season — the same wear argument the room applies to Jonathan Taylor. The offsets are that he was among the top two backs in the league in yards against 8-plus man boxes, alongside Derrick Henry, and that DJ Moore's arrival should lighten boxes even if it costs some rush volume. Joe Brady's offense is expected to go downfield more with Kromer gone from the run game, and Ray Davis could take a bigger timeshare.
- Market: he goes around 7.5 overall and the room does not expect him to last past pick 12 — drafters at picks 3 through 10 all report planning to take him, and the board rank matches. Maher has him RB4 and 8th overall in half PPR, just ahead of Christian McCaffrey and Justin Jefferson, and takes him at 1.08. Hartitz ranks him ahead of Jonathan Taylor. Twerski is the dissent: he does not love Cook at 1.05, would not take him over Amon-Ra St. Brown, and specifically dislikes stacking him with Josh Allen.
- Sentiment frames him as floor rather than ceiling — "RB1 or RB12 and nothing outside that", the cleanest injury history in his tier. The sharpest bear line is that he led the NFL in rushing, was unbelievably efficient and still finished only RB6, so this price already assumes the good version. Practical drawbacks: a week 7 bye shared with many good teams, and no definite handcuff behind him.

**Draft take:** A defensible pick anywhere from 1.04 to 1.08 for the floor, but pay for RB6-with-more-receptions, not for a leap he has not shown.
