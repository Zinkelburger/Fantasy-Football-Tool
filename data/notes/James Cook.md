**James Cook** (BUF, RB, bye 7) — board rank 7 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the room's default safe pick at the back of round 1 — the argument is entirely about whether the ceiling is top-5 or capped near where he already finished.

- No injury or job question. The live debate is snap share and touchdown source, and the receiving-role story now has names on it. Ben Solak of ESPN (8/11): his sense was already that Buffalo wants Cook more involved in the passing game, and if Ty Johnson is at all limited that drumbeat only gets louder. Alex Brasky, on hearing OC Pete Carmichael talk about using Cook as a receiver, expects at least 50 receptions for him this year.
- The snap-share case behind that: a 48% share in 2024 and 57% in 2025, with room to grow under new head coach Joe Brady. Murchison says the passing-game talk gives him a realistic shot at a top-5 overall finish; a commenter counters that last year was his ceiling season and doubts the camp reports now that DJ Moore is in.
- The cap is Josh Allen and the committee: last year Cook had 16 carries inside the 5 and Allen 17. Cooper says he does not get the full workload — pass work split with Ty Johnson, goal line split with Allen, mop-up to Ray Davis — and notes he has never topped roughly 302 PPR points even in an 18-TD season. One commenter expects rush volume to dip with DJ Moore as a real WR1; the in-thread rebuttal is that a real WR1 lightens boxes.
- Most-discussed player in the biggest thread (62 mentions). Backers cite 1,621 rush yards, 12 TDs, an RB6 finish and an elite offense; skeptics call 17.8 PPR per game closer to Swift than Bijan and say "high floor but won't likely be top 5." The TD-regression fade was heavily rebutted on the grounds that the new coach is the old OC.
- Market: he does not last to pick 10. Drafters report him going 5th or 6th overall in a 12-team and "Cook ain't falling to ten" from the 10 slot — late-first at the latest, so the room pays roughly what board rank 7 says. Maher has him RB4 and 8th overall in half PPR, ahead of McCaffrey and Jefferson, and takes him at 1.08 in a 10-teamer; Hartitz puts him ahead of Jonathan Taylor. Twerski is the dissent — he wouldn't take Cook at 1.05 over Amon-Ra St. Brown and specifically dislikes stacking him with Josh Allen.

**Draft take:** take him inside the top 10 for the floor, and the two named beat reports on a 50-catch receiving role are the reason the spike case is live for the first time.
