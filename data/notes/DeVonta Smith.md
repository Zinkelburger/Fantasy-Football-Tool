**DeVonta Smith** (PHI, WR, bye 10) — board rank 44 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** With A.J. Brown in New England he is Philadelphia's unquestioned WR1, and the split is over whether the vacated targets outweigh the coverage Brown used to pull away from him.

- Role change is settled: Brown and his 121 targets are gone, and Smith is the only established receiver left. His nearest competition for looks is rookie Makai Lemon, with Dontayvion Wicks behind. Commenters note over 25% of Hurts' career attempts went to Brown and those 50/50 targets have no obvious home — **conditional on some WR2 emerging to stop defences bracketing Smith**.
- The bull evidence is his production without Brown: roughly 18 PPG in the games Brown missed, near-WR5 grades by several metrics on about 200 routes without him, and five straight seasons of roughly 1,000 yards and 7 TDs as the No. 2. His 95/1,200/7 peak would have finished WR7 last year.
- The bear case is dilution, not talent. Under new OC Sean Mannion the split may run five ways — Lemon in three-WR sets, Wicks, the Goedert/Stowers tight ends, and Saquon Barkley taking more than 50 targets. Only four Eagles cleared 50 targets last year. He has also never been the red-zone player. Maher shares the concern, calling the offence more run-heavy and likely to spread the ball; he ranks Smith 26th overall, WR13, against an ECR of 27 — no edge at cost.
- The sharpest bear line in the room is simply the price: he finished WR21 last season playing all 17 games, so paying a WR13 price requires a real leap rather than a repeat.
- Market: the room is paying more than the board asks — going around overall pick 32 in PPR, roughly WR13 and a slot ahead of Tee Higgins, after going in the low 50s last year. He is being kept around pick 51 at a 4th-round cost, and is treated as an automatic keep at a 6th. In dynasty he is still WR17, where the buy case is that film watchers have called him a top-10 talent for years and he only ever lacked target share.
- Sentiment: eight top-level nominations for about 189 upvotes as a top-12 pick waiting to happen, citing a $75M extension and a JSN-style target consolidation. The room is comfortable starting him as the WR1 you get after opening RB-RB — the 164-point comment frames him exactly that way. Skeptics point at his size and durability over a full season and at Hurts' reluctance to build chemistry outside Brown, Smith and Goedert.

**Draft take:** Fair value at his third-round price and no better — take him as the WR1 on an RB-heavy build, but do not pay the WR13 tag expecting the WR7 season.
