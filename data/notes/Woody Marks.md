**Woody Marks** (HOU, RB, bye 8) — board rank 111 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** broadly skeptical and loud about it — he was the most-nominated player in the thread that debated him (26 top-level comments, 254 upvotes), and the repeated verdict was "bullshit" on the split report.

- Healthy. He played in preseason week 2 while David Montgomery sat; a commenter adds he was battling an injury late last season and is fine now, and Texans fans say he looks more fluid and physically bigger.
- **The strongest-sourced claim is a real committee.** Jonathan Alexander of the Houston Chronicle expects Marks to split the backfield pretty evenly with Montgomery on a week-to-week hot-hand approach — conditional on **Montgomery staying healthy**. Maher stops short of calling Montgomery a true workhorse for the same reason, expecting Marks to stay involved on passing downs.
- The room does not believe the even split. Consensus casts him as a passing-down/change-of-pace back: 4th-most receptions in NCAA history and good in pass protection, but poor between the tackles and bad at the goal line (3.59 ypc, 3 TDs as a rookie behind a bottom-tier run-blocking line). The pushback on the 50/50 reporting is that Houston traded for Montgomery and paid him, which reads as a plan for a clear lead back with Marks on third-and-long and for breathers.
- The defence comes from Texans followers: camp reports — not one stray tweet — have him in a true 50/50 split and lining up in the slot, people close to the team expect 40-50% of the backfield and most of the pass-game work, and his bad advanced metrics are blamed on bottom-two run blocking and being miscast as a three-down back after Mixon disappeared. Cooper's 2026-09-01 framing is the honest one: coach speak points to a significant role while reporters say Montgomery gets the bellcow job, and we do not yet know it is a split.
- Market: about what the board says, maybe later. Round 10-plus as an RB4/5, "right next to Bucky Irving," with several taking him in the 11th and benching him and one cutting him outright for Jeanty insurance; a minority would pay the 6th or 7th. The argument against paying up is that 40% of a bad offense's backfield is not startable even if the split is real. Twerski calls him the Houston back more likely to have standalone value but a worse contingency than Emmett Johnson; Dopp likes the same stash case he makes for Chris Rodriguez but trusts the Houston offense less.

**Draft take:** take him in the 10th or later as a cheap PPR flex with genuine contingency value behind Montgomery, and let someone else pay the 6th-7th round price some are floating.
