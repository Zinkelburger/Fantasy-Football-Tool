**Malik Nabers** (NYG, WR, bye 8) — board rank 35 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Trending toward playing Week 1, but on a managed snap count, and nobody involved will say so on the record.

- **Recovering from an ACL tear, with a follow-up cleanup knee surgery mentioned by one commenter and attached to no beat source.** He came out of the red non-contact jersey at practice on 8/24, and HC John Harbaugh told Jordan Raanan on 9/1 that Nabers took "most of the team reps", that the workload plan varies day to day, and that Week 1 is "trending in that direction". A beat report has him on the field again 9/2. That is the strongest evidence available; it is also short of a clearance, and Harbaugh is known for underselling injury detail.
- **The player himself will not commit.** Speaking to Raanan on 9/1 he said he feels "good" but would not say whether he plays Week 1, talking about getting his mind and body right; on 9/3 he said he is "focused on returning this season", which one commenter reads as deliberate vagueness aimed at making Dallas prepare for him. Hartitz noted on 9/2 that the latest updates have not been quite as optimistic, while still expecting him to play with vague talk of easing him in.
- **Assume a partial Week 1 workload.** The Giants room's guesses run from a handful of decoy snaps to 20-50% of his usual load — normally around a 99% snap share — with the offense leaning run-heavy early so he does not need it. One poster's standard is the right one: it is smoke and mirrors until he actually draws 10-15 targets in a game. A commenter's summary is "'85% not full speed' Nabers".
- Role beyond that is barely covered. His only named target competition on the Giants is Isaiah Likely. His rookie season was WR7 in half PPR across 15 games with Daniel Jones, Drew Lock and Tommy DeVito throwing to him, which is the whole bull case.
- **Market: board rank 35 is roughly where he goes, sometimes later.** Several independent draft recaps have him available at the 2/3 turn in 10- to 14-team leagues, and multiple managers report getting him in the 3rd or even the 4th. Cooper takes him in the third after eight named receivers and will not let him fall further; Twerski takes him in the third and prefers his upside to anyone in that range; Sanderson wants every dynasty share he can get but only a modest redraft overweight, explicitly because he will not bet a season on an injury recovery he calls hard to predict. One well-upvoted counter says he is priced correctly and the ACL plus cleanup surgery is worth a 10-20 spot drop.
- The 10 top-level fades (201 upvotes) are mostly about the Giants' offensive coaching rather than Nabers. Giants fans note the rehab is run by the same trainer who brought Stefon Diggs and Christian Watson back from ACL tears.

**Draft take:** Worth a third-round pick as a WR2 with WR1-overall upside, but plan on a limited September and do not draft him as a Week 1 starter.
