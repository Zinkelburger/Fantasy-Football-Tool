**Emmett Johnson** (KC, RB, bye 5) — board rank 155 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The handcuff of the moment — the No. 2 job is settled and coach-confirmed, and the argument is whether a Kansas City backup is ever more than a handcuff.

- His case is Kenneth Walker's availability, and Walker is fine — the 09-02 Walker practice news deflated Johnson on the spot ("Emmett believers in shambles"). Backers still expect 8-12 touches a game with Walker healthy and Chiefs fans project a 30-40% touch share; others hold him purely for a Walker absence, which Walker's injury history keeps live.
- Role, and the strongest source on the page: Andy Reid, quoted in an article the OP excerpts (09-03), says pass protection is what won the fifth-rounder the No. 2 job — "We knew he could run the ball, but the thing we had to see was pass protection. For him to be that No. 2 back, you have to be able to do that. He's a smart kid and willing to stick up in there." Kansas City waived Emari Demercado at the end of camp and never added a traditional three-down backup. He splits the backup work with Brashard Smith.
- The preseason record: 26 carries for 123 yards (4.73 per carry) and a touchdown with another wiped out by penalty, 6 catches on 7 targets, zero pressures allowed. Hartitz, citing PFF, had him leading all players in preseason Week 1 with 10 missed tackles forced, nobody else over 5 — with cautions in the corpus that the number is inflated by volume, and one skeptic who watched every touch counting four real ones.
- The receiving angle is why some prefer him to other backups at the same cost: Reid throws to his backs, and Johnson was the No. 10 back in the country in receiving yards at Nebraska and tops in his draft class.
- The objection is process, not talent: several commenters say Kansas City signs a veteran within a day of a Walker injury — Kareem Hunt is named repeatedly — and that "all Chiefs RBs always just mix in," leaving Johnson the pass-blocking back in a committee. The loudest dissent calls him a low-RAS back with one productive college season and a prospect model ranking him 112th; supporters comp him to Kyren Williams and Bucky Irving.
- Market: rostered in about 5% of leagues per one OP (15% on Yahoo; the 94% figure quoted in-thread is dynasty, not redraft), taken in the 13th and 14th rounds or claimed off waivers the day Demercado was cut. Hartitz calls him the best stash among Braelon Allen, Jonah Coleman and Chris Brooks, but prefers Ray Davis, Tank Bigsby, Brian Robinson and Kaelon Black to all of them.

**Draft take:** Spend a last-round pick only if you own Kenneth Walker, who is currently healthy — Reid has confirmed Johnson's job, but Kansas City's history says the payoff needs a Walker absence and may be split even then.
