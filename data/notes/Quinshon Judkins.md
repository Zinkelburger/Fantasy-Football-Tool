**Quinshon Judkins** (CLE, RB, bye 11) — board rank 42 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Nobody disputes the volume; the fight is over whether volume in this offense is worth a round-4/5 price.

- Health reads resolved. He is a season removed from a broken fibula with a dislocated ankle, and the PT breaking it down on 9/2 says it was less severe than it looked — no significant ligament damage, and research shows no significant dip in performance metrics or playing time after operative ankle fractures. He has looked explosive in preseason and practice. The room still treats the injury as a legitimate discount reason.
- Role is the bull case and it has team backing: the new head coach and RB coach Duce Staley have both said publicly he is capable of a three-down workload, and both have leaned on a single back before. Only Dylan Sampson is behind him. The room's supporting file: 36th overall draft capital, 1,000 yards and 7 TDs in 14 games on a bad team while facing a stacked box on 46% of carries — the highest rate in the NFL — with a revamped line, Harold Fannin and two top-40 receiver picks now available to unstack it.
- The cap on that role: essentially no passing-down work while Sampson is healthy, and the quarterback room. Bears cite Deshaun Watson, a defense without Myles Garrett or Jim Schwartz producing bad game scripts, and 3.6 YPC last year — which Loza attributes to the offense, ranking him around RB18 as a comfortable hero-RB RB2 while flagging two brutal road games to open.
- Market: he goes in the late 50s and the analysts split hard. Maher has him RB23, right on the RB2 border and comfortable behind a stud like Bijan Robinson. Hartitz uses him as the definition of a dead-zone RB — volume in a bad offense, limited pass-catching upside. Twerski lists him among his ADP fades and would package him with Brian Thomas for a better back. Murchison would keep him in the 7th over Rhamondre Stevenson in the 8th. The bull camp in the room wants him in the Hall/Swift tier about ten picks earlier.
- Sentiment splits on efficiency: he was near the bottom in per-touch efficiency in 2025 while Kenneth Walker was near the top, but he still averaged more fantasy points per game than Walker despite the situation. A Ringer host put it more bluntly: "Cleveland is the easy answer, I don't want Fannin or Judkins."

**Draft take:** Take the touches at his current cost as your RB2 — bell-cow snap share and goal-line work survive a bad offense, but he will not win you a week through the air.
