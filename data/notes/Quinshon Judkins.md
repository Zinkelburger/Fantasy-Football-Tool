**Quinshon Judkins** (CLE, RB, bye 11) — board rank 42 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** split, and split on the team rather than the player — the fade is almost always "I will not draft a Brown with Deshaun Watson at quarterback".

- Health looks resolved. He is coming off a late-season broken leg with a dislocated ankle. A physical therapist in the corpus says it was less severe than it looked — a fibula fracture and ankle dislocation with no significant ligament damage — cites research showing no significant dip in performance metrics or playing time after operative ankle fractures, and notes he has looked explosive in preseason and practice. Commenters presume him healthy but still treat the injury as a discount reason.
- Role is the strongest thing he has, and it is on the record: the new head coach and running backs coach Duce Staley have both said he is capable of a three-down workload, and both have leaned on a single back before. Dylan Sampson is the only back behind him.
- The volume case in detail: 36th overall draft capital, 1,000 yards and 7 TDs in 14 games on a bad Browns team while facing a stacked box on 46% of his carries — the highest rate in the NFL — with a revamped offensive line, Harold Fannin and two top-40 receiver picks now available to unstack it. Loza is bullish on him as a massive volume play at around RB18 and attributes his 3.6 yards per carry to the offense, while warning he opens with two brutal road games.
- The bear case is the pass game and the scoreboard: essentially no passing-down work while Sampson is healthy, two quarterbacks the room does not want, and a defense without Myles Garrett or Jim Schwartz producing bad game scripts. Hartitz uses him as the definition of a dead-zone back — volume in a bad offense, limited receiving upside. Twerski lists him among his ADP fades and would package him for a better back. On efficiency the room splits: near the bottom per touch in 2025, but more fantasy points per game than Kenneth Walker anyway.
- Market: the room is drafting him later than the board. He goes in the late 50s, the bull camp argues for the Hall/Swift tier about ten picks earlier, and reported prices include an 8th-round keeper and $40 in a 14-team $225 auction that the room called wild. Maher has him RB23, right on the RB2 border, and is comfortable with him as the RB2 behind a stud. Murchison would keep him in the 7th over Rhamondre Stevenson in the 8th.

**Draft take:** buy the workload at the room's price in the late 50s, not the board's round-4 rank — the touches and the coaching quotes are real, and the touchdowns are not.
