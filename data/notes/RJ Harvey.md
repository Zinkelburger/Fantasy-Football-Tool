**RJ Harvey** (DEN, RB, bye 10) — board rank 55 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The most polarizing back in the corpus — the same thread has him as its most-nominated dark horse and as a runner whose underlying numbers say he cannot run.

- Role, by consensus: change-of-pace and passing-down back with red-zone work, not an every-down option. The running joke in an 08-26 thread, asked six-plus times and called "established" in the OP's closing edit, is that he is the Darren Sproles of Sean Payton's offense. He scored 12 all-purpose touchdowns as a rookie.
- The backfield is three deep and that is the problem: an oft-injured J.K. Dobbins, who commenters call a zero in the passing game, and fourth-round rookie Jonah Coleman. Cooper ranks Harvey last among the risky upside RBs because a three-headed backfield kills the handcuff payoff if someone gets hurt. One OP goes further — even if Dobbins went down, Coleman is the likelier lead back, only while Coleman keeps looking like a three-down back.
- The tape case against him is repeated and specific: he cannot find the hole or hit it on time and goes down to arm tackles, with one commenter citing bottom-five yards over expected per carry and conceding it partly reflects defensive quality. The defence is that 7 of his 10 games came against top-10 defenses, he led college in 20-plus-yard explosive plays two years ago, and his draft pedigree (60th overall) clearly beats Coleman's (108th).
- Distrust the depth-chart signals: a commenter warns Payton talked the community into believing Harvey would beat out Dobbins last preseason and then barely played him for six weeks. A self-identified Broncos fan says no one in this backfield emerges as a clear number one because that is not how Payton operates, but takes Harvey anyway because touchdown-scoring is a trait Payton values.
- The most-upvoted Broncos-fan reply in one subthread says the touchdowns are unsustainable and he proved last year he cannot be a three-down back — great as a pass catcher, terrible as a rusher.
- Market and sentiment: "get off of Harvey for value while you still can" drew the reply that it is already too late, nobody wants him, everyone thinks he is a JAG. One owner refuses to sell without a very good offer on the strength of the 12 rookie TDs, and hedged by drafting Coleman instead. Bulls cite an RB20 rookie finish, seven RB1 weeks and PFF's top-graded offensive line; he was the most-nominated player in one dark-horse thread (6 top-level comments, 21 upvotes) and the most argued-over.

**Draft take:** Buy the receiving and goal-line role, not a workhorse — at board rank 55 you are paying for a joker in a three-man committee whose own defenders concede he cannot run.
