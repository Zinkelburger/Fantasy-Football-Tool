**RJ Harvey** (DEN, RB, bye 10) — board rank 55 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room has converged on what he is — a joker, not a workhorse — and only disagrees about whether that is worth the board's price.

- No injury or availability claim. The role question is the whole file: a three-man Denver backfield with J.K. Dobbins and Jonah Coleman, and nobody expects a clean lead back, because — as a self-identified Broncos fan put it Sept 1 — that is not how Sean Payton operates.
- The bull framing is specific and consistent: Harvey as the Bush/Sproles/early-Kamara joker with red-zone and receiving usage, 12 touchdowns and an RB20 finish as a rookie, behind what PFF grades the league's best offensive line. Supporting points the room raises: Dobbins is a zero in the passing game and not durable, and Bo Nix coming off a broken ankle should check down more. That same Broncos fan takes Harvey of the three because Payton genuinely values touchdown production.
- The bear case is the tape and it has not been answered. The most upvoted reply in the Aug 18 subthread called his touchdown rate unsustainable and said he proved last year he cannot be a three-down back — great as a pass catcher, terrible as a rusher. Commenters cite bottom-five yards over expected per carry and say he cannot find the hole on time and goes down to arm tackles; the Sept 3 consensus repeats that he "didn't look great running the ball last year." The defence: 7 of his 10 games came against top-10 defences, he led college in 20-plus-yard explosive plays two years ago, and his draft pedigree (60th overall) clearly beats Coleman's (108th).
- The same OP argued that if Dobbins went down, Coleman is the likelier lead back, not Harvey — only while Coleman keeps looking like a three-down back. Cooper (Sept 1) ranks him last among the risky-upside RBs on that logic: a three-headed backfield kills the handcuff payoff if someone gets hurt.
- Market: the room pays less than the board asks. Drafters report getting him around pick 100, in the 10th round, and for $7 in auction, against board rank 55. An Aug 18 seller was told it was already too late — "nobody wants him, everyone thinks he is a JAG" — though one owner will not sell without a very good offer.

**Draft take:** Worth taking at the room's price around pick 100 for the receiving and goal-line role, but do not pay the board's round-5 rank for a back the room repeatedly says cannot handle early downs.
