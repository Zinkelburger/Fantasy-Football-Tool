**MarShawn Lloyd** (GB, RB, bye 11) — board rank 189 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** the most-traded and most-argued name in the corpus — the room agrees he inherits the Green Bay backfield and does not believe he will hold up in it.

- Everything he is worth rests on Josh Jacobs being unavailable on the commissioner exempt list. Consensus is that Lloyd gets first crack as lead back, and owners are rolling into Week 1 starting him — without confidence, since they drafted him as a handcuff. Vegas has him as the Week 1 RB15 at 13.9.
- Availability is the binding question, not the role. He is healthy now and has been through camp, but he has roughly six to eight career touches across two-plus seasons, has been IR-eligible almost his whole career, and the dynasty room cites specialist visits for compensation issues dating to 2020 — "made of glass" is the recurring word. Maher notes the positive scouting impressions date to a Senior Bowl over two years ago. The room's own summary is a 94-point prediction that "Marshawn Lloyd stays healthy" is the craziest thing anyone could forecast.
- How big that role actually is, is disputed at the top of the source ladder. Sanderson calls him "the guy" while Jacobs is out but profiles him as a 12-15 touch back, leaving room for a 1B. Murchison expects the Packers to give him every chance while Chris Brooks mixes in to keep his load down. A camp report on 8/27 had him with the freshest legs in the NFL, fitting the outside zone scheme Green Bay is running, and expected to see real usage even with a healthy Jacobs. Green Bay traded for Kaleb Johnson on 8/30, which most read as depth rather than competition; one Week 1 guess is a 65/35 Lloyd/Brooks split, and several expect a genuine three-way committee.
- The market is where the decision is, and the board is badly behind it. Pre-news he went in rounds 13-15 or for $1-2 in auctions. Since, he has gone in rounds 8-12 and as early as the 6th, for $28 in one auction, and Maher has him RB26 and 66th overall against an ADP near 96, 23 spots ahead of ECR. Hartitz prices him round 6-7, Twerski around 70th overall, Murchison's ceiling is late round 5 — and the room's counter-camp says he belongs in round 10 or later and they would take J.K. Dobbins every time. The board rank of 189 is four to six rounds stale.
- Dynasty has repriced him harder than redraft: he is the single most-traded player DynastyDealer has recorded, 2,265 deals in seven days, jumping from about 230 a day to 636. Four top-level comments call him the current sell-high and compare him explicitly to Trey Benson a year ago — a back who inherited a job without having shown anything.

**Draft take:** he is the sell, not the buy — if you already own him take the early second or the Corum-tier back the room is offering, and if you do not, refuse to pay past the 8th for six career touches and a job that ends when Jacobs is reinstated.
