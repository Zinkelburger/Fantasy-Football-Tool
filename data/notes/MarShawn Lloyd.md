**MarShawn Lloyd** (GB, RB, bye 11) — board rank 189 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Heavy discussion and a fast-rising price built almost entirely on Josh Jacobs being on the Commissioner's Exempt List, with a loud minority pointing out Lloyd has never been healthy or productive.

- The board rank is stale. Lloyd's ADP moved from rounds 13-15 pre-news to rounds 6-9 after Jacobs's exempt-list placement, and analysts are pricing him higher still — Mike Maher of FantasyPros has him RB26 and 66th overall, 23 spots ahead of consensus. Anything you can still get near the board's number is a different player than the board thinks.
- Health is the whole case against him: about six career NFL snaps and roughly eight touches in two seasons, IR-eligible for most of his career, and one analyst notes his positive scouting impressions date to the Senior Bowl over two years ago. But he has been healthy through camp and is described as fully healthy this year; one camp report (2026-08-27) says he has "the freshest legs in the NFL" and fits Green Bay's outside-zone scheme.
- He gets first crack as the lead back while Jacobs is out — that is the consensus, and owners report they are starting him Week 1 without confidence. Conditional on Jacobs staying on the exempt list, Lloyd staying healthy, and Green Bay not adding an outside back.
- The workload is theoretical, not established. Green Bay traded for Kaleb Johnson on 2026-08-30 (read as a depth move) and Chris Brooks has produced before; several expect a three-way committee, with one Week 1 guess at 65/35 Lloyd/Brooks, and one poster notes nobody knows who takes Green Bay's first snap. One commenter expects Lloyd to hold a role even after Jacobs returns, if healthy — that is a single voice, not the room.
- Market split is wide and sharp: Vegas has him as the Week 1 RB15 at 13.9, while several analysts say the price has run past them — Murchison's ceiling is late round 5 and he advises Lloyd owners to sell into the excitement (trade him for Blake Corum "without a doubt"); the room called a 6th-round price "way too high" and said round 10 or later. The recurring joke is that he gets injured the moment good news arrives.

**Draft take:** A fine RB3 dart in rounds 8-plus and an obvious sell if someone pays a 5th or 6th for him — the role is real only while Jacobs is away, and Lloyd has never stayed on the field long enough to prove he can hold it.
