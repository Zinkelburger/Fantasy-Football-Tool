**Colston Loveland** (CHI, TE, bye 10) — board rank 49 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** The room likes the player and argues about the price — a round-4 tight end whose case requires him to out-target at least one of Chicago's receivers.

- Role, and the strongest source is the head coach: Ben Johnson made him his first pick at No. 10 overall and praises him repeatedly — "one of the most consistent players I've been around," in the building early and always in his playbook, and "we're going to take his route tree to the next level." That is a coach on a player he drafted, but the 2025 usage backs it: he led the Bears in receptions and receiving yards on a 15% target share despite barely playing early, ahead of DJ Moore, and was the only Chicago pass-catcher to outproduce Moore in the playoffs, with four straight double-digit-target games counting those, and was TE5 from Weeks 9-17 at 13.0 points per game.
- The live item as of 09-03: a pileup of Bears injuries. Commenters expect targets to funnel to him with Odunze, Burden and Swift missing time — "Loveland 200 target season incoming" was the top-upvoted read — including a red-zone bump. That holds only while those absences do; one commenter counters that with Swift and Monangai both down the Bears cannot move the ball in the red zone at all.
- The bear case is target competition and it is honest: skeptics project him third behind Rome Odunze and Luther Burden, making him touchdown-dependent, and note his four big games came when Burden or Odunze were hurt and that Kmet is still there. The room's answer is that DJ Moore is gone to Buffalo, Caleb Williams peppers those three about equally, and the Bears run two- and three-tight-end sets at a very high rate. Cooper puts it plainly: drafting Loveland is a bet against Burden or Odunze.
- The comparison the room reaches for is LaPorta, a rookie tight end who fell off in year two; the counter is a projected jump to a full route share from a 20-40% rate. Hartitz calls him the Bears' likeliest breakout and a possible overall dynasty TE1 a year from now.
- Market: ADP 49, going 45-50 overall, with reported spots from 3.5 through the 5/6 turn and most drafters landing in the 4th or 5th — the room pays about what the board says, at the back of it. He is already priced as a top-3-to-4 tight end, which is the substance of the fade. The analysts run the other way: Twerski calls himself a huge Loveland fan and says the early 30s is where it starts to make sense in PPR, Loza takes him around round 4 as her cheaper alternative to the elite tight ends, and Murchison would prioritise him at the 2/3 turn in TE-premium. In keeper leagues he is the most agreed-on bargain in the thread, four managers told to keep him at 8th-to-10th-round prices.
- Sentiment is split down the middle: four top-level predictions name him the overall TE1 and he is called "one of the surest bets in fantasy this year," against 10 top-level fades in one thread and 10 too-high nominations in another. The pushback on a bust nomination cites 47/597/6 from Week 9 on and 25 targets in two playoff games.

**Draft take:** Take him at the 4/5 turn and not before — the Bears' current injuries make the target path real for now, but at his price you are betting he beats Odunze or Burden for the receiving lead, and the room has not settled that.
