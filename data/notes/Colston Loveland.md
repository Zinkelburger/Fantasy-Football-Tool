**Colston Loveland** (CHI, TE, bye 10) — board rank 49 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** split, and the split is about price rather than player — the room likes him and a large bloc thinks rounds 3-4 is too much to pay.

- No injury of his own. What moves him is everyone else's: as of Sept 3 the room reads a pileup of Bears injuries — Odunze, Burden and Swift all out — as funnelling targets to him, the top-voted comment calling for a "Loveland 200 target season". That is conditional on those three being out, and a read in the same thread cuts the other way: with Swift and Monangai down the Bears lose their run game and struggle in the red zone at all.
- Ben Johnson, who made him his first pick as head coach at No. 10, calls him "one of the most consistent players I've been around", says he is in the building early and always in his playbook, and that "we're going to take his route tree to the next level".
- What he actually did: led the Bears in receptions and receiving yards last season on a 15% target share, ahead of DJ Moore, and was the only Chicago pass-catcher to outproduce Moore in the playoffs, with four straight double-digit-target games counting those. He is projected for a full route share this year, up from 20-40%.
- The bear case: at full health he is Chicago's third option behind Rome Odunze and Luther Burden, which makes him TD-dependent, and his big games came when one of those two was hurt — Cooper puts it sharply, drafting Loveland is a bet against Burden or Odunze. DJ Moore is also gone to Buffalo, and a commenter who follows both teams says Loveland benefited a lot from the coverage Moore drew. The counter is that Moore's departure frees those targets, Caleb Williams peppers the three about equally, and the Bears run two- and three-tight-end sets at a very high rate.
- Market: picks 44-49, fourth round of 12-team redrafts, already priced as a top-three tight end, so board rank is roughly what the room pays. TE-premium costs more — Murchison would prioritise him at the 2/3 turn, Loza takes him around round 4, Twerski says the early 30s overall. Hartitz calls him the Bears' likeliest breakout and a candidate for overall dynasty TE1 a year from now.
- Sentiment: the fades are the loudest bloc — ten "too high" nominations in one thread, ten more the next day, several drawing the LaPorta comparison of a rookie tight end regressing in year two. The bull side carries upvotes too: four top-level predictions naming him the overall TE1, and 47/597/6 from Week 9 on, TE2 at 14.3 ppg.

**Draft take:** pay for him in TE-premium and let him fall in redraft — at a top-three-TE price you are betting the Bears' receiver injuries last, and at full health he is Chicago's third option.
