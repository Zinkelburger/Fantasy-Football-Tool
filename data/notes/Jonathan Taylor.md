**Jonathan Taylor** (IND, RB, bye 13) — board rank 5 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** a proven bellcow whose fantasy season is a bet on Daniel Jones's Achilles and on Indianapolis playing with a lead.

- Healthy, and extended by the Colts — the fourth-highest-paid back behind Gibbs, Bijan and McCaffrey at about $20M a year, signed after Bijan without resetting the market. His own record of 6, 7 and 3 missed games in recent seasons is real but the room calls it fluky; he carried an 80%-plus snap share whenever active.
- Workload is not in question and is itself the worry: 369 touches in 2025, second only to McCaffrey, and he led the NFL at 19 carries a game as one of only six backs above a 70% snap share. That is back-to-back 300-plus-touch seasons with injury issues in three of the last five years. The defence is that he is 27, a physical outlier, behind an elite offensive line.
- The bear case is Daniel Jones, not the backfield. "If DJ goes down he becomes a low-end RB2" is the room's phrasing, and it says it already watched that happen last year — Taylor was far less involved when Jones missed time and is much more effective under center than from shotgun. One poster is out entirely because Jones is coming off a torn Achilles.
- The strongest fade is game script, and it is sourced: most game-script-dependent back in the league since 2021 at 24.6 FPG in wins against 13.8 in losses, 31.5 against 12.4 in 2025, with 23.9% of his 2025 points scored while Indianapolis led by two-plus scores in the second half — against a 7.5-win team total. A separate commenter shows he slowed markedly from week 9 of 2025 onward, *before* Jones got hurt: he cleared 20 points only against Atlanta over that span and broke 15 just four times.
- Touchdown regression is likely. He scored 20 times, 13 from inside 10 yards and 9 from inside the 3, and a Colts commenter expects some goal-line looks to flip to Tyler Warren now Michael Pittman is gone. He is not a receiving back — 46 catches is called his ceiling — so a healthy Keenan Allen or Josh Downs taking short-yardage targets cuts his PPR floor without touching his rushing volume. With Pierce, Downs and Warren limited, one commenter expects a slow start of stacked boxes and short passes.
- Market: the room pays roughly what board rank 5 says, and calls that his ceiling price. He goes 1.02-1.05 in most reported drafts at ADP 5.0-7.7, with outliers at 1.12 and pick 8, and was the cheapest of the elite tier in a 12-team superflex auction at 24% of budget (Gibbs 35, Bijan 33, Allen 32, CMC 26). Analysts split: Murchison leans Taylor over McCaffrey at pick 4 on injury fear, Twerski would swing at 1.04 in a first-down-scoring league, and Hartitz ranks James Cook above him — using Taylor's second half as the proof that RB production follows the offense, "Taylor didn't get worse, his offense did."
- Sentiment is oddly soft for a top-five pick: 10 top-level fades whose stated reasons are mostly not football — "the vibes are off," dislike of the Colts.

**Draft take:** worth pick 4-6 as the safest workload in football, but he is a leveraged bet on Daniel Jones — if you doubt the Achilles, take St. Brown or Cook instead and do not pay up.
