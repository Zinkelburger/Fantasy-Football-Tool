**Tank Bigsby** (PHI, RB, bye 10) — board rank 158 · as of 2026-09-04

**Room sentiment:** Uncontested as Philadelphia's RB2; the whole argument is whether a handcuff with no standalone role is worth a bench spot.

- His value is entirely Saquon Barkley's availability, and no claim in the corpus reports Barkley as anything but healthy — so right now Bigsby is a contingency, not a player. He is named the best handcuff in one thread precisely because, unlike Emmett Johnson, "we know he's the guy if Saquon gets hurt" — a settled backup role rather than a projected one.
- Role: clear RB2 behind Barkley, with Will Shipley third and a complementary special-teams job; Blue's practice-squad signing is not seen as a threat. No committee is expected while Barkley is healthy, so standalone value is close to zero. One commenter calls him the best backup RB across the Philadelphia, Miami and Los Angeles backfields and better than either of Omarion Hampton's backups.
- The analysts split on how to rank a cuff with no path: Cooper puts him with Kaelon Black as the highest pure-upside handcuffs because the team he would inherit is good, but ranks him low on standalone value. Murchison would hold him over Kaleb Johnson as a potential league-winner. Dopp agrees he technically has the most upside of the late stash options but the least direct path to opportunity, and ranks him behind Chris Rodriguez for that reason.
- Market: Twerski names him among his must-grab handcuffs and favorite late-round or early-waiver RBs. Nothing in the corpus argues the room pays more than board rank 158.
- Sentiment is thin and mostly bare nominations. The one substantive negative: he is cited as an example of a top handcuff worth cutting for a target-getting WR, with a reply that there is a strong likelihood these handcuff RBs do not do much this year.

**Draft take:** With Barkley healthy this is a bench spot spent on an insurance policy — take him only if you own Barkley.
