  Best-case path: emerge as a consistent WR3 with weekly FLEX upside in a grid of injuries or a fast-paced game plan.

  - High competition for targets in a pass-heavy but unproven offense; Collins is the target anchor and Dell, plus other receivers, can cap Higgins’ floor.
  - Injury variance in the WR room and uncertain offensive efficiency in 2025.
  - Limited track record in NFL-level usage if the offense prioritizes other receivers or if Stroud doesn’t lock onto Higgins consistently.

- Draft strategy implications:
  - Treat Higgins as a late-round dart with tangible upside, especially in Best Ball leagues.
  - In standard leagues, consider him as a speculative stash behind Collins and Dell; avoid banking on him as a weekly WR3 unless camp reports show clear, sustained target separation.
  - Pair with a safer WR early to balance risk against upside; monitor camp reports for target splits and injuries.

- Key discussion points to watch in 2025:
  - Camp insights on target competition behind Collins (Dell’s status; injury recovery timeline).
  - Early-season usage vs. weeks of pressure from other WRs in the Texans offense.
  - Any coaching or scheme tweaks that unlock Higgins as a true WR2/WR3 option.

Bottom line: Higgins has legitimate 2nd-round pedigree and a plausible WR2 role in Houston behind Collins, with upside if Dell’s health and the offense cooperate. He’s best viewed as a late-round dart with upside in best-ball or as a bench stash in standard leagues; not a reliable weekly starter unless clear target share emerges early in the season.
